## cells-index-join (31)

31 runs in four families: H3/Quadbin cell utilities on Honshu epicenters (17), S2 cells (4), spherical geometry on Great Lakes shores (5) and planar joins on Great Lakes zones (6, including buffer selection and nearest joins). Most pictures are honest and legible, but 17 of 31 cards scrub a Data: or Simulated knob, and 9 scrub by full rebuild (resolution, k, level). Best posters are cell-rollup, cell-topology, point-in-polygon-join, spherical-convex-contains and buffer-selection. Worst are cell-geometry (fill by vertexCount is one flat color), s2-cell-hierarchy (48 overlapping seeds), hilbert-keys (random points hide the curve), s2-cell-measures (auto range turns a tiny latitude trend into a full ramp) and spatial-join-prepared (the prepared index is invisible). Several entries are near duplicates: cell-pyramid / cell-level-selection, cell-measures / s2-cell-measures, spherical-arc-overlay / spherical-polygon-overlay, spatial-predicate-join / spatial-join-candidates / spatial-join-prepared. The biggest lever is a shared 'pointer row' helper: append the pointer as one extra point to an existing per-frame positions buffer, so the real operation (GPUPointToCell, the joins, the contains tests) answers for the cursor on the GPU without a CPU H3/S2 port; this serves about 12 entries. Second lever: spherical-surveys.ts helpers taking a pointer longitude and latitude, so the five spherical runs become place/drag of their survey shapes. Third: join.ts drawing zone bounding boxes, so the join family explains candidates versus exact matches.

## dataframe-pointcloud-records (44)

Forty-four runs: dataframe operations on Midwest lightning flashes, Chicago tract and workplace tables, point-pattern and variogram charts beside a flash map, King County lidar point clouds, and six computation-record runs. 33 of 44 already scrub with update, but 13 scrub a Data: knob (six of the eleven lidar runs scrub jitter, strays, slump depth or coordinate magnitude), and 11 rebuild on every step (the six record and snapshot runs, ground, smrf, icp, voxel-merge, local-positions). Best pictures: point-buffer, transfer-mask, pointcloud-change, composite-score and temporal-reduction. Worst: pointcloud-smrf (a flat purple raster), class-breaks (flat sorted curve, lone bar), color-scale (fake palette), pointcloud-voxel-merge and pointcloud-statistical-filter (the effect cannot be seen from above). Nine chart-beside-map runs (variogram family, Ripley family, marked points) share one layout, have no reference lines and look alike. One shared distance-axis probe with rings or pair links would fix most of them at once. The lidar runs need a shared nearest-point probe, and a side-profile view in _lib/helpers.ts for operations that work in z.

## geometry-processing (36)

36 runs, all built on four shared helpers (polygon-set, shape-segments, topology-output, geometry-tree): a grid of 12 to 18 star-shaped shore polygons, one river reach, or the crossing river lines. The pictures are tidy, but most show many small copies of the same shape, so a card shows a texture more than an operation. 17 runs scrub a Data: knob, 11 scrub by full rebuild, and most of the real props (tolerance, gridSize, offsetDistance, ratio, distance) are WGSL constants fixed at construction. Pointer interaction here is mostly about geometry: drag a vertex or line (make-valid, polygonize, minimum bounds/clearance, inscribed circle), reach a tolerance ring (snaps, precision grids, offsets, concave hull), or move a window (bounds-filter, rectangle-clip, both already per-frame). Best pictures: ratio-concave-hull, rectangle-clip, line-precision-reduction, local-frame-projection, minimum-bounds, polygonize. Worst: geometry-cleanup (a nearly straight line, so nothing happens), convex-straight-skeleton (offset edges shoot to the world origin), lon-lat-polygon-preparation (everything squeezed into the top strip of a world map), and the two canonical-equality runs and normalize-geometry, whose effect cannot be seen at card size. Shared changes that would fix many entries at once: a drag-a-vertex helper and a proper fill triangulation in shared/polygon-set.ts, writable crossing-line positions so the four crossing-line runs can drag a line per frame, a geodesic-arc helper in shared/shape-segments.ts for the two geodesic runs, and a framework way to snap a rebuildOn parameter to a few prebuilt variants.

## recipes-trajectories (42)

The group is a mix of 28 analysis recipes on synthetic zones, tracts or rasters and 14 trajectory runs on the San Francisco vessel fleet. 16 of the 42 cards scrub a `Data:` knob, and four scrub by full rebuild (classCount, edgeDepth, resolution, sampleCount). The strongest pictures are trajectory-kalman, straight-line-catchments, drainage-network and points-in-polygons-choropleth. The weakest are resistance-connectivity (potential saturates to one colour and the wall cannot be seen), surface-preparation (the output covers the holes it filled), period-comparison and space-time-hot-spots (sparse cells with no geography), and the encounter trio, which shows a squashed fleet over an unlabelled bar chart. Several entries look the same: clock-encounters, trajectory-encounters and trajectory-closest-approach; zonal-distribution and points-in-polygons-choropleth; the two segregation recipes; the two resistance-connectivity recipes; zone-events and the two fleet-dwell recipes; track-similarity and track-similarity-search. Many cheap wins are spatial inputs that `update` already writes per frame: the zone rectangles (createZoneRectangles), the restriction box, the clock start, the encounter distance and the wall-gap conductances. Moving them under the pointer needs only a pointer-to-world value in update. Shared fixes would help many entries at once: lib/fleet.ts (the fleet fills about a third of the frame, and raiseFleet squeezes it further), lib/zones.ts (fixed zone centres, one of which no track visits), lib/tracts.ts and lib/quads.ts (no neighbour or member lookup for probing), and lib/earthquakes.ts (earthquake grids drawn with no geography). ## regression-weights (38)

The group splits into 15 weights runs (point or tract link graphs) and 23 regression runs that almost all use one layout: a Chicago tract map of residuals on the left and coefficient bars against true-value dots on the right. Most cards scrub a Data knob: 27 of 38 do, usually the generating process (rho, lambda, noise, contrast). The best pictures are spatial-lag, gwr-nonstationarity-test, geographically-weighted-regression and multiscale-gwr, where the map shows the operation's own output and the slider changes it legibly. The weakest are spatial-regression-diagnostics (its test bars barely render at range [0, 40]), pair-gather, neighborhood-summary (dark on dark), spatial-weights-traces (no spatial picture, a spike artifact) and the chunked-weights family, which colors links by chunk and hides what the operation does. The census is wrong for the 7 runs built through shared/table-model.ts and shared/panel.ts: they do have update(), so the card scrubs `strength` or `unitEffects` per frame, not maximumPower, instrumentOrder or estimator by rebuild. In shared/weights.ts, a per-row highlight of links (`probe`) would serve all 15 weights runs. The table-model and panel runs look alike (duplicates); a probe or period playhead is the cheapest way to tell them apart.

## terrain-relief-light (31)

All 31 runs use the same measured Grand Canyon crop through support/terrain.ts, and 27 of them already have per-frame update(), so the change to the pointer is mostly a matter of binding rather than rebuilding: only terrain-rgb-decode, terrain-ruggedness and topographic-position scrub by rebuild, and only peak-prominence, spike-repair and terrain-critical-points scrub a Data: knob. The visibility runs (viewshed, line-of-sight, point-horizon-profile, point-horizon-visibility) already write the observer column and row each frame, so 'place' costs only the binding plus a pointer-to-cell helper. The sun runs (relief-shading, slope-aspect-hillshade, solar-shadow-mask, terrain-cast-shadow) are natural 'aim' pads where angle maps to azimuth and distance maps to altitude, all per-frame today. The strongest posters are geomorphons, terrain-cast-shadow, solar-position and terrain-curvature. The weakest are cumulative-viewshed (towers clustered in one corner, most of the frame dark), peak-prominence and terrain-summits (scattered single pixels), profile-peaks (four profiles concatenated with full-height on/off bars), weiss-landforms (ten classes wrapped into the 8-colour default palette with no legend) and solar-irradiance (Swiss sun table over the Grand Canyon, saturated sun-hours). Two duplicate families hurt the catalog row. In the first, relief-shading, slope-aspect-hillshade, solar-shadow-mask and relief-blend all render as the same magma hillshade because there is no grey ramp. In the second, simple-local-relief, multi-scale-relief and texture-shading are all saturated red/blue diverging maps. Shared fixes in support/terrain.ts (a pointer-to-cell inverse, spread-out summit picking, and a grey relief drawer) and fixed ranges instead of 'auto' on five runs would lift about half of the group.
