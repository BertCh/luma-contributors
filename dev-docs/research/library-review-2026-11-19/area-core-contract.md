# Shared core and contributor contract (2026-11-19)

Twelve of the fifteen CORE findings from November 17 are fixed for their reported cases. Three are partly fixed: a host-side limit can change without invalidating a loop allowance, the operation adapter still misattributes its chunk restriction, and the vocabulary guard still omits point-cloud analysis.  
Four new failures were reproduced on the CPU: local-frame conversion silently loses rows in a short destination, a public clustering packer emits zero or infinity for an admitted radius, parameter defaults fail for valid field names, and a rejected internal reduction poisons a corrected graph declaration.  
The first device probe to run is a two-point DBSCAN case. At a projected origin of one million metres, the shared grid writer and the clustering reader calculate different cells for points exactly one radius apart. Four other numerical leads also need device results.  
The largest enforcement gap is in the kernel audit. Its parser detects a resolved 32 KiB workgroup allocation against a 16 KiB budget, but the registry-wide assertion discards that finding. Case membership also conflates two different visibility recipes.  
The public catalog covers the enumerated qualified identities. The audit covers 537 distinct names across eight entries, and 31 of those names have no named API heading. Each number measures a different obligation.  
Before alpha, repair the reproduced public host failures, qualify the normalized grid, and make the registry enforce all audit findings by package and export. The November 18 gate results remain historical.

## Limits of this report

No shader was compiled or executed, and no current suite or release gate was certified. Five shader leads have explicit refutation probes. The Jacobi lead additionally lacks a demonstrated ordinary public input. Reach grades describe the inputs and paths inspected; they do not estimate production incidence.

All six slice reports were read. Scope exclusions that affect interpretation are the geometry-owned triangulation/ring bodies beyond the historical fixes, most domain fixture bodies, every downstream grid traversal, all optional-output branches, all temporal-validity consumers, all compiler variants and every one of the 127 allowed scalar loops. The regression inventory was checked against selected GTWR, MGTWR and restricted-regime routes, without revalidating its entire list. Entry graphs exclude third-party transitive bytes. The 30 unresolved producer shapes and inferred recipe returns prevent a claim of exhaustive semantic status classification. Public mask polarity, ID versus row naming, validation message quality and all output limits still require per-family evidence.

The report distinguishes host behavior reproduced from the live source, historical behavior reproduced from two source revisions, and named tests inspected in source. The November 18 fixes are better pinned than the November 17 evidence alone indicated. The remaining risk most relevant to release is that a passing structural gate can discard a real diagnostic or credit the wrong implementation, while an ordinary projected-coordinate route still lacks an independent two-point device result.
