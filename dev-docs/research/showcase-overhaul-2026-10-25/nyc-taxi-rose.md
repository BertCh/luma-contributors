# Modeled taxi route headings: circular summaries

The existing NYC taxi trail study now directly executes `GPURoseStatistic`. The [TLC trip-record description](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page), read 25 October 2026, identifies pickup/drop-off times and locations, distance and fare fields supplied by technology providers. These fields do not constitute observed intermediate GPS trajectories. The showcase's routed and timed paths therefore remain modeled geometry between real trip endpoints; the rose does not claim measured bearings or traffic demand.

At a selected playhead time, GPU playhead outputs identify each active track's current segment and heading. Zero-length segments and inactive tracks are excluded. Four precompiled statistical variants combine directional (360°, 36 bins) or axial (180°, 18 bins) interpretation with equal positive weight or current modeled segment length in projected metres. The latter is descriptive geometry weighting, not frequency weighting. All variants use the same five fare-quantile group IDs; the view either aggregates their returned weighted moments or displays five small multiples. Fare grouping implies no causal relation between fare and route orientation.

The rose renderer centers its sectors. The contributor floors angles at bin edges, so inputs rotate by half a 10° bin before binning and returned mean angles rotate back. This aligns north/east/south/west sector centers without changing resultant length. Axial bins appear in two opposite sectors at half weight each, preserving total displayed weight. The three-sector concentration label uses the midpoint of the three centered sectors. No Rayleigh significance claim or p-value is shown: the contributor's Rayleigh z is based on unweighted valid directions, even when descriptive weights are supplied.

The independent CPU generator `scripts/nyc-taxi-rose-reference.mjs` reads bundled path, time and fare columns, reconstructs projected coordinates and active segments, and computes circular moments and bins independently. At 600 seconds it includes 1,468 nonzero active segments, with roughly 1,042,996 metres of current-segment length. This sum is not distance traveled during the current interval.

The browser verifier `scripts/check-nyc-taxi-rose-reference.mjs` covers eight direction × weight × grouping variants. It checks every rendered sector, aggregate resultant length and support count after the requested chart has arrived. Sector comparisons allow half of the displayed rounding unit plus a small float32 accumulation allowance (`max(0.005, abs(value) * 1e-6)`); compact K/M/B formatting is explicitly decoded. These are checks at displayed precision, not full GPU-buffer comparisons. Resultant length is displayed to two decimals. Every report records overall pass/failure separately from console errors and closes its browser.
