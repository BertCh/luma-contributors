Status date: 2026-11-20. Baseline: `@luma-contributors/analysis`, `@luma-contributors/lenses` and `@luma-contributors/kepler` use published luma.gl. The [alpha package contract](./release-package.md) records the current integration target, accepted peer range and unreleased status separately.

Reuse item IDs in issues and changes. H means clear scope and analysis or correctness value; M needs a consumer or design step; L is research or deferred work. Source paths are repository-relative; analysis paths below omit `modules/analysis/src/`. Research sources hold detail, not an instruction to repeat a finished pass.

Delete completed rows. Update coverage when coverage changes, and the workaround register when accepting a regression. Review the backlog at each luma.gl bump and release. Validate retained code references before changing them. New contributors include their catalog entry, documentation and kernel-audit case in the same change.

## Library review 2026-11-19 (R19)

A second review of `@luma-contributors/analysis`, two days after the first, with consumer seams sampled. Evidence, the defect tables with the state of each finding and the limits are in [research/library-review-2026-11-19][review19]; finding IDs below (SSR, GEO, PTS, DGGS, TRJ, URB, RCP, NET, RT, DPC, CORE, ERG, DOC, CAT, TOOL, LUMA, SEAM, LENS, each from 101) are defined in its area files. Every roadmap edit made from it, with the source line checked, is in [roadmap-changes.md](./research/library-review-2026-11-19/roadmap-changes.md); documentation corrections are in [doc-corrections.md](./research/library-review-2026-11-19/doc-corrections.md).

What it found: the 2026-11-17 fixes held (188 of 231 follow-through rows fixed, 35 partly), and first reads still found 118 wrong results or visible failures (23 in ordinary use), most of them in seven classes: completeness that does not reach the consumer, float32 range and cancellation at projected-coordinate magnitudes, loss reported only through an optional status view, packers that validate a double and write float32, outputs without a writer, tail probabilities, and catalog or documentation claims the source contradicts.

The fix pass, 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-20: after the fourteen area reports, a second round of 32 coherence and ergonomics reports gave 493 findings by the synthesis's recount ([coherence](./research/library-review-2026-11-19/coherence/README.md)). Six waves of Codex implementers worked the findings, each stream followed by an independent reviewer and a fix round. Waves 1 to 5 tracked 1,975 items, with implementers' verdicts 1,638 fixed, 240 partly, 49 deferred, 46 already fixed and 2 refuted. A read-only triage of the 289 partly or deferred items plus 155 unplaced handoffs found 338 of 487 rows done and 111 open. Wave 6 (six areas plus a leftover track) fixed the test failures of the end-of-pass run and the open rows in finished areas, and carried out the decisions below. The known-issues pass (KI) was merged into the same tree on 2026-11-20 (see "Completed passes").

Decisions of 2026-11-20, taken by the lead under the maintainer's instruction to make the right decisions ([decisions20]; [review19], section "Outcome"). Rule: before the alpha there is one name per concept and no compatibility aliases; renames are listed in `CHANGELOG.md` under "Breaking changes before the alpha", and lens scenario IDs and committed record names keep their old names. Those that change rows here:

- 1 (open): package identity (owner, URL and name of a public package) is the maintainer's. R19-05, DEC-PACKAGE.
- 2: the alpha stays held until this work lands and one full gate passes on the committed tree. R19-01, R19-05.
- 3: stable and experimental tiers adopted; every catalog record is `stability: 'experimental'` until the R19-22 admission rule is met. R19-22.
- 4: the five journeys are confirmed and built after the alpha. R19-20.
- 5: luma.gl alpha.17 is assessed after this landing, then a weekly bump. R19-10.
- 6 (open): a second GPU vendor and Safari and Firefox runs need hardware. R19-09, DEC-SECOND-GPU.
- 7: LRN-06, EMB-03, EMB-04 and SIM-03 are kept; the other LRN, EMB, SIM, CART and ENC rows are deleted. "New contributors: unexecuted options".
- 24: the roadmap split is not done now. R19-25.

Test state: the last full run before wave 6 (staging commit of 2026-11-20 afternoon) had 218 failing Node tests of 38,774 and 128 failing headless files of 2,018; wave 6 was assigned all of them. #### Kepler analysis workbench (KE-WF, in progress 2026-11-14)

A node-graph analysis workbench in the Kepler host, over one serialisable workflow document. The design study is [research/kepler-workflows-2026-11-14](./research/kepler-workflows-2026-11-14/README.md); section 9 of that file records the experiments and the decisions below. Built in a staging copy and landed after KE-PORT wave 3.

Provisional decisions taken for this build, not yet confirmed by the maintainer (each is the study's recommendation unless noted; the canvas dependency and the host-only persistence are the two that most need confirming):

- **Canvas and list over one document.** A bottom dock holds a React Flow canvas (`@xyflow/react` 12.12.1, MIT; verified under React 19 in the host), an inspector and a run log, with the conventions of noodles.gl and Houdini (Tab or N adds a step, B disables, D displays, F frames, undo by snapshot). A Workflows tab in the Kepler side panel (`CustomPanelsFactory`) is the narrow step-list view. No Kepler fork.
- **Every node is a step.** Data enters through source steps (a host dataset, all rows or the current selection, or embedded GeoJSON), not through per-input bindings as in the draft spec. Steps connect output port to input port.
- **Hand-off by table** on the CPU, within the catalog capacities, checked before a result is passed on. `WorkflowData` is the seam where a GPU-resident form can be added (KE-WF-08, not built).
- **Catalog bindings are not changed.** Each becomes a workflow operation through an adapter that derives port kinds; glue operations (join attributes, filter, calculate, select, sort, summarise, concatenate) are workflow-only because a catalog input cannot be a table.
- **Status is derived from content keys**, not stored: a step is done when it holds a result for its current key, stale when it holds one for an older key. Editing a step or a change in a host dataset, filter or selection re-keys exactly the affected steps.
- **Display publishes to the map.** A displayed output becomes a resident dataset with a layer and is replaced in place on re-run; from there the existing resident analysis panels, filters and charts apply.
- **Workflow inputs** (parameters promoted to the workflow) are in; step-output parameter binding, expressions on parameters, iterators and branches are out.
- **Persistence** is the host sandbox extension `settings.extensions.workflows` only. Kepler's own Save Map drops it.
- **Assistant** tools list and describe operations and propose a draft; nothing runs before the user approves the whole workflow.
- **Templates** read embedded synthetic geometry only, so no third-party licence applies.

### New contributors: unexecuted options

The maintainer chooses the directions and order. Already built and no longer in the table: forest inference (LRN-01, `GPUForestInference`), the fitting kernels (LRN-02, `GPUAdamStep`, `GPUFittingGradientSums`; no contributor consumes them yet), Gaussian random fields (SIM-01, `GPUGaussianRandomField`) and CLAHE (CART-03, `GPURasterContrastEqualization`). On 2026-11-20 four options were kept (LRN-06, EMB-03, EMB-04, SIM-03) and the other LRN, EMB, SIM, CART and ENC rows were deleted ([decisions20], decision 7); the research keeps their reasoning. Second-device probes come first.

Planning limits: at 0.1 trillion multiply-accumulates/s, fitted/per-point networks use ≤100,000 parameters and ≤5 billion multiply-accumulates/run; WGSL pretrained models use ≤2 million parameters (8 MB float32). Enforce named limits at construction, keep each binding ≤128 MiB unless checked against the device, and keep runs to tens of dispatches. Use minibatches: the 262,144-row probe needed a 281 MB binding. Second-device, real unbalanced forest, hash-grid, attention, float16 and command-graph overhead measurements remain open.

Fits publish loss history, converged and iterationLimitReached, including the iterate at the limit; use fixed-order tiles or fixed-point sums for repeatability. Models and weights are caller-supplied; no bundled weights/datasets. Record example data licences. Use fused analysis kernels, shared fitting helpers in core, and propose generic layers upstream. Proposed location for models: dataframe analysis. Verify each decoder formula and independent oracle before implementation; check required oracle availability rather than relying on a machine inventory.

Keep the research’s not-recommended candidates out of implementation; its reasons and the measurements supporting these options remain in [the research](./research/new-contributors-2026-11-11/README.md).
