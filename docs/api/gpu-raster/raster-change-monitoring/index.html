Raster change monitoring

GPURasterHarmonicRegression, GPURasterClimateRunAccumulator, GPURasterGeometricMedianComposite, GPURasterBestAvailablePixel, GPURasterBFASTMonitor, GPURasterClimateAnomaly, GPURasterHANTS, GPURasterEOFProjection and GPURasterCOLDMonitor accept aligned chunked cell records. Keep each complete time series, mask and output record within a chunk; slice limits still apply per cell. Harmonic regression defaults to storage-backed QR; the other reference slice caps remain.

Contributors for irregular-date seasonal-trend regression and for change detection on raster time series: GPURasterHarmonicRegression, a simplified COLD monitor (CCDC family), and BFAST Monitor. Import from @luma-contributors/analysis/raster-analysis. All are GPUCommandNodeProducer classes, take a GPURasterIrregularDateAxis as dateAxis, are cell-major, and take mask per observation with zero rejecting a sample; non-finite values are missing. Years are centred fractional years of 365.2425 days, so a fit's slope is per year and its intercept is at the midpoint of the axis.

GPURasterHarmonicRegression

Default storage-backed QR supports 4,096 slices with bounded scratch batches; mode reference retains 256 slices. Aligned cell chunks retain complete time-series records.

Ordinary least squares per cell of intercept + slope t + sum(cos, sin harmonic j / period) over the valid observations (getRasterLeastSquaresWGSL). Outputs the coefficients, a two-word summary [status, observationCount], and optionally the fitted curve and the residual standard deviation with count - coefficients degrees of freedom. Status is 1 at most 2 + 2 * harmonicCount valid observations, and 2 on a rank failure. Storage-backed QR is the default through 4,096 slices. The reference route retains its construction-time private-memory bound: 256 slices allow one harmonic. Reference mode writes coefficients, summary, fitted values and RMSE in one dispatch, so disjoint ranges of one imported output buffer are supported. Storage mode publishes fitted values in a second dispatch that reads coefficients and summary; fitted values must use a different physical buffer from those two fields, even when their byte ranges are disjoint.

GPURasterCOLDMonitor

A simplified COLD monitor (Zhu et al. 2020, RSE 238:111116; CCDC, Zhu and Woodcock 2014), per cell and per observation date, on several bands:

  1. Initialization: from a start position, a window of minimumObservations (12) valid observations spanning at least minimumSpanYears (1). A gap of maximumInitializationGapYears (1) or more shifts the window by one observation. Each candidate is fitted with 4 coefficients and accepted when sum over bands ((|slope| * span + max(|first residual|, |last residual|)) / max(RMSE, minimumRMSE))^2 <= changeThreshold, otherwise the window shifts. The test is pyxccd's; how the window moves after a rejection is not (see the table). (Until 2026-11-11 this monitor added the two end residuals instead of taking the larger. That rejected windows pyxccd accepts, so segments started 1 to 9 observations later on the pyxccd fixture.)
  2. Monitoring: each following observation is scored as sum over bands (residual / max(RMSE, minimumRMSE))^2. A change is confirmed when consecutiveCount (6) observations all exceed changeThreshold and the mean cosine between consecutive residual vectors is at least minimumMeanCosine (cos 45deg). The break is the first confirming observation. A lone observation above outlierThreshold is skipped. Otherwise it joins the segment, and the model is refitted when the included count has grown by a factor of 1.33.
  3. The number of coefficients follows the COLD rule by included observations: 4 below 18, 6 below 24, 8 above, capped by maximumCoefficientCount.

Choose changeThreshold and outlierThreshold from the chi-squared distribution with bandCount degrees of freedom. pyxccd 1.1.0 uses probabilities 0.99 and 1 - 1e-5 (its T_MAX_CG is 30.8562, that quantile for five bands).

How "simplified" differs from pyxccd

pyxccd's cold_detect (Apache-2.0) is the reference implementation of COLD. This monitor differs as follows, each stated so a result can be read correctly. Comparison with pyxccd 1.1.0

test/oracles/rts04-cold-pyxccd.py runs cold_detect (with lam=20 and lam=0) on eight synthetic seven-band series of 140 to 160 irregular dates with known breaks, 0 to 10000 scaling and noise 40, and test/gpu-raster/raster-time-series/rts04-cold-pyxccd.spec.ts runs this monitor on the five scored bands with pyxccd's thresholds, its variogram floor as minimumRMSE and its year. On the seven series with abrupt breaks (one or two disturbances, a small one, flagged clouds, an unflagged cloud, four confirming observations, none):

  • Break dates are equal to pyxccd's with and without LASSO, and to the known dates. Eight series do not show how far this holds; "Break size" below does. The small one is a series where the two happen to agree.
  • Segment starts and ends are equal to those of pyxccd's default run, except one start (lookback, above).
  • A segment that pyxccd last refitted on the observations this monitor fits has its coefficients to 0.07 and its RMSE to 0.02 (lam=0). Where pyxccd's last refit is a few observations old the gaps are 2.7 and 0.35.
  • Change magnitudes are within 20 of pyxccd's at lam=0.

Break size. Whether the two confirm the same break depends on how far the break stands above changeThreshold. The same generator draws 160 series of 150 dates with one break at a random slice: the full disturbance of the series above, a half and a quarter of it, on an 8 to 24 day revisit and on alternating dense and sparse revisits. The "score" column is the change score the break alone would give, sum over bands (shift / max(RMSE, minimumRMSE))^2, over the threshold (15.09). Counts are from the test GPU.

  • At 4 times the threshold and above, every break date is pyxccd's and the true one. Starts differ in 14 of those 80 series (lookback and window advance).
  • Near the threshold there is no parity, and neither implementation finds the break reliably. Of the 31 series that differ, in 23 pyxccd confirms nothing and this monitor confirms a break, often some observations after the true one; in 8 both confirm, at different slices; in none does only pyxccd confirm. A reviewer's independent draw of the same kind gave 35 of 80, with one series where only pyxccd confirmed.
  • Between 1.45 and 4 times the threshold nothing was measured.
  • Why: mostly the refit schedule. With pyxccd's schedule in the binary64 reference (patched in scratch, not committed and not in the kernel) the break dates are equal in 64 of the 80 quarter-size series instead of 49; adding the temporally adjusted RMSE of the reference gives 65. The remaining differences were not attributed.
  • What matching the schedule would take. In the same patched reference, pyxccd's schedule costs a median of 56 to 73 fits of all bands for one of these series (at most 81), against 12 to 14 (at most 17) now. The default maximumFitCount is 96 and the per-cell work budget allows 163 fits at 160 slices, 5 bands and 8 coefficients (117 at 7 bands), so a series with two breaks would stop with status 8. It needs a fit that is updated per observation instead of redone (the normal equations of each band accumulated, and one 8 by 8 solve per observation), or a larger work budget. Neither is done: the schedule stays a documented difference.

test/oracles/rts04-change-monitoring-numpy.py records a binary64 reference for the monitor's own definition, plus a toggled run for four COLD elements written from the papers, so the spec fails if the monitor quietly starts behaving like the full algorithm.

Outputs per segment: [start, end, break, coefficientCount] as slices, 8 coefficient columns per band, RMSE and median change magnitude (NaN when the segment ended without a break), and a three-word cellSummary [segmentCount, status, fitCount]. Status bits: 1 no segment could be initialized, 2 rank failure, 8 maximumFitCount reached (partial result published), 16 more segments needed than maximumSegmentCount.

float32 limits: fits are the shared QR per band, so coefficients agree with the binary64 reference to about 2e-3 and RMSE to 1e-3 on bands of magnitude 0.2 to 0.6, and with pyxccd (itself float32) to 0.07 and 0.02 on bands of 600 to 3200. Per-thread working memory is checked at construction: 8 coefficients allow about 167 slices at 5 bands; 4 coefficients allow 256 slices.

GPURasterBFASTMonitor

BFAST Monitor (Verbesselt, Zeileis and Herold 2012): one least-squares fit of the harmonic and optionally trend model on the fixed history (historySliceCount leading slices), then OLS-MOSUM monitoring (Leisch, Hornik and Kuan 2000). With n valid history observations, h = floor(bandwidth * n), and residual scale sigma on n - k degrees of freedom, the process at observation t is sum(residuals of the last h observations) / (sigma * sqrt(n)), compared with lambda * sqrt(2 * log+(t / n)), where log+ is max(1, log). Windows reach back into the history. Missing observations are dropped, not interpolated.

criticalValue (lambda) is supplied by the caller. The bfast package and its critical-value tables are GPL and R-only, so neither is reproduced here; the fixture is a binary64 implementation written from the published definitions. Stable-history selection (ROC and Bai-Perron) is not included: the history is fixed.

Outputs: coefficients, scores [magnitude, maximumBoundaryRatio] (the median monitoring residual, bfastmonitor's magnitude, and the largest |MOSUM| / boundary), cellSummary [breakSlice, status, historyObservationCount], and optionally the MOSUM per monitoring slice. Status is 1 for too few valid history observations, 2 for a rank failure or zero residual scale.
