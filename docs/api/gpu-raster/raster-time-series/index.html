Raster time series

GPURasterHarmonicRegression, GPURasterClimateRunAccumulator, GPURasterGeometricMedianComposite, GPURasterBestAvailablePixel, GPURasterBFASTMonitor, GPURasterClimateAnomaly, GPURasterHANTS, GPURasterEOFProjection and GPURasterCOLDMonitor accept aligned chunked cell records. Keep each complete time series, mask and output record within a chunk; slice limits still apply per cell. Harmonic regression defaults to storage-backed QR; the other reference slice caps remain.

Both time-series execution routes use the same cell topology: packed fields can accompany a single-chunk vector when each field holds exactly cellCount * entriesPerCell entries. Once a field has multiple chunks, every cell field must have the same number of chunks and the same cell counts per chunk, with no split cell records. Shared date and parameter tables stay packed. Calls using only packed fields retain their documented spare-capacity support. HANTS and temporal resampling type their output views independently of the source view: a packed source can feed matching one-chunk outputs, and a one-chunk source can feed packed outputs.

Writable logical ranges must be disjoint across fields and chunks. Co-bound writable ranges must also have disjoint storage-aligned binding ranges. HANTS and temporal resampling can use one pooled output buffer under these rules. A dispatch's read-only inputs and writable outputs must use separate physical buffers, even for disjoint ranges. A physical buffer participating in writes must use one imported handle; a buffer used only by read-only inputs may have multiple imports. Pooling output fields requires an explicit per-dispatch access declaration in the contributor; callers without one retain separate output buffers because a later dispatch may read one output while writing another. HANTS and temporal resampling declare their accesses explicitly.

Contributors for stacks of raster time slices beyond regular composites: an irregular acquisition-date axis, streaming accumulators without a slice limit, penalised and harmonic smoothers, gap filling onto a regular grid, and climate indices. Import from @luma-contributors/analysis/raster-analysis. All are GPUCommandNodeProducer classes. Every contributor is cell-major (cell * sliceCount + slice) and takes mask per observation with zero rejecting a sample; non-finite values are missing. Each class documents its per-frame and compile-time props in its source.

Irregular date axis

GPURasterIrregularDateAxis declares a stack's acquisition dates: one uint32x2 Int64 (low, high) row per slice, with a GPUTemporalContext of encoding int64-words and {kind: 'irregular'}, any unit and epoch. Dates must be strictly increasing. They become centred fractional years in float32: years of 365.2425 days after the midpoint of the first and last acquisition, so the trend column is O(1) and harmonics stay in [-1, 1]. Elapsed years come from the Int64 words directly, so nearby dates keep full float32 precision. getGPURasterCentredFractionalYears gives the same axis on the CPU, for example to choose a regular output grid.

GPURasterTemporal, GPUChangeDetection and GPURasterSeasonalBreak take this axis as dateAxis. Omitted, slot behaviour and results are unchanged.

  • GPURasterTemporal: the Savitzky-Golay polynomial is fitted on real elapsed time over the same window of neighbouring acquisitions. Composites do not depend on time.
  • GPUChangeDetection: senSlope is per year of real elapsed time. Other outputs depend on order only.
  • GPURasterSeasonalBreak: period is in fractional years, the slope is per year, and missingPolicy: 'interpolate' uses elapsed time. Dummy seasons are defined on regular slots and are rejected.

GPURasterStreamingAccumulator

Accumulates counts, sums, squared deviations and extrema chunk by chunk, so a stack has no slice limit. Each dispatch consumes at most 256 slices; memory is O(cells x calendar slots). Slot indices assign a calendar slot (for example a month) to each chunk slice.

Set reset on the first chunk of a stream. activeSliceCount allows a short last chunk. Declare maximumValueMagnitude: a sample above it is treated as missing. Sums and squared deviations are 96-bit fixed-point integers (three uint32 words per slot, decoded with getGPURasterFixedPointValues), because float32 compensated summation was optimized away by the shader compiler on the tested GPU (the low word stayed zero). Fixed-point sums are exact to the declared unit, about maximumValueMagnitude * 2^-70 at the default stream length.

GPURasterWhittakerSmoother

Weighted Whittaker smoother per cell: minimises sum w (y - z)^2 + lambda |D z|^2 by a banded Givens QR of [sqrt(W); sqrt(lambda) D] on the values minus their per-cell mean (added back afterwards; the smoother is exact on constants), with zero weight on missing observations so gaps are filled. D is the order d difference (1 to 3). With dateAxis it is the divided difference on acquisition dates, scaled by the mean spacing, so a regular axis reduces to ordinary differences and lambda keeps its usual meaning. lambda is limited to 1e7.

Fixed robust reweighting passes: Tukey bisquare on the residual over 6 MAD, or upper-envelope for cloud-depressed series. Reference: the phenofit smooth_wWHIT definition (Eilers 2003, divided differences); R is not installed, so the fixtures use a binary64 SciPy solve_banded implementation of the same algorithm (test/oracles/rts02-whittaker-hants-scipy.py). phenofit itself remains a pending oracle.

float32 limits: the result agrees with a binary64 solve to about one float32 step of the published value for every difference order and every lambda up to the cap of 1e7 (256 slices, series at 0.5, 300 and 3000 with one gap in five), and to within 2e-5 on the irregular fixture series. The error does not grow with the offset of the data (kelvin, scaled reflectance). Status 2 means the triangular factor has a zero or non-finite diagonal or the solution is not finite, for example lambda = 0 with a gap; it is not raised for a system that is only badly conditioned. Very sparse support is solved, with status 0: 2, 3 or 5 valid observations out of 256 slices, at either end, in the middle or spread out, agree with an exact rational solve (test/oracles/rts02-whittaker-sparse-exact.py) for lambda from 1 to 1e7. There, orders 2 and 3 continue the curve as a line or a parabola across the gap and the float32 error grows with the gap: over 250 slices beyond 3 or 5 adjacent observations it stays within 6e-4 (order 2) and 1.3e-2 (order 3) of the spread of the curve, and within one float32 step of the values for order 1 or for observations spread over the series (order 3 spread: 1e-3 of the spread).

GPURasterHANTS

Harmonic ANalysis of Time Series (Roerink, Menenti and Verhoef 2000; GRASS r.hants): a least-squares harmonic fit (getRasterLeastSquaresWGSL) with a bounded reject-and-refit loop that drops the worst observation beyond the fit-error tolerance on the chosen side. A cell out of rejection passes publishes its latest fit and reports status 8 (converged = 0). 2 * harmonicCount + 1 + degreeOfOverdetermination observations are always kept. Coefficients are centred on the axis midpoint. The GRASS module is a definition reference only; the fixture is a binary64 NumPy implementation, and GRASS itself remains a pending oracle.

Working memory is checked at construction: 256 slices allow two harmonics; 128 slices allow six.

GPURasterTemporalResample

Fills a regular time grid (gridStart, gridStep in centred fractional years, up to 4096 points) by linear interpolation between bracketing valid observations. Grid points outside the observed range get status 1; points inside a gap larger than maximumGap get status 2. Nothing is extrapolated. A Whittaker or HANTS output is finite in its gaps, so it feeds this node directly, which then feeds regular-time operations.

Compositing and phenology

Per-cell compositing across the slices of a stack, and phenology metrics on a smoothed curve. The multivariate contributors use the layout (cell * sliceCount + slice) * bandCount + band and treat an observation with any non-finite band as missing as a whole; mask is per observation (cell * sliceCount + slice) and zero rejects it. A cell without a valid observation publishes NaN and 0xffffffff as its slice.

Reference: hdmedians (Apache-2.0) defines nanmedoid and the geometric median. hdmedians is not installed here, so the fixture test/oracles/fixtures/rts05-compositing-numpy.json comes from binary64 NumPy implementations of those definitions (test/oracles/rts05-compositing-numpy.py); the geometric median is also checked against the true minimiser found by SciPy. TIMESAT parity and double-logistic fits are out of scope: the latter are ill-conditioned in float32.

GPURasterMedoidComposite

The valid observation with the smallest sum of Euclidean distances (over all bands) to the other valid observations. The result is a real acquisition, so output.sliceIndex is the provenance of every band. Ties go to the earliest slice. Work is O(slices^2 * bands) per cell on one invocation, so sliceCount is limited to 64 and bandCount to 16. Float32 distance sums that differ by less than rounding can pick a different slice than binary64.

GPURasterGeometricMedianComposite

The point minimising the sum of Euclidean distances to the valid observations over all bands together (Roberts, Mueller and McIntyre 2017): band relationships are preserved, so an index computed after compositing equals the index computed before. The result is synthetic. It runs Weiszfeld's recursion from the mean for exactly iterationCount iterations (at most 64, no data-dependent exit), with each distance floored at distanceEpsilon. output.lastStep is the length of the last step, the convergence indicator to read when the observations are few or skewed. The fixture cells agree with binary64 to within 2e-5 and sit within 1e-5 relative of the SciPy minimum after 32 iterations.

Call getGPURasterGeometricMedianCompositeOutputLayout({cellCount, bandCount, lastStep}) before allocating outputs. It returns scalar formats and element counts without allocating storage. lastStep defaults to false: its descriptor is absent unless requested. The contributor's validation, GPURasterGeometricMedianCompositeOperation, the CPU operation's graph outputs and the pure CPU result's allocation all use this layout. The pure CPU function always returns lastStep; the operation publishes it only when output.lastStep names a graph value.

{/* geometric-median-output-layout:start */}

Output field Scalar format Element count Condition and meaning
composite float32 cellCount * bandCount Always: cell * bandCount + band; NaN when the cell has no valid observation.
status uint32 cellCount Always: per-cell code 0 for a composite, 1 for no valid observation.

The table is checked against the public layout in the node tests. Counts and the cellCount * bandCount product must be positive integers no greater than 4,294,967,295. The layout covers both execution forms; GPU execution additionally limits each cell to 256 slices, 16 bands and 64 iterations. The CPU twin retains the iteration budget and uint32 indexing limits, and allows larger slice, band and iteration counts. The GPU operation uses exact-sized packed graph values; a direct contributor also accepts packed output views with spare capacity and nonzero byte offsets, writing only the used prefix. Chunked cell fields must follow the shared time-series topology rules above. Output formats must match the table, including uint32 status.

For example, two cells with three bands need six float32 composite entries and two uint32 status entries. Requesting lastStep: true adds two float32 entries. Input masks and output buffers remain caller-owned.

GPURasterBestAvailablePixel

Best-available-pixel scoring (Griffiths et al. 2013): score = wDay * exp(-0.5 (dDay / sigma)^2) + wYear * max(0, 1 - |year - targetYear| / maximumYearDistance) + wCloud * clamp((cloudDistance - minimum) / (maximum - minimum), 0, 1), with dDay the circular distance in days to the target day of year. The cell takes the highest score, the earliest slice on ties, and optionally its band values. Per-slice day of year and year come in sliceAttributes. cloudDistance is per observation, in pixels to the nearest cloud or shadow of its slice: run GPUDistanceField on each slice's cloud mask and pass the result (positive infinity means no cloud in reach; without the view the cloud score is zero).

GPURasterPhenologyMetrics

Up to two seasons per cell from amplitude thresholds on the smoothed curve, for example output.smoothed of GPURasterWhittakerSmoother (same cell * sliceCount + slice layout; pass the same dateAxis and times are centred fractional years, otherwise slice indices). Base is the curve minimum and amplitude the maximum minus the minimum. A season starts where the curve last falls below startFraction of the amplitude before its peak and ends where it first falls below endFraction after it, both by linear interpolation (the Copernicus MR-VPP definitions use 0.25 and 0.15). The first season is the highest sample; the second is the highest sample outside the first season's span that reaches secondSeasonMinimumFraction. Seasons are ordered by peak time. A crossing that is not found cuts the season at the series end or at the other season and sets a bit of GPU_RASTER_PHENOLOGY_FLAG. output.metrics holds the ten fields of GPU_RASTER_PHENOLOGY_METRIC per season: start, end, peak time, length, peak value, base value, amplitude, left and right slope, and the integral between the curve and the base from start to end. Cell status is 1 for a non-finite curve and 2 for an amplitude below minimumAmplitude.

SPI and SPEI

GPURasterStandardizedIndex evaluates up to 16,777,216 slices per chunk, subject to device bindings. Calibration keeps its separate per-dispatch limit. Cell records and aligned outputs accept chunked input without changing the fitted calendar-slot model.

GPURasterStandardizedIndexCalibration streams calibration chunks (for example 30 years of months) into per-slot statistics, with zeros and non-positive values excluded from the fit and counted, and writes a fit per calendar slot. GPURasterStandardizedIndex then evaluates any chunk against that fit. Pass the rolling mean output for aggregations such as SPI-3. Both match xclim's method='APP' (Thom's gamma estimator for gamma with floc, the method-of-moments estimator for fisk with floc) and xclim's defaults for zero inflation (prob_zero_interpolation='upper', plotting_position_zero='ecdf'). location is the fixed floc used for water-balance series that may be negative.

Reference: xclim 1.0.0, Apache-2.0, run for test/oracles/fixtures/rts06-climate-indices-xclim.json (numba and dask imports stubbed; see the fixture). SPI-1, SPI-3 and SPEI agree with xclim to within 0.01 over 420 months in float32.

float32 limits and what the code does about them:

  • APP, not iterative ML. Newton iterations on the shape are noisy in float32; APP is closed form. Only sums over the slot enter it.
  • Fit sums are fixed-point, not float32. The gamma estimator is driven by ln(mean) - mean(ln x), a difference of nearly equal numbers. The sums of x, ln x and squared deviations are exact 96-bit integers, so the only float32 rounding is the final conversion to a mean.
  • Tails are computed directly. The gamma CDF and quantile near 1 cannot be represented as 1 - p in float32, so whichever tail is small is computed directly (series below, continued fraction above) and the quantile is taken of that tail. Results stay accurate on both sides.
  • Clipped to +-8.21, xclim's bound. Tails flush to zero near 1e-38 in float32, well beyond that.
  • The fit needs at least two positive values per slot; a slot without a usable fit marks every output NaN with status 2.
