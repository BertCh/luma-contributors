Bounded statistical estimation and validation

These contributors extend the existing spatial statistics and regression graph with explicit numerical, missingness and convergence contracts. All computation is recorded in the caller's GPU graph. They neither submit work nor read buffers back, and have no CPU fallback. Inputs and outputs are caller-owned packed views; the graph owns transient workspace. Each generated kernel stays within the default eight-storage-buffer limit.

The supported modes below are bounded subsets of the ST01–ST11 backlog. They do not establish complete PySAL, mgwr, blockCV or spint parity. Exact capacity counts, converged numerical estimates, Monte Carlo inference, exact leave-one-out model selection and bounded continuous profile optimization are different guarantees.

IV covariance and robust spatial 2SLS

GPUInstrumentalVariablesCovariance takes structural design Z, instrument design H and structural residuals from the same fitted IV model. Both designs omit the implicit intercept. The coefficient count is 1–15; the instrument count is between the coefficient count and 16; there are at most 2^24 observations (float32 counts), and fewer when the design, instrument or score rows exceed the device's storage binding size. All estimation rows must be finite. There is no silent complete-case change after a model has already been fitted.

Every row sum (column means, the centered moments H'H, H'Z, diag(Z'Z) and the meat matrix) is a row-tile reduction merged in a fixed pairwise order, so results are reproducible; one invocation solves the small projection system. The HAC kernel is applied over its CSR neighbors, one invocation per row (O(nnz · p)), never over all pairs.

The covariance uses the projected-design influence matrix, not an OLS sandwich applied to endogenous regressors. Columns are centered and norm-scaled before both Cholesky solves. The intercept influence is recovered with its cross terms, so the output includes the full (coefficientCount + 1)² covariance in intercept-first row-major order.

  • HC0 uses squared residual scores without a finite-sample multiplier.
  • HC1 multiplies HC0 by n / (n - p), where p includes the intercept.
  • HC2 and HC3 divide residuals by sqrt(1-h) and 1-h, respectively, using projected-design leverage including the intercept. A denominator at most 1e-6 or leverage below -1e-6 is invalid.
  • CLUSTER requires dense uint32 clusters and clusterCount capacity; only occupied groups count in the CR1 multiplier G/(G-1) * (n-1)/(n-p). At least two groups must be occupied.
  • HAC takes a separate spatial kernel, without a finite-sample multiplier. It must be symmetric, nonnegative, positive semidefinite and have unit diagonal. Bounds, finite weights, symmetry and diagonal are checked on GPU. Positive semidefiniteness is a caller precondition.

The returned covariance is certified PSD to relative tolerance 1e-5 with a shifted Cholesky test; zero and rank-deficient covariance are accepted. This does not certify the entire HAC kernel. Covariance status is 0 success, 1 singular projection, 2 insufficient observations, 3 nonfinite inputs/results, 4 invalid HAC kernel, 5 invalid leverage, 6 invalid clusters and 7 a failed PSD certificate. Failed covariance entries are NaN.

For spatial 2SLS, set robust: {method, kernel?, clusters?, clusterCount?, output: {covariance, status}}. Existing point estimates and classical summary diagnostics remain unchanged; the coefficient table's SE, z and p-value columns use the robust covariance. The robust status is separate from the estimator's status. A robust covariance does not change the classical Anselin–Kelejian diagnostic's assumptions.

Regimes, panels and SUR

SUR, including spatial SUR, and panel-error fits support 16,777,216 total rows with tiled reductions; panel periods cannot exceed the total row count. Device bindings may impose lower limits.

GPURestrictedRegimeRegression.common includes the intercept followed by each predictor. A true entry estimates one shared coefficient; a false entry estimates one coefficient per regime. Output order is source-column-major, with regime order inside each varying column. The unrestricted model is limited to 16 coefficients; its complete cases are exactly those of the restricted model. Covariance uses the restricted model's pooled SSE / (n - p) estimate. The Chow output contains [F, numeratorDegrees, denominatorDegrees]. Optional stability contains [Wald chi-square, degrees, upper-tail p] for each source coefficient and then jointly. It tests equality of the varying regime coefficients, matching spreg.Chow; common columns are [NaN, 0, NaN]. This is a different hypothesis from the nested-model F test. Empty or singular regimes are explicit, and a singular unrestricted fit does not erase a valid restricted coefficient estimate. The joint test assumes independent homoskedastic normal errors. Rows (at most 2^24, and within the device's binding size for the predictors) are reduced in parallel: per-regime means and centered moments come from a stable regime sort and one workgroup per regime, one invocation solves both nested systems, and the residual sums of squares are row-tile sums merged in a fixed pairwise order.

Both systems are solved in a shifted basis, never from raw cross-products, so a predictor with a large mean (a year, an elevation, a projected northing) costs no precision. With a varying intercept the predictors and the response are centered on their regime means; with a common intercept the response and the common-slope predictors are centered on their pooled means. A varying slope under a common intercept is not invariant to a shift, so when such a predictor has a mean larger than its spread the fit changes basis instead: x - mean takes the place of the constant and the raw columns x 1[g] stay (1 = (sum_g x 1[g] - (x - mean)) / mean), which spans the same model with a conditioning that does not depend on the size of the mean. The published coefficients and covariance are those of the raw design. Regime means are float32, so the sums of the deviations from them are carried through the moments and every difference of a value and its shift is fixed before it meets a small term (the shader compiler of the test GPU otherwise regroups (y - mean) - fit as y - (mean + fit)): a response whose mean is far above its residuals keeps its Chow statistic. The Chow numerator is the direct sum of squared differences of the two fits (equal to SSE_restricted - SSE_unrestricted), and each Wald contrast is formed from differences of shifts before it meets the coefficients. A system is singular (status 2 restricted, 3 unrestricted) when a Cholesky pivot of the unit-diagonal normal matrix is below 1e-3, the limit of GPUOrdinaryLeastSquares; GPUSpatialRegimeRegression, GPUSeeminglyUnrelatedRegression, GPUProbitRegression and GPUSpatialPanelLagMaximumLikelihood apply the same limit.

GPUSpatialPanelErrorMaximumLikelihood takes the existing spatial-error ML arguments plus periodCount. Data are balanced and period-major; the supplied weights describe only the entity axis. Unit means are removed on GPU, and I_T ⊗ W supplies the repeated spatial determinant. The objective and variance divisor use N*T, matching the profiled fixed-effects Gaussian ML convention. This is not REML or a short-panel bias correction. It inherits the existing bounded trace-series search, boundary and approximation diagnostics. The absorbed intercept table row and information criteria are NaN. Predictors constant through time are singular after demeaning. Optional diagnostics.tests returns statistic/p-value pairs for pooled-panel LM error, robust LM lag and robust LM error. These fit pooled OLS on the original panel and use its residual variance, spatial lags and projected spatial fitted values, matching spreg 1.8.3. That variance is RSS / (n - p) (spreg ols.sig2, n = N*T, p = predictorCount + 1), where the cross-section LM tests of GPUSpatialRegressionDiagnostics use RSS / n (spreg LMtests): on the same residuals and block-diagonal weights a panel LM error is ((n - p) / n)^2 times the cross-section one, so compare each with its own reference and not with the other. Diagnostic status is 0 valid, 1 failed/incomplete pooled fit, or 2 degenerate/nonfinite test information. The robust tests require positive spatial fitted-value information beyond the weight trace. They are specification tests, not clustered uncertainty for the FE estimator.

GPUSeeminglyUnrelatedRegression requires 2–4 equations with the same predictor count but potentially different predictor values. Data are equation-major, then row-major; observation IDs must be aligned explicitly. Any nonfinite value in any equation excludes that observation from all equations. The total number of coefficients, including each intercept, is at most 16. Equation OLS residuals estimate a joint cross-equation covariance with divisor n, followed by one full feasible-GLS solve. The output covariance is the conditional GLS information inverse using that first-stage error covariance. Singular design and singular error covariance have separate status codes. This is one-step FGLS, not an iterated likelihood fit.

Optional spatial selects the spreg.SURerrorGM convention: equation OLS residuals estimate Kelejian–Prucha spatial-error parameters; filtering transforms responses, designs and original OLS residuals before the joint GLS solve. The intercept and full covariance are transformed back to original units, and reported residuals are structural residuals. This selected mode requires complete aligned rows and nonnegative row-standardized zero-diagonal weights without islands; it rejects a mask or caller-supplied initialResiduals. The shared weights are checked once with bounded row tiles, and the resulting admissibility flag applies to every equation. Each equation has its own parameter and GM status; status 4 denotes inadmissible weights. Nonspatial initialResiduals allows an explicit first-stage residual covariance, with the same common complete-case mask.

Probit and local collinearity

GPUProbitRegression uses centered/scaled predictors, Fisher scoring and monotone likelihood backtracking. It accepts 1–11 predictors and at most 2^24 rows (fewer when the predictors, or the diagnostic residual rows, exceed the device's binding size). Each iteration is a row-tile pass for the score and information, a one-invocation solve, one row-tile pass that evaluates all 24 halved line-search steps, and a one-invocation step choice; iterations are unrolled (estimatedCommandNodeCount, about 6 · maximumIterations) and gated on the GPU once the fit stops. Complete cases are selected jointly; included responses must be exactly 0 or 1. The expected-information SEs are model based. Marginal effects average the normal density over estimation rows, then multiply by each slope; they are not effects evaluated at the means.

Its summary is [completeRows, logLikelihood, deviance, iterations]. Status 0 means converged, 1 insufficient data, 2 singular information, 3 not converged and 4 nonbinary data or separated data. Three invariants hold:

  • Status 0 is a maximum.
  • Status 4 for binary data means separated, and only with a certificate: a direction of the coefficients that provably moves no row towards the wrong side and some row towards the right side. Data that are not separated are never status 4.
  • Everything else that is not status 1 or 2 is status 3, with the last iterate written (and NaN standard errors): iterations or line-search steps exhausted, a step pinned by the bound on misclassified rows, a linear predictor that overflowed, or a small step on data that float32 could not tell from separated. An ordinary well-conditioned fit is never status 3 at the default settings. Separated data whose certificate the search does not find are status 3 (or 2, when the information matrix of the runaway fit loses rank first), never 0.

Status 3 writes the iterate however large its linear predictors are: 2,001 rows that converge in 7 steps, stopped after 3 with a correctly classified row beyond a linear predictor of 16, are status 3 with their iterate. Every other failure writes NaN coefficients. All-zero/all-one outcomes are status 4.

fitted, marginalEffects and the diagnostic residuals are computed from the centered, scaled design of the fit and its estimate, not from the published raw coefficients: the raw intercept is a - sum mean_j b_j in float32, which for a predictor near 2^24 with a spread of five float32 steps is rounded to a step of 0.5. Rebuilt from the raw coefficients such a fit had fitted probabilities of 0.185, 0.421, 0.691 where statsmodels has 0.136, 0.345, 0.618, and a marginal effect 2.7% low; they now agree to 1e-7. The slopes were right throughout.

The likelihood of a row is log Phi(s eta) (s = +1 for a response of 1, -1 for 0), with Phi evaluated through erfc so that its lower tail keeps relative accuracy down to the smallest normal f32; the score and the information weight are ratios of the density to the two tail probabilities, and no 1 - p is formed. A row whose probability is within float32 rounding of 0 or 1 therefore still pulls the fit as the likelihood requires. (fitted and the spatial diagnostics use probabilities floored at 1e-7.)

  • A row on the right side of the fit is not bounded: once its probability rounds to 1 it adds nothing to the likelihood. 2,000 rows from a latent index 0.2 + 2x plus one correctly classified row at x = 9 (a linear predictor of 18.9 at the maximum) return the maximum of the other rows, slope 2.072, with status 0, as statsmodels Probit does. So does a fit whose maximum has a largest linear predictor of 12.06.
  • A row on the wrong side leaves the float32 range of Phi near 13, so the line search accepts no step that takes such a row beyond ±12. A fit is pinned when that bound, not the likelihood, shortened the accepted step and the shortened step is below tolerance while the full Fisher step is not: status 3 with the pinned iterate (it was status 4 with NaN coefficients; the data are not separated). The same 2,000 rows plus a response of 0 at x = 9 are such a fit (the float64 maximum is a slope of 1.447 with that row at 13.2; the iterate has a slope of 1.314 with that row at 12); a response of 0 at x = 4 is not (slope 1.862, that row at 7.7, returned with status 0).
  • Separated data have no finite maximum: some direction of the coefficients moves every row to the right side or leaves it where it is. The fit certifies separation by exhibiting such a direction, not by the size of a linear predictor, and a direction counts only when the sign of every row's move is known. There is no tolerance: a move towards the wrong side disqualifies a direction however small it is, and a row whose sign float32 cannot decide is not counted as standing still. Three kinds of direction are tested, each reduced over the rows with maxima of flags, which are exact whatever the row count:
    • A column that separates at its lowest or highest value: the rows above the lowest value of a column (or below its highest) all have the same response. Raw values are compared, which is exact, and the test does not depend on the fit. An indicator of a group of rows that all have a response of 1 is certified this way at any maximumIterations and any tolerance: one or three rows of 600, an indicator among eleven predictors, every second row, one row of 100,000, three of 200,000, five of 400,000, five and twenty of 1,000,000.
    • A single-column threshold from the Fisher direction. With its rounding taken out (a column whose largest contribution to any row is below 1e-4 of the largest contribution of any column is dropped; the intercept is set so that the rows at an end of the kept columns do not move when their move is below that level), a direction with one column left is sign · (x - value). Rows are compared with value, and in the next pass with the two data values next to it, so a column that separates at an interior value where both responses occur is certified one iteration after the direction points there: quasi-complete separation, and a three-valued predictor whose rows with both responses are at its middle value (100,002 rows; status 3 at the defaults before, status 2 with 128 iterations and status 0 with a tolerance of 1e-3).
    • The Fisher direction itself, for separation by a combination of columns. The move of a row takes its sign from the float32 value only when that is beyond the evaluation error bound, (predictorCount + 5) 2^-23 of the sum of the magnitudes of the row's terms; a row all of whose terms are exactly zero stands still; any other row is undecided and the direction is not a certificate. Complete separation by two predictors is certified this way. Quasi-complete separation by a combination of columns, whose tied rows have moves that cancel to rounding, is not: 606 such rows ended as status 2 at the defaults and status 3 with 8 or 16 iterations. A certificate is kept once found, and a certified fit is status 4 however it ends: iterations exhausted, a singular information matrix (without a certificate that is status 2), a failed line search, an overflowing linear predictor, or a step below tolerance. statsmodels Probit does not converge on any of these scenes, and a linear program over directions (SciPy linprog) finds a separating direction on each.
  • What the certificates need. A column that separates at an end of its range: nothing. Complete separation by one predictor: a budget of two iterations; by two predictors: seven; quasi-complete separation at an interior value: five (measured). With a smaller maximumIterations those are status 3. The tolerance no longer matters for an indicator (one row of 600 at a tolerance of 1e-2 was status 0 with a coefficient of 3.94 and a standard error of 5.39; it is status 4).
  • Data that are not separated are never status 4. 1,000 rows (x = -1, y = 0), 2,000 rows (1, 1) and the rows (-1e-5, 1) and (1e-5, 0) have a finite maximum at a slope of 6.02; the first Fisher direction moves the two central rows towards the wrong side by 1.5e-5 of their own terms, which a tolerance of 1e-3 took for separation (status 4 at every budget). They now converge in 23 steps to the statsmodels maximum (slope within 2e-6 standard errors), and are status 3 with their iterate when the budget is smaller. 60 samples of 25 to 69 rows with one to four predictors that a linear program and statsmodels agree are not separated are all status 0, as are an indicator group with both outcomes at 600, 20,000 and 100,000 rows and two rare-event fits.
  • A candidate direction that moves no row towards the wrong side as far as float32 can tell but leaves some row undecided is remembered, and a later step below tolerance is then status 3, not 0. No scene of the specs needs this guard.

Optional spatialDiagnostics uses the fitted probit probability and generalized/standardized residuals to report [Pinkse chi-square, p, Kelejian–Prucha z, two-sided p, Pinkse–Slade chi-square, p]. Weights must have a sorted zero-diagonal CSR; the induced common complete-case graph is used. Diagnostic status is 0 valid, 1 failed/no-case model, 2 invalid weights, 3 zero test information. This does not estimate latent spatial probit.

GPULocalCollinearity uses supplied local estimation weights, including diagonal weights if the fit uses the focal observation. Every predictor uses the same complete neighbors. VIFs are the diagonal of the inverse weighted correlation matrix; the condition index is the square root of its maximum/minimum eigenvalue ratio. The intercept is excluded from this correlation calculation. Paired optional designConditionNumbers and varianceDecomposition reproduce mgwr's intercept-inclusive column-normalized design convention: multiply each design row by normalized spatial weight, not its square root, and report the variance proportion associated with the smallest singular value. A bounded Jacobi eigensolver returns status 3 on exhaustion; status 1 means insufficient neighbors and 2 singular/nonfinite correlation or design. The eigenvalue threshold is 1e-7 of the largest eigenvalue, an explicit f32 conditioning limit.

GPUGeneralizedGeographicallyWeightedRegression.inference returns local scale-one Poisson/logistic standard errors and diagonal influence. The covariance is A^-1 X' V W² X A^-1, with A = X' V W X; it matches mgwr GLM local uncertainty and does not assume that spatial weights are frequency weights. Failed fits retain NaN inference.

bandwidthSelection supplies 2–32 positive candidate bandwidths and outputs their exact leave-one-out mean deviance, the selected bandwidth and status (0 selected, 1 no valid candidate). Adaptive candidates must be integer neighbor counts within maximumNeighborCount. Each inner fit excludes its own focus, including from adaptive neighbor selection. Every eligible training row must converge for a candidate to be valid; ties choose the first candidate. The logistic deviance of a row is taken from its leave-one-out linear predictor, 2 log(1 + exp(-eta)) for a response of 1 and 2 log(1 + exp(eta)) for 0, so a confident prediction scores finitely (the float32 fitted mean is exactly 1 from eta of about 17 and cannot carry it). This is the default criterion: 'loo-deviance'. Optional criterion: 'aicc' uses in-sample fits and the mgwr Poisson/Binomial deviance/influence correction. method: 'golden-section' searches bounded fixed or adaptive AICc bandwidths with GPU gates and named convergence/budget status; see Adaptive generalized GWR. The pinned oracle executes mgwr local GLM solves with the self weight removed to verify the exact candidate scores.

For nested spatial evaluation, supply one outer fold's training mask and set predictExcluded: true. Bandwidth selection, centering/solves and inference use only training rows, while finite held-out predictor/position queries receive predictions without reading their responses. The direct test changes held-out counts from zero to 100,000 and verifies unchanged candidate scores, selected bandwidth, coefficients and predictions. Apply this graph separately to each fold; score held-out responses afterward. Finite query offsets remain caller inputs.

Joint randomization and rank dynamics

GPUMultivariateLocalGearyPermutationTest preserves the existing GPUMultivariateLocalGeary statistic API and adds a separate inference producer. It supports at most 256 observations, 16 variables and 4096 permutations (maximumPermutations, fixed when compiling the graph). A common complete-case mask applies to all variables; population standardization is performed once. Active local weights are row-standardized. The focal vector is fixed while distinct whole neighbor vectors are sampled without replacement. Independent column permutations would destroy the cross-variable dependence and are not used.

The conservative equal-tail Monte Carlo p-value is min(1, 2 * min((lower + 1)/(P + 1), (upper + 1)/(P + 1))), counting ties in both tails. tail: 'esda-min-tail' instead computes (min(upper, P-upper)+1)/(P+1), where upper counts simulated values greater than or equal to the observed value. This reproduces esda's undoubled, tie-asymmetric tail convention; equal-tail remains the default. Optional Bonferroni adjustment uses all included rows, including islands, as the family. Classes are 0 nonsignificant, 1 significant similarity, 2 significant dissimilarity and 0xffffffff unknown. Constant variables invalidate the joint statistic; islands are unknown. Optional simulations expose every row/replicate draw for replay. Seed words and the shared Philox conditional-permutation purpose determine the stream.

GPURankMarkov follows the pinned giddy 2.3.6 rank conventions with zero-based descending ranks. Equal values receive distinct ranks; default tieOrder: 'giddy' assigns the higher descending rank to the later original row, matching ascending ordinal ranks reversed by giddy. tieOrder: 'row-id' preserves earlier-row-first ties explicitly. Any missing value removes the row from all periods, avoiding changing rank populations over time. Full rank follows a location through rank states; geographic rank follows the occupant of a fixed rank through location states. The original n × n output capacity is retained when rows are excluded. Empty transition rows stay zero. Optional existing Markov diagnostics retain their ergodicity assumptions; optional mobility reports mean absolute rank movement per adjacent period, NaN below two complete rows.

Weights and spatial evaluation

GPUFuzzyContiguity uses the existing feature→polygon→ring→vertex layout, with a packed float32x2 position view. Polygon distance combines segment distances with even-odd filled-part containment, preserving holes and multipart features. Rings close implicitly. tolerance is the maximum separation, equivalent geometrically to intersecting exact round buffers of half that radius; it does not build approximated buffer polygons or use grid snapping. Features are limited to 65,536 and total vertices to 65,536. Candidate pairs come from a BVH over feature bounds expanded by the tolerance, and only candidates run the exact distance test, so no feature-pair workspace is stored. The BVH leaves are sorted along a Morton curve of the bound centers, so the search cost does not depend on the input order (16,384 shuffled squares take about 5 ms on the headless test GPU, against 110 ms with leaves in source order); the emitted pairs are radix-sorted by row and neighbor, so each row stays ascending. The query box widening scales with both the coordinates and the tolerance, so a pair the distance test accepts is found from both features and the CSR stays symmetric. Feature status distinguishes valid, empty and malformed/nonfinite input. Simple valid rings are required; self intersections and near-degenerate f32 predicates are not repaired. Edges are binary and ordered, with added = 1 only for strictly positive separation. Output overflow reports the exact required neighbor count.

GPUIslandAttachment selects nearest finite supplied representatives for original empty CSR rows, with lowest-ID distance ties. Selection is simultaneous, so islands may attach to each other. Reciprocal insertion is the default; directed insertion is explicit. It preserves existing weights and uses weight 1 only for added edges. Empty means no stored slots, not a zero-weight row. Nearest comparisons use a shared distance scale to avoid squared-distance overflow or underflow for finite float32 representatives. nearestSearch selects how the nearest is found and never changes the result: 'scan' tests every representative per island (islands × rows), 'grid' takes the 16 nearest candidates of each island from a GPUNeighborSearch grid and decides among them with the scan's own comparison, and 'auto' (the default) scans up to GPU_ISLAND_ATTACHMENT_SCAN_MAXIMUM_ROW_COUNT (4,096) rows and uses the grid above. Ties go to the lowest row on both paths. An island whose candidate list cannot prove the answer (the farthest candidate is not clearly farther than the chosen one, or the coordinates are so large that the grid bounds or squared distances overflow) rescans every representative, so the result always equals the scan's; such islands cost the scan, one pass over all rows (up to 65,535) in a single invocation. That fallback is never silent: the optional output.status.uncertainCount holds the exact number of islands that used it (always 0 with 'scan'). A count near the island count means the representatives are too tied (15 or more at one address) or too large for the candidate list to help: use 'scan' or deduplicate. On 60,000 shuffled rows with 25 percent islands the grid took 7 ms against 52 ms for the scan (Apple GPU, second run, readback included). Writing the output costs each row's own degree. No representative is inferred, and no implicit normalization is performed. Truncated CSR remains ordered and bounded, with required counts, attachment IDs and per-edge provenance.

GPUSpatialCrossValidation deals explicit blocks to folds in a seeded random order. Every block that holds a finite unmasked row draws one Philox word keyed by the uint32 seed and the block ID; the blocks are ranked by that word (ties by block ID) and the block of rank r goes to fold r % foldCount. Fold sizes therefore differ by at most one block (not one row), no fold is empty when there are at least foldCount such blocks, and a different seed gives a different order. With fewer blocks than folds the empty folds remain visible in status. The fold of a block depends on the seed and on the set of blocks that hold a usable row: masking rows without emptying a block changes nothing, masking whole blocks re-deals the rest (ranking masked blocks too would leave folds empty), so GPUNestedInterpolationValidation deals its inner folds anew for each outer fold. Every finite unmasked position appears in exactly one test fold. A training point is excluded when its planar distance from any held-out point is less than or equal to bufferDistance. Distance zero also excludes duplicate positions in different blocks. Invalid coordinates enter neither training nor testing. The baseline performs exact all-pairs checks and is not an indexed large-domain performance claim.

GPUSpatialCrossValidatedOLS composes those masks with a separate OLS fit for each fold. OLS centering and all estimation statistics use training rows only. It predicts held-out rows using that fold's centered slopes and mean correction, preserving precision at large predictor origins, and reports [scoredRows, missingTestRows, MSE, MAE, bias, R²]. Bias is prediction minus observation. Metric status bits report no scored rows, missing coverage and undefined R² for constant test outcomes. Changing held-out responses cannot change that fold's fit. Generalized GWR supplies a training-only bandwidthSelection graph as described above; GPUNestedInterpolationValidation supplies reusable inner-fold covariance/variogram and RBF bandwidth selection, outer refitting and held-out metrics using those masks.

Calibrated spatial interaction

GPUSpatialInteraction fits a one-covariate Poisson mean proportional to exp(coefficient * covariate) over an explicit finite coefficient grid or continuous bounded search. Supported constraints are unconstrained total mass, origin totals, destination totals and both margins. The latter uses bounded iterative proportional fitting. Default calibration: 'grid' retains a finite-grid optimum. calibration: 'bounded' takes exactly two ascending coefficient endpoints and uses golden-section profile optimization, with maximumCalibrationIterations (1–64, default 32) and coefficientTolerance (default 1e-5). The profiled deviance is optimized over the explicit interval; optimum-boundary and f32 flat-objective behavior remain visible in the interval. Candidates with exponent spans above 80 are rejected explicitly.

Rows are explicit OD count cells. pairState distinguishes structural zero (0), observed (1) and missing (2). Missing cells are excluded from both support and margins and predict NaN; structural zeros must have observed flow zero and predict zero. Observed zero counts remain in the support. Duplicate supplied OD rows are independent cells; aggregate duplicates explicitly when appropriate. Zero observed margins remain zero. Dense routes support at most 4096 cells, 128 origins, 128 destinations and 128 coefficient candidates. The sparse doubly constrained route uses explicit pair tables within its device and command budget; candidate predictions occupy transient storage.

The selected summary is [coefficient, deviance, maximumRelativeMarginError, iterations]. Each candidate exposes deviance, error, iterations and status. Unconverged candidates are never selected as converged fits. If none succeeds, predictions are NaN with a failure status. Optional origin/destination residuals are observed minus predicted margins. A successful fit is conditional on the specified support and search domain; coefficient uncertainty is unavailable. Optional bounded-only output.calibration is [lower, upper, width, converged]. Status 4 retains the best finite fit but reports an exhausted coefficient search; inner IPF failure remains separate. The final two candidates are exposed in bounded mode. Direct spint GLM fixtures cover all four constraint modes with the same explicit support.

Random and unit-effects spatial lag maximum likelihood

GPUSpatialPanelLagMaximumLikelihood: Auto selects tiled unit effects when entity eigenvalues are supplied. Tiled fits support 1,024 entities and 16,777,216 total rows, subject to device bindings; reference fits retain 32 entities and 32 periods.

GPUSpatialPanelLagMaximumLikelihood fits y = rho Wy + X beta + unitEffect + error on complete balanced panels in period-major order. The random-effects fit alternates analytic variance-component shrinkage phi with an exact dense log-determinant profile search for rho; unit effects use within-entity demeaning. The reference capacity is 2–32 entities, 2–32 periods and 1–7 predictors. With fitMode selecting tiled unit effects and a supplied entity spectrum, the bound is 1,024 entities and 16,777,216 total rows, subject to device bindings. Weights must be nonnegative, row-standardized, zero-diagonal CSR with sorted unique neighbors and no islands. Missing observations invalidate the complete fit.

Both GPU routes and the CPU twin refuse incomplete weights: nonzero weights.status.overflow[0] (final capacity loss) or weights.status.candidateOverflow[0] (candidate loss) returns status 1 with NaN coefficients, covariance, summary, fitted values and residuals. Clearing the input flags permits a subsequent submission to fit normally; the two input flags retain their distinct meanings.

CPUSpatialPanelLagMaximumLikelihoodOperation uses the same fitMode and eigenvalues ports. Auto selects the supplied spectrum only for unit effects; reference rejects a spectrum and tiled requires one. Supply exactly one eigenvalue per entity: a length mismatch throws, while a nonfinite value or an absolute value above float32(1.00001) returns status 1 and NaN outputs. The CPU objective and determinant derivatives use the supplied spectrum; the caller must ensure it belongs to the entity weights matrix. The pure computeSpatialPanelLagMaximumLikelihoodOnCPU accepts the spectrum as input.eigenvalues and the mode as options.fitMode; with no spectrum it evaluates the dense CSR determinant. GPU-specific size caps do not limit the CPU fit. Explicit maximumSearchIterations and maximumIterations budgets bind the CPU fit: exhausting a search budget with a rho bracket wider than tolerance, or an outer budget before convergence, returns status 3 with the current coefficients, fitted values and residuals; covariance is withheld. One golden-section step on [-0.999, 0.999] leaves a bracket about 1.23483 wide. When budgets are omitted, the CPU search continues to binary64 resolution and alternation continues to convergence or a repeated state. Residual-square sums use compensated float64 accumulation so repeating periods preserves the estimate and scales covariance by the inverse repetition count.

The contributor and both operation forms capture declared fit options, spectrum presence and graph port/status maps by property read at construction, including inherited or non-enumerable getters. Each getter is read once; changing its return value later cannot remove a loss flag. Mutating the original props cannot change the declared spectrum dependency; the spectrum's values are read anew on each execution. A graph spectrum-length error starts with the configured operation ID; the standalone CPU function uses its own function name.

The fit runs on centered data: the pooled mean of each predictor and of the response is removed before quasi-demeaning, so a predictor with a large mean (a year, an elevation, an income) costs no precision. Only the intercept depends on the shift; it is mapped back as intercept + (1 - rho) mean(y) - slopes' mean(x) and its covariance row and column are transformed to match. Normal and information matrices are factored after diagonal equilibration, and a Cholesky pivot of the unit-diagonal matrix below 1e-3 (the limit of GPUOrdinaryLeastSquares) is status 2.

The concentrated variance is formed directly. With e0, e1 the residuals of the quasi-demeaned response and of its lag on the predictors, rho0 = e0'e1 / e1'e1 and r = e0 - rho0 e1 summed per row, u'u = S + e1'e1 (rho - rho*)^2, two non-negative terms; the search and the published sigma2, log-likelihood and covariance all use it. The former e0'e0 - 2 rho e0'e1 + rho^2 e1'e1 of three float32 sums is rounding once the model fits well. Measured on the test GPU against spreg 1.9.1 Panel_FE_Lag and Panel_RE_Lag (16 entities, 16 periods, rho 0.4, residual deviation 0.004): with u'u at 3e-5 of e0'e0 the published sigma2 was 1% to 2% off and is now within 1e-5; at 3e-7, 36% low and 96% high, now within 6.3e-5; at 3e-9 (eight scenes, both effects) it was 10 to 241 times too large with status 0 in 9 of the 16 fits, negative with status 2 in one and status 3 in six, and is now within 1.7e-3 with status 0 in all 16, where the float32 rounding of the fitted values (0.6% of a residual per row) is what remains. The lag of the response is summed from differences rounded as written, with the row sums of the float32 weights carried exactly.

During the rho search, the tiled GPU route removes the rho-independent log-variance constant and evaluate small relative variance increments with a series. Otherwise float32 can round nearby variances to the same value, so a narrow search bracket can still surround a biased estimate. The published log-likelihood retains the full log variance and its existing constants.

Entity effects survive the pooled centering. Each column is stored as its within deviation (centered value less its entity mean) and its entity mean, and the quasi-demeaned value value - (1 - phi) mean is formed as deviation + phi mean, whose terms do not cancel; the normal equations of each step are the polynomial in phi of three moment sums taken once. Formed as written, (value - mean) - fitted was regrouped by the shader compiler of the test GPU as value - (mean + fitted): with entity effects of +3000 and -3000 (16 entities, 16 periods, unit effects, residual deviation 0.004) all 256 published residuals were multiples of the float32 step of 3000 and rho was 0.026 standard errors from spreg Panel_FE_Lag; it is now within 0.002. What float32 leaves there: the entity means are sequential float32 sums, off by up to 6e-4, which leaves a constant per entity in the residuals and sigma2 0.6% high (1.1% before), and the lag of the centered response is rounded to a float32 step of the effect, which leaves the within-entity part of a residual within 7e-5.

effects defaults to random. maximumIterations (1–128, default 64) bounds outer sweeps; maximumSearchIterations (1–64, default 32) bounds each golden-section search. tolerance (default 1e-5) controls phi change and final rho bracket width; the change of phi is held against it as an absolute change for a phi of 0.01 or more and in proportion to phi / 0.01 below. Rho is restricted to [-.999,.999], and random phi to [1e-8,1]. Boundary summary bit 1 flags rho near either endpoint; bit 2 flags random phi at either endpoint; bit 4 (with bit 2 and status 3) flags a phi whose estimate is below 1e-8 and was held there: the published numbers are then those of the constrained problem, not the maximum, and there is no covariance. Unit phi is exactly zero and sets neither bit. The lower limit was 1e-5 and was reported as status 0 with bit 2 only: entity effects of +3000 and -3000 with residuals of 0.004 (spreg Panel_RE_Lag phi 2.5e-7) came out with sigma2 105 times spreg's and rho 3.5 standard errors off; that fit is now status 0 with phi within 0.2% of spreg, rho within 0.009 standard errors, the intercept within 0.2 and sigma2 within 1.1% (the float32 entity means). float32 data cannot carry residuals much below 1e-6 of an entity effect, so with 16 periods phi stays above about 1e-8 on data the kernel can resolve (effects of 16,000,000 with residuals of 0.5: spreg phi 9.1e-9, status 3).

fitted is the quasi-demeaned response at the published phi less the residual (fitted + residuals = y - (1 - phi) mean_i(y)). A phi inside the tolerance of the alternation but d away from spreg's moves fitted by d times the entity mean of the response and leaves residuals alone: with a response mean of 10,000 and residuals of 0.004, a phi within 4.5e-7 of spreg's gives fitted values 1.2 residual deviations from spreg's.

With p = predictorCount + 1, output shapes are: coefficients[p+2] (intercept, slopes, rho, phi), covariance[(p+1)²], summary[6], status[1], and observation-sized fitted/residuals. Unit intercept and its covariance row/column are NaN. Covariance matches spreg's conditional varb expected-information block for beta/rho, excluding phi and innovation variance. Summary is [sigma2, reportedLogLikelihood, sweeps, phiChange, rhoBracketWidth, boundaryBits]. The log likelihood deliberately follows spreg 1.8.3 reporting: unit effects retain its additional -NT/2 * log(NT) normalization; comparing random and unit reported values as a likelihood-ratio test is inappropriate. Fitted values and innovations use the quasi-demeaned response convention.

Fit status is 0 converged, 1 invalid/incomplete inputs, 2 singular or nonpositive/nonfinite innovation variance, and 3 iteration/search exhaustion. Exhaustion exposes provisional estimates and diagnostics but NaN covariance. Other failures never publish completed inference.

Optional hausman: {tests, status} additionally fits unit effects and compares slopes/rho using spreg's RE.varb - FE.varb convention. tests[3] is [statistic, df, p]. The compatibility df is predictorCount + 2 (spreg's RE.k, including intercept and rho), not the contrast rank predictorCount + 1. Status is 0 valid, 1 unsuccessful constituent fit, or 2 non-positive-definite covariance difference/nonfinite comparison; invalid comparisons return NaNs. This explicitly rejects negative Hausman statistics instead of interpreting them as chi-square inference.

The pinned statistics-advanced.py generator executes spreg 1.8.3 Panel_RE_Lag, Panel_FE_Lag and panel_Hausman, and mgwr 2.2.1 full response-smoother inference. Native tests compare panel estimates, conditional covariance, innovations, reported likelihood and valid/invalid Hausman cases, and exercise missing data, singular within-design, zero variance and both iteration budgets.
