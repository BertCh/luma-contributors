Accessibility & change of support

GPUCatchmentAccessibility

Scores each demand location by the supply-to-demand ratios of the facilities whose catchments reach it, with two-step floating catchment areas.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.catchment-accessibility rung=result caption="14 libraries and 199 tract centroids: 2 km straight-line catchments, not travel time or network distance."}

Floating catchment accessibility on any GPUSpatialWeights (the access package two_step_fca). method: '2sfca' or '3sfca', with supply, demand, facilityWeights (row j lists the demand points i with weight f(cost)), optional demandWeights (row i lists facilities; it defaults to facilityWeights, which is only valid for symmetric self-join weights and is not checked) and the caller-owned output: {accessibility, ratios?, reachableFacilities?, validity?} (GPUCatchmentAccessibilityOutput). A demand row whose demand is NaN or infinite is missing: it adds nothing to any facility's ratio, and the optional validity output (uint32 per demand row) is 0 for it and 1 otherwise, as in GPUHuffTradeAreas. Its own accessibility does not read its demand and stays valid.

Pass a weights producer's outputWeights port to retain its status and metadata. Supplied nonzero overflow or candidateOverflow on either weights table suppresses all accessibility scores to NaN. Facility-weights loss suppresses the ratios too; demand-weights loss suppresses ratios only for 3SFCA, whose selection totals depend on those rows. Demand-weights loss sets every reachableFacilities entry to 0xffffffff (unknown), while facility-weights loss alone leaves the complete forward count usable. validity continues to report finite demand only. After the producer rebuilds complete weights and clears the flags, the next submission restores the ordinary scores/counts without recompilation. Both trade-area demos forward described search ports and display unavailable summaries instead of totals computed from suppressed outputs.

  • 2SFCA: R_j = S_j / sum_i P_i w_ji, A_i = sum_j w_ij R_j.
  • 3SFCA (Wan, Zou and Sternberg 2012; PySAL access three_stage_fca) applies the selection weight G_ij = w_ij / T_i, T_i = sum_k w_ik over demand row i, together with the decay w. The ratio step reads the facility weights, R_j = S_j / sum_i P_i w_ji^2 / T_i, and the score step the demand weights, A_i = sum_j (w_ij^2 / T_i) R_j. Both are G_ij W_ij only when the demand weights are the transpose of the facility weights. Demand rows with T_i = 0 are skipped. Matches access 1.1.10 on network costs for binary, exponential and Gaussian decay.
  • Supply conservation holds for both methods, with any decay function (binary, Gaussian, a network-cost kernel), when the demand-side weights are the transpose of the facility-side weights: w_ij = w_ji for every pair, with the same set of pairs. Then sum_i P_i A_i = sum_j S_j over the facilities that capture demand (sum_i P_i w_ji > 0, or sum_i P_i G_ij > 0 for 3SFCA). For 2SFCA this is sum_i P_i A_i = sum_j R_j sum_i P_i w_ij, and the inner sum is the denominator of R_j, so each facility contributes exactly S_j. It breaks when the two steps use different weights (different decays or radii, or a demandWeights that is not the transpose), and by the supply of facilities with no demand in range, which is not allocated to anyone. Sums are f32, so the identity holds to rounding. The default demandWeights = facilityWeights satisfies the condition for symmetric self-join weights. 3SFCA differs in what A_i means, a selection-weighted average of the R_j, not in conservation.

A non-finite demand, or a non-finite ratio from a non-finite supply, is a missing value and adds nothing. The operator reads weights, not positions, so it has no spatialContext; distance units and the coordinate space (planar, or great-circle meters) are those of the weights' producer. When both facilityWeights and demandWeights carry spatialContext, the contexts must be compatible; otherwise construction throws.

This is the one 2SFCA/3SFCA kernel. Over a network cost matrix, turn the matrix into weights with GPUNetworkCostWeights, or use the catchment option of GPUNetworkAccessibility, which composes the two.

GPUHuffTradeAreas

Estimates the probability that each demand location patronises each facility from facility attractiveness and distance-decay weights, and each facility's expected demand.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.huff-trade-areas rung=result caption="Tracts shaded by modal-park probability: 102 parks weighted by area, inverse-square distance within 2.5 km."}

Huff probabilities p_ij = A_j^alpha w_ij / sum_k A_k^alpha w_ik from attractiveness and demandWeights, with alpha from a one-element parameters view. The trade area of demand i is its modal facility (lowest ID on ties, GPU_HUFF_NO_TRADE_AREA when none). Props: attractiveness (float32, one row per facility), demandWeights (row i lists the facilities of demand i), optional parameters (float32, GPU_HUFF_TRADE_AREAS_PARAMETER_LENGTH = 1 word written by getGPUHuffTradeAreasParameterValues({exponent}); without the view alpha = 1), and the caller-owned output (GPUHuffTradeAreasOutput) with the required tradeArea (uint32 per demand row). Optional outputs are probabilities (one per demandWeights slot, aligned with demandWeights.neighbors), tradeAreaProbability (per demand row) and expectedDemand, expectedDemand_j = sum_i P_i p_ij, which needs facilityWeights (the transpose of demandWeights) and demand. A demand row whose demand is NaN or infinite is missing: as in GPUCatchmentAccessibility it adds nothing to expectedDemand, and the optional validity output (uint32 per demand row, needs demand) is 0 for it and 1 otherwise. Like GPUCatchmentAccessibility it reads weights only and has no spatialContext. When both demandWeights and facilityWeights carry spatialContext, the contexts must be compatible; otherwise construction throws.

Each submission writes zero to probability slots past the terminal CSR offset, including when a reused weights table shrinks or becomes empty. Zero attractiveness or zero weight contributes no probability. Normalization uses row-centred log scores before exponentiation; changing the common scale of positive attractiveness leaves probabilities, the winning facility and expected demand unchanged within float32 rounding, even when the unnormalized powers overflow or underflow. Relative scores use the original binary mantissas, retaining differences between adjacent float32 attractiveness values; exact weighted ties at the default exponent keep the lowest facility ID. Expected-demand contributions combine the demand's binary exponent before rounding: a share that rounds to zero may still make a representable contribution when demand is large. Signed finite demand keeps its sign; non-finite demand remains missing.

Supplied nonzero demandWeights.status.overflow or candidateOverflow suppresses every trade area to GPU_HUFF_NO_TRADE_AREA and every probability (including padding and winning probabilities) to NaN. Either forward or reverse weights loss suppresses every expectedDemand value to NaN; reverse weights loss leaves forward probabilities usable. The optional demand validity still reports finite demand only. Clear the input flags after rebuilding complete weights to restore ordinary results on the next submission of the same compiled graph.

GPUArealInterpolation

targetZoneCount times categories.categoryCount is at most 4,294,967,294, reserving the final scan sentinel; device bindings impose lower limits.

Transfers source-zone values to target zones by rasterized overlap area, with extensive or intensive area-share weights.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.areal-interpolation rung=result caption="199 tracts (719,651 residents) shared to 13 community areas by overlap area on a 100 m raster."}

Maximum: at most getGPUArealInterpolationMaximumCellCount(device) (2^25 - 1 on a default device) grid cells; with cellWeights, unweightedFastPath: false or a longitude/latitude raster at most GPU_AREAL_INTERPOLATION_MAXIMUM_WEIGHTED_PAIR_COUNT (2^24 - 1) pair slots.

Area-weighted transfer between two zone systems (tobler area_interpolate, CARTO ENRICH_POLYGONS) on a common fine raster. Rasterize both systems to the same grid with GPUPolygonRasterization (or any zone raster) and pass the two zone views, so H3 or hexagon rasters work too.

  • Props: grid (GPURasterMetadata; its width * height is the cell count), sourceZones, targetZones (uint32, one row per grid cell; a value at or above the count means no zone), sourceZoneCount, targetZoneCount, optional cellWeights (a dasymetric raster; area becomes a sum of weights, and non-finite or non-positive cells carry no area), denominator (per kind, see below), mode ('extensive' by default or 'intensive', which selects output.weights.weights), categories ({sourceCategories, categoryCount}) and the caller-owned output (GPUArealInterpolationOutput): weights (a CSR with targetZoneCount + 1 offsets; the neighbor capacity is the maximum number of pairs), status.overflow, and the optional status.requiredPairCount, alternateWeights (the other normalization, from the same run), areas (raw overlap area per slot), the diagnostics below and categoryShares (T x K, required with categories).
  • The cell weight m (1, or cellWeights) summed per source s and target t gives a_st. Extensive: w_ts = a_st / A_s. Intensive: w_ts = a_st / B_t. A_s and B_t are the zone or overlap areas. Rows are targets and neighbors are sources, so the CSR invariants hold; it is a cross weights matrix.
  • Denominators (default change). denominator is one value for both kinds or {extensive?, intensive?}, and the default is {extensive: 'zone', intensive: 'overlap'}. 'zone' divides by the whole zone (for extensive variables, tobler area_interpolate with allocate_total=False): the part of a source that falls outside the target system is not moved onto the targets, and is now reported (below). 'overlap' divides by the part overlapping the other system (tobler's own default, allocate_total=True; tobler's intensive denominator is always the overlap). The extensive default therefore differs from tobler's: a source half outside the target system with value 100 transfers 50 here and 100 in tobler, unless 'overlap' is passed. The intensive default is 'overlap' because a target only partly covered by sources should be the mean of what covers it, not diluted toward zero; earlier versions used 'zone' for both. Pass denominator: 'zone' to restore the old intensive result.
  • Mass-conservation diagnostics. output.sourceAreas (sourceZoneCount rows) is the raster area of each source (0 when it rasterized to no cell), sourceTransferredFractions is the overlap area over the extensive denominator (1 - f of the extensive mass is lost; with 'zone', the share of the source that lies in the target system; NaN without raster area), and status.invalidCount (one uint32) counts sources with no raster area, whose mass is lost. They are exact for the rasterized zones, not for the polygons.
  • Sources smaller than a cell. sourceAnchorCells (sourceZoneCount cell indices; out of range means none) gives a zone without cells a fallback cell, for example the cell containing its label point. The claim is made in a transient copy of sourceZones, in ascending source order, and succeeds only if the cell belongs to no source or to one that keeps another cell. Remaining empty sources stay in status.invalidCount.
  • Coordinates. Planar by default (areas are cell counts or cellWeights sums, which is an equal-share only on an equal-area raster). With spatialContext: {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} and parameters (the per-frame getGPUPolygonRasterizationParameterValues(grid) view, the same view as GPUPolygonRasterization.parameters), each cell weighs its spherical area R^2 dLon (sin(top) - sin(bottom)), times cellWeights. R comes from getGPUAuthalicSphereRadius: the WGS84 authalic radius or context.sphereRadius, in the context's length unit. Pair areas and sourceAreas use that unit squared: square meters by default, square kilometers with units: 'kilometers' (previously still square meters). Breaking: longitude/latitude now requires metric: 'great-circle'; 'none' throws. Declares measure: 'area': an azimuthal-equidistant local frame throws; project into a Lambert equal-area frame. North-up grids (a negative packed cell height) are handled: the area of a stored row follows the mirrored row. Other spaces throw. Checked against a numeric integral of R^2 cos(lat) (spherical_cell_area.py).
  • Categorical share (t, k) is the overlap area with sources of class k divided by the area of t covered by any source, so it sums to 1 where covered.
  • On overflow the offsets clamp to capacity, status.overflow is 1 and status.requiredPairCount gives the size needed.
  • unweightedFastPath (default on, used when there are no cellWeights) takes pair areas from run lengths and zone areas from counts instead of per-cell accumulation. Results are bitwise identical; it was 1.4x faster on the explorer's graph, about 35 percent on 384 by 384 zone denominators and about equal for the overlap denominator, where the two radix sorts dominate.
  • Cell centers decide membership, so every area is a cell count and accuracy is about one cell along every boundary; slivers smaller than a cell can be missed. It is never an exact polygon intersection. f32 sums are exact up to 2^24 cells per zone. Results are deterministic.

Transfer the values with GPUSpatialLag over the area shares (its source count is the values row count, sourceZoneCount; columnCount transfers several variables at once). The tobler area_interpolate extensive and intensive results match to 1e-5 on grid-aligned zones when allocate_total is set to match the denominator (False for 'zone', True for 'overlap').

graph.add(new GPUArealInterpolation({
  grid, sourceZones, targetZones, sourceZoneCount, targetZoneCount,
  output: {weights: {offsets, neighbors, weights}, alternateWeights, status: {overflow}}
}));
graph.add(new GPUSpatialLag({
  weights: {offsets, neighbors, weights}, values: population, output: populationOnTargets
}));

GPUPycnophylactic

Smooths zone totals into a non-negative raster surface whose cells sum back to each zone total.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.pycnophylactic rung=result caption="199 tract totals (719,651 residents) smoothed onto a 100 m raster; tract outlines for reference."}

Tobler pycnophylactic interpolation on a raster. Props: grid (GPURasterMetadata), zones (uint32; a value at or above zoneCount is outside), zoneCount, totals (per zone), maximumIterations (0 to 512, compile-time), kernel ('rook', the default 4-neighbor mean, or 'box', a 3x3 mean including the cell) and the caller-owned output (GPUPycnophylacticOutput) whose mass is the mass per cell. Each cell starts at total / area(zone). Each iteration takes the focal mean over in-area neighbors, restores the zone total additively, clamps negatives to 0 and rescales multiplicatively to the total; zero-sum zones fill uniformly. Totals hold to f32 accuracy and values are non-negative after every iteration. Zone sums are fixed-order segmented sums over cells sorted once by zone. There is no ancillary initial surface and no barrier. 'rook' on a checkerboard has a -1 eigenmode (Tobler's original); 'box' is smoother. Reference: Tobler (1979), JASA 74(367).

  • Deviation from Tobler (1979). The restoring step is additive, then clamped, then multiplicative. Tobler alternates smoothing with a volume-restoring adjustment; the multiplicative step is kept because it is the only variant here that keeps exact totals and non-negativity together, but where the additive step clamped it can leave flat plateaus, and an additive-only variant would go negative next to much larger zones.
  • Convergence. maximumIterations is the cap. A one-row tolerance (per frame) compares, after each iteration, the largest |new - old| / (total / area(zone)) over all cells with it. Once at or below the tolerance the surface is final: the per-cell kernels of every remaining compiled iteration are skipped through a GPU-written indirect dispatch, and the surface is not copied forward (one settle pass copies the scratch raster only when the last iteration that ran wrote it). Deterministic. output.status.converged and output.status.iterationCount (one uint32 each; they need tolerance) report whether it converged and how many iterations ran; with 0 iterations both are 0. A NaN or negative tolerance never converges.
  • Zones without cells. A zone smaller than a cell has no raster cell and its total used to vanish silently. zoneAnchorCells (zoneCount cell indices) lets such a zone claim a fallback cell (see GPUArealInterpolation.sourceAnchorCells; ascending zone order, the cell's owner must keep one cell), and output.status.invalidCount counts zones that still have no cell or no area.
  • Coordinates. With a longitude-latitude spatialContext plus parameters (as above; north-up grids are handled) the smoothed quantity is density per context length unit squared (per square meter by default, per square kilometer with units: 'kilometers', previously still per square meter). Cell areas use getGPUAuthalicSphereRadius in the context's length unit, and totals are enforced on density times the spherical cell area, so output.mass is mass per cell (density times area) and the optional output.density view is the surface itself. Without it every cell counts the same, which is only right on an equal-area raster. Breaking: longitude/latitude now requires metric: 'great-circle'; 'none' throws. Declares measure: 'area': an azimuthal-equidistant local frame throws; project into a Lambert equal-area frame. The focal mean is still by cell index, so away from the equator it is anisotropic on the ground.
  • Limits. Counts are f32 sums, exact to 2^24 cells per zone; a larger zone loses count precision (the mass still holds to f32 accuracy). Oracles: a float64 numpy reimplementation of the documented algorithm including tolerance and anchors (pycnophylactic.py).

GPUSegregation

Computes aspatial and spatial segregation indices from a unit-by-group count table, with per-unit contributions.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.segregation rung=result caption="Five population groups across 199 tracts; tracts shaded by their contribution to the entropy index H."}

Segregation indices from a unit-by-group count table, aspatial or spatial (PySAL segregation).

  • Props: groupCount K (2 to 16), groupCounts (units * K float32, row-major; the unit count is groupCounts.length / K), scales (default [null]; null is aspatial, otherwise one square GPUSpatialWeights per bandwidth, which gives the multiscale profile), selfWeight (1), atkinsonInequalityAversion (b, 0.5, in (0, 1)), spatialForm and output: indices (scales * stride floats, layout from getGPUSegregationLayout(K): entropy, multi-group dissimilarity, diversity, dissimilarity [K], isolation [K], Atkinson [K], interaction [K * K]) and optional per-unit views (localEnvironment, localEntropy, localDissimilarity, localTheil, per scale; they sum to the global indices).
  • spatialForm: 'environment' (default) is the Reardon and O'Sullivan (2004) form: pi_im = x~_im / t~_i with x~_im = selfWeight x_im + sum_j w_ij x_jm (j over the active slots: positive weight, another unit; a self slot in the weights is not read, so a unit is never counted twice) replaces p_im, and the sums keep the unit populations t_i. 'smoothed-population' replaces the whole table by the smoothed table x~ and runs the aspatial formulas on it, which equals PySAL's spatially implicit indices (PySAL also rounds the smoothed counts).
  • With P_m = X_m / T, E = sum P ln(1 / P), E_i = sum pi ln(1 / pi) and I = sum P (1 - P): Theil H = sum_i t_i (E - E_i) / (E T); multi-group D = sum_i sum_m t_i |pi_im - P_m| / (2 T I); D_g = sum_i t_i |pi_ig - P_g| / (2 T P_g (1 - P_g)); isolation xPx_g = sum_i (x_ig / X_g) pi_ig; interaction xPy_gh = sum_i (x_ig / X_g) pi_ih; Atkinson A_g = 1 - P / (1 - P) |sum_i (1 - pi_ig)^(1 - b) pi_ig^b t_i / (P T)|^(1 / (1 - b)). Undefined indices are NaN, never 0 (0 means perfectly even): H and the diversity with an empty total, H also with a single present group (E = 0, while E itself is the defined 0), D with an empty total or I = 0, D_g and A_g for a group with zero or all of the population, and isolation and interaction of a group with no population. A unit with an empty environment (no population in it or its neighbors) has NaN local values and contributes nothing to the sums, so use a NaN-skipping sum to add the local outputs up to the global indices; unit contributions to D_g and H are also NaN when that global index is undefined. Behavior change: these were 0.
  • Small-sample bias: the indices are not bias corrected. D and H are biased upward when units hold few people, because sampling noise alone makes unit compositions differ from the global one even under random allocation (Cortese, Falk and Cohen 1976; Winship 1977; Carrington and Troske 1997). Compare units of similar size, or against a random-allocation null, before reading a small D or H as segregation. Isolation and interaction are less affected but depend on the group share.
  • Float32 counts and totals are integer exact only up to 2^24 = 16,777,216 total population; above that T and X_m round (about 6e-8 relative per addition).
  • Sums are f32, within 1e-5 to 2e-4 relative of float64 and of PySAL on the test fixtures. Memory is O(units * K^2) per scale. The boundary-based SpatialDissim and permutation inference are not provided: PySAL's evenness null redraws every person's unit from the unit sizes (a binomial or multinomial draw per unit) and reruns the whole index pipeline per simulation, whereas the permutation operators here shuffle values across rows of an existing weights structure, so it needs its own count-table resampling kernel. atkinsonInequalityAversion and selfWeight are compile-time.
