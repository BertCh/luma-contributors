Geometry utilities

All of these are contributors: construct, then graph.add. Per-frame values go through a GPUParameterBuffer packed with the matching getGPU...ParameterValues helper, so nothing recompiles.

GPUOutlineGeometry

Compact output layouts are opt-in; offsets and counts describe only emitted geometry. Position addressing is at most 2,147,483,647 and candidate/fragment word counts must fit uint32.

Builds round-joined triangle outlines around points, lines or polygon rings at a fixed distance, for drawing only.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.outline-geometry rung=result caption="Grey: 51 Chicago parks · blue: 100 m outline triangles, overlaps not unioned"}

Buffer-like outline geometry for drawing (turf buffer and lineOffset, PostGIS ST_Buffer as a picture). Props: positions, geometryType ('points', 'lines' or 'polygons'; lines take lineOffsets, polygons the four-level featureOffsets, polygonOffsets and ringOffsets), spatialContext (omitted is planar; {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} gives meters on a sphere of sphereRadius, default the mean Earth radius; this replaces the removed coordinateSystem and radius props, and other contexts throw), joinSegments (default 16), parameters (getGPUOutlineGeometryParameterValues({distance})) and output.positions. The default fixed-stride layout writes a non-indexed triangle list with exactly positions.length * getGPUOutlineGeometryVerticesPerInput(joinSegments) vertices: per input vertex a round-join disc fan plus a quad to the next vertex, with unused triangles degenerate. Fixed-stride output has no capacity or overflow. With output.layout: 'compact', vertexOffsets and status delimit the emitted triangle vertices; sourceRows is optional. Compact capacity overflow retains a bounded prefix and reports required counts. Overlaps are not unioned, so it is for drawing only; queries use dwithin and GPUBufferSelection. Spherical mode converts meters with each vertex's local east and north scale (equirectangular, not geodesic; wrong near the poles and across the antimeridian). It measures distance, so local-lambert-equal-area frames throw. There are no negative buffers. A one-sided offset line is GPUOffsetCurve.

Pass optional spatialContext to getGPUOutlineGeometryParameterValues(settings) to warn on short distance values; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.

GPUOffsetCurve — Offsets lines or polygon rings to one side by a signed distance, with round, mitre or bevel joins.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.offset-curve rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: the same streets offset 20 m to the left"}

GPUVertexSnap — Moves each vertex onto the nearest reference vertex closer than a tolerance, keeping the layout unchanged.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.vertex-snap rung=result caption="Grey: 51 parks · blue: park vertices snapped to community-area vertices within 50 m"}

GPUOffsetCurve is a one-sided offset of lines (lineOffsets) and polygon rings (geometryType: 'polygons' with the four-level layout; the ring count is preserved, so the input featureOffsets and polygonOffsets describe the output rings) (shapely.offset_curve, geo OffsetCurve); a positive distance is to the left of the path direction. Per vertex it writes a round arc (quadSegments per quarter circle, at most GPU_OFFSET_CURVE_MAXIMUM_QUAD_SEGMENTS = 256), a mitre (clipped beyond mitreLimit, default 5) or a bevel on the outside of a turn, the single intersection point on the inside, and a plain normal offset at path ends. The output has getGPUOffsetCurveRowsPerVertex rows per vertex (2q + 1 round, 2 otherwise); the optional output.counts gives the distinct points per vertex and padding repeats the last point. Distance and mitre limit are per-frame. Which side of a vertex is the inside of its turn is an exact orientation sign, so the join is on the right side where two edges nearly reverse (the tip of a sliver thinner than one float32 step). It is render-grade: GEOS loop removal is not done, so tight inside corners can self-cross. GEOS clamps quad_segs to at least 8, so round-join parity is pinned at 8 and 16; GEOS leaves the corner at a ring's first vertex open on the outside and this closes it. Points were within 2e-4 of Shapely 2.1.2. Planar only (spatialContext defaults to planar; a longitude/latitude context throws, so project first with createGPULocalFrame and GPULocalFrameProjection), rings are implicit-closed. Both GPUOffsetCurve and GPUVertexSnap measure distance and reject local-lambert-equal-area frames.

GPUVertexSnap is the vertex-to-vertex subset of shapely.snap: each vertex with a reference vertex strictly closer than the per-frame tolerance moves to the nearest one (lowest row on ties) and the layout is unchanged. Optional pairwise mode (featureOffsets and referenceOffsets) and output.referenceRows. It is O(V * R). Segment insertion and snapping onto segments, which GEOS also does, are out of scope. It matched Shapely on 60 random lines.

GPUPolygonOverlay — Computes intersection, union, difference, symmetric difference or dissolve of planar polygon sets as compact polygons.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.polygon-overlay rung=result caption="Grey: 51 parks, light grey: 17 areas · blue: parks clipped to Uptown, area 3"}

GPUBufferSurface — Buffers lines or polygons by a signed distance into dissolved polygons, with round, mitre or bevel joins.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.buffer-surface rung=result caption="Grey: 51 Chicago parks · blue: dissolved 50 m buffer polygons"}

Maximum (GPUPolygonOverlay): both operands together have at most getGPUSegmentIntersectionMaximumVertexCount(device) (4194304 on a default device: the 32-byte segment-table row per vertex must fit one storage binding) positions rows plus one per ring. Those rows plus capacity.intersections must also fit floor(device.limits.maxStorageBufferBindingSize / 128): snap rounding keeps four diagonal vertices per hot pixel in the segment table. This is a device-derived limit of 1,048,576 on a 128 MiB device or 8,388,608 on a 1 GiB device, not a fixed row cap. The 23-bit lattice keys encode coordinates, independently of the row count.

GPUBufferSurface maximum: the boundary rows (raw offset-curve rows plus one closing vertex per raw ring, (1 + 2 * rowsPerVertex) * vertices + 3 * rings for polygons) plus capacity.intersections are at most getGPUBufferSurfaceMaximumBoundaryRowCount(device) (1,048,576 on a default device, a quarter of the overlay's segment table); 17 rows per vertex for the default round joins.

Both classes are planar only: spatialContext defaults to native planar coordinates and a longitude-latitude context throws. GPUBufferSurface measures distance and rejects local-lambert-equal-area frames; its distance is in position units, so project first (createGPULocalFrame, GPULocalFrameProjection) and buffer in the frame's meters; buffering degrees gives an ellipse on the ground.

GPUPolygonOverlay produces polygon geometry for intersection, union, difference, symmetric-difference, and unary dissolve. It combines both operand boundaries and nodes them by snap rounding on a lattice (vertexTolerance is the grid: the step is the smallest power of two that is at least vertexTolerance, at least 2^-22 of the input extent and at least 2^-24 of the largest coordinate magnitude (one float32 step there, so every lattice point is a float32 value), anchored at a multiple of the step, so a power-of-two vertexTolerance reproduces shapely grid_size and GEOS fixed precision whenever float32 can store that grid at the coordinates of the data; at a projected northing of 4,000,000 the step is 0.25 to 0.5 whatever the tolerance, so use coordinates relative to a local origin for a finer result). Vertices and crossings are rounded to the lattice, every edge is rerouted through the hot pixels it passes, and the resulting pieces meet only at shared lattice vertices; coincident pieces are bit-identical. The faces are then labelled without testing pieces against rings: one ray toward +x per group of coincident pieces finds the nearest group to its right (exact predicates on the lattice, a BVH walk), and pointer jumping along those parents sums the winding number of each polygon beside each group and then the number of covering polygons of each operand. A group is kept where the Boolean result changes across it, oriented with the result interior on the left, and the kept edges are assembled into shells and holes by exact vertex matching. The result is compact polygon geometry rather than a face mask, with every vertex on the lattice. For valid pointwise lattice-rounded operands, the result equals GEOS OverlayNG with grid_size applied to those operands (shapely on set_precision(..., mode='pointwise') inputs). Invalid rounded rings retain the specified winding-rule meaning on their snap-rounded linework; GEOS polygon overlay on invalid operands is not the oracle. In the three crowded-pixel intersection fixtures (grid sizes 0.25, 1 and 1/64), rounding makes both star rings self-touch with retraced spikes. The even-odd result has one ring and lattice area 2.5, while GEOS 3.14.1 returns four polygons with area 53.5. For example, lattice point (31.25, 29.5) has operand windings (0, 1) yet lies in that GEOS intersection. GEOS raises for union, difference and symmetric difference on these same inputs. The fixtures retain the GEOS output, validity reasons and counterexample alongside an independent exact rational snap-rounding and winding oracle; this difference is specific to invalid operands. output.boundary retains the operand and source feature of one row per kept group (the first piece of the group in lattice order, so a boundary shared by two features reports one of them).

Operand membership follows fillRule, applied to the winding number w of all rings of one polygon (counter-clockwise +1, clockwise -1). The default 'even-odd' keeps w odd, which is the shell-and-holes reading for valid polygons and also defines self-touching or self-crossing rings, holes outside their shell and overlapping holes. 'positive', 'negative' and 'nonzero' keep w > 0, w < 0 and w != 0, so regions a raw boundary covers twice stay inside; a point is in the operand when it is inside any polygon. Every decision is exact: coordinates are lattice integers, crossings are rounded by exact integer division and ordered along the ray with 96-bit integer arithmetic. Noding reports uncertain segment pairs, non-finite vertices and candidate loss from either intersection BVH through candidateOverflow, and final output capacity loss through overflow. An uncertainty counter is diagnostic; omitting it does not clear incompleteness. The optional output.status.requiredRingCount is the unclamped ring (shell plus hole) requirement of the assembly, for sizing the output ring capacity, and the optional ringCount is the number of rings written. There is no output.status.requiredCount: contributor contract rule 8 reserves it for features, and the unclamped feature count is not known once rings were dropped. An output polygon is a piece of operand unions and can cover several input features, so it has no single source row and output.geometry has no sourceIds (a view passed there throws); provenance is per boundary edge (output.boundary.operandIds, sourceFeatureIds). Breaking (2026-11-10): status.requiredCount (it held the ring requirement) and the deprecated geometry.sourceIds (it held the output row index) are removed. A GPULabelPoint joined to each operand with GPUPointInPolygonJoin identifies features containing that point, not every source under the output polygon. For example, dissolving adjacent parcels [0,1] × [0,1] and [1,2] × [0,1] yields one shell, but the interior point (0.5,0.5) belongs only to the first parcel. Even all matches at that point cannot recover the second parcel. For membership, join each output polygon to each operand with an explicit polygon relation, such as positive-area interior intersection, and record boundary-only contacts separately. Attribute preservation requires a relation with output feature row, operand and source row. Optional featureSources reports a representative retained boundary owner and whether retained boundary ownership is mixed; it does not enumerate that membership relation. GPUBufferSurface has the same output and dissolves all features into one surface. Optional output.status.nodedSegmentRequiredCount reports the unclamped piece count for capacity.nodedSegments (exact when candidateOverflow is zero). Once boundary overflow is zero, size the three output offset arrays to requiredRingCount + 1 and positions to output.boundary.status.requiredCount + requiredRingCount (one closing vertex per ring). Ring capacity is independent of input feature count: the A/B star-grid intersection at 1,000 vertices per operand needs 521 rings, exceeding its former 512-ring allocation. Each additional ring needs 12 bytes across the caller's offset arrays, plus assembly scratch. For the snap noder, expanded input rows V and crossing capacity I reserve V + I hot pixels; their diagonal segment table alone needs 128 * (V + I) bytes. Incidence capacity is max(nodedSegments + V, 2 * (V + I)), with twice that many candidate pairs. Size from reported requirements before timing repeated runs; reject truncated candidate plans. Boundary groups are already unique, so overlay assembly skips the redundant opposing-edge cancellation search.

GPUBufferSurface is the topology-producing counterpart of GPUOutlineGeometry and GPUOffsetCurve. Consecutive duplicate vertices are dropped from lines and rings first, and a polygon ring's closing vertex is dropped when it repeats the first (GeoJSON and GeoArrow rings are closed, the offset curve needs open rings). Lines receive independent left and right offsets plus round, flat, or square caps and emit their clockwise outline. Polygons are a union of simple pieces: the polygon itself (rings normalized to counter-clockwise shells and clockwise holes, then reversed) plus the stroke of every ring, the region between its two closed offset rings at +|d| and -|d|; a positive distance keeps polygon plus strokes, a negative one polygon minus strokes. Inside turns of buffer offsets meet at the offset-line intersection while it lies on both offset segments and are otherwise routed through the input vertex (the GEOS buffer rule), so tight switchbacks keep a consistent winding. A ring whose envelope is narrower than 2|d| (the GEOS eroded-ring test) drops its offset toward its own interior, so the stroke covers that whole interior. Both paths finish with the same noding and dissolve as overlay, using the 'negative' winding fill rule, so regions the raw boundaries cover twice (hairpins closer than twice the distance, self-crossing lines, slots narrower than twice the distance) stay inside, holes a positive buffer collapses disappear, holes a negative buffer merges through a thin wall join, and necks thinner than twice the distance split, as in shapely.buffer. Checked on hairpin, figure-eight, slot, thin-neck, collapsed-hole and thin-wall shapes. A polygon ring of n vertices contributes n (1 + 2 rowsPerVertex) raw boundary rows, so size capacity for about twice the segments of a plain ring offset. Joins are round, mitre, or bevel; round resolution is controlled by quadSegments. Positive polygon distances grow shells and shrink holes, negative distances do the reverse, and non-positive line distances produce an empty surface.

Both contributors are planar float32 operations with explicit intersections and nodedSegments workspace capacities plus caller-owned boundary, ring, and vertex capacities. candidateOverflow means arrangement construction was incomplete, including uncertifiable predicates and non-finite input. Geometry and representative boundary provenance are eligible only when both flags are zero. overflow covers capacity loss in noded segments, compact boundary or polygon capacity. Capacity, optional outputs, join/cap style, and topology are compile-time; distance and mitre limit remain GPU parameters and can change per encoding.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.geometry-orientation rung=result caption="Left: parks as supplied · right: rings reversed to counter-clockwise, flag 1"}

GPUGeometryCleanup — Snaps vertices to a grid and removes repeated vertices, compacting positions and republishing offsets.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.geometry-cleanup rung=result caption="Grey: 51 parks · blue: snapped to a 10 m grid, vertices within 20 m of the last kept removed"}

All three classes are planar only: spatialContext defaults to native planar coordinates and a longitude-latitude context throws; GPUAffineTransform forwards the resolved context (and its frame) to its measures. GPUGeometryOrientation 'orient-polygons' judges ring winding at the lexicographically smallest vertex with exact orientation, so sliver rings are not misjudged by f32 shoelace cancellation (a bow-tie ring is judged by that vertex, like shapely.is_ccw, not by its signed area).

Geometry edits that keep the GeoArrow layout. Polygon inputs take the four-level layout (positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets), lines take lineOffsets (plus optional featureOffsets for multi-lines) and points take positions (plus optional featureOffsets); the output keeps the input offsets, so pass them with the output positions to the next contributor. GPUAffineTransform is shapely.affinity (affine_transform, translate, scale, rotate, skew): either one per-frame matrix packed by getGPUAffineTransformParameterValues({rotate, scale, skew, translate, origin}) (applied as scale, skew, rotate, translate about one origin) or per-feature featureTransforms (6 floats per feature, Shapely order). The origin is a point, or 'center' or 'centroid' computed per feature on the GPU (compile-time origins); it is computed once from the input, where Shapely recomputes 'center' at each step, which differs only for skew about the center. Agreement was within 0.03 at coordinates near 1e5 (f32 spacing 0.0078).

GPUGeometryOrientation is shapely.reverse (mode: 'reverse', polygons or lines) or orient_polygons (mode: 'orient-polygons', which needs the four-level polygon layout and a per-frame exteriorClockwise set by getGPUGeometryOrientationParameterValues). The layout is unchanged and reversedRings flags are optional. The sign comes from an f32 shoelace sum, which differs from GEOS only for self-intersecting rings.

GPUGeometryCleanup is remove_repeated_points(tolerance) and set_precision(grid_size, mode='pointwise'): gridSize snaps half-up as GEOS does, and tolerance removes repeats with the exact GEOS semantics (first and last kept, inclusive tolerance against the previous kept vertex). Line and polygon output is compacted positions with republished lineOffsets (lines) or ringOffsets (polygons) and status: {count, overflow, requiredCount, collapsedCount} (polygon rings with fewer than 3 surviving vertices are emptied, where GEOS raises; the input featureOffsets and polygonOffsets stay valid). GEOS set_precision(pointwise) does not drop repeats, so use removeRepeatedPoints: false for exact parity. With geometryType: 'points', each position row is an independent Point: the direct O(n) kernel applies only precision snapping, preserves coincident rows, and publishes positions and status: {count, overflow, requiredCount?} without offsets outputs. The sequential per-ring walk (latency grows with the largest ring) runs when tolerance > 0; at tolerance = 0 (exact repeats after snapping, the default) a vertex-parallel path does constant work per vertex: a vertex repeats when its snapped position equals the previous vertex's (-0 equals +0; NaN and infinite coordinates never repeat), a per-ring step applies the closing-vertex and polygon-collapse rules, and a per-vertex step clears the affected flags. Both paths are compiled and the GPU chooses per frame from the tolerance word, with output bitwise identical to the sequential walk. Line and polygon cleanup matched Shapely exactly on 5 parameter sets by 24 features; point tests cover dynamic snapping, ties, duplicate preservation and capacity overflow. normalize is not provided.

GPUPolygonTriangulation

Triangulates polygons with holes into vertex-index triangles using Earcut, with a monotone fallback for large polygons.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.polygon-triangulation rung=result caption="Grey: 51 parks · blue: Earcut triangles, corners gathered on the GPU from the indices"}

Earcut on the GPU (earcut, deck.gl PolygonLayer tessellation, geo TriangulateEarcut, Sedona ST_TriangulatePolygon as earcut, not constrained Delaunay). Props: the four-level layout (positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets), output: {indices, valid, triangleCount?, triangleOffsets?} and maximumWork (default 4M steps, roughly rings of a few thousand vertices). Slices, valid and triangleCount are per polygon; the polygons of feature f are [featureOffsets[f], featureOffsets[f + 1]). The first ring is the shell and the others holes, with any winding. GPUPolygonTriangulationOperation and CPUPolygonTriangulationOperation capture their shape, work/routing options, spatial context and graph port names at construction. Later edits to the caller's props or output map cannot change the declared outputs or the lowered/executed operation. Input graph values still supply fresh geometry each run; construct a new operation to change its shape, options or ports. The CPU function and operation enforce a positive uint32 maximumWork per polygon (default 4,000,000 elementary steps). Budget exhaustion publishes valid = 0, triangleCount = 0 and first-vertex failure padding, discarding partial triangles. The budget resets for each polygon. The CPU reference uses un-hashed Earcut; its work count can differ from the GPU's hashed path. Both forms honor largePolygonMethod (default 'monotone'), its vertex threshold and total routed raw-vertex capacity. With 'none', both fail the committed wavy_grid_holes case at 1,000 steps; the monotone retry completes it. Both operation constructors reject zero budgets; dynamic geometry contents still change on every run. The ear search's diagonal-midpoint inside test is an exact parity (the Simulation-of-Simplicity rule of utils/exact-geometry-wgsl.ts), not an f32 ray crossing. The hole bridge is exact as well: the shell edge that the ray from a hole's leftmost vertex meets first, the vertices inside the bridge triangle, the candidate with the smallest angle to the ray (collinear candidates keep the nearer one, as in Earcut) and the order of holes that share their leftmost vertex are decided by orientation tests, so a hole a few float32 steps from a shell edge, two shell edges that the ray crosses closer than float32 resolution, and bridge candidates in almost the same direction are bridged correctly. CPU and GPU layout preparation skip degenerate rings and scan triangle slice capacities. A ring is degenerate when it has fewer than three vertices or every first-vertex fan triangle has exact orientation zero; uncertified orientations are retained, matching GPUStudyWindowPreparation. Empty polygons and polygons with a degenerate shell contribute zero triangles, including any holes, and complete with valid = 1. Other polygons own 3 * (vertices + 2 * holes - 2) index slots after the skipped rings are removed. Both Earcut and monotone triangulation retain that live-ring selection. Skipped rings do not become monotone holes, and output indices still refer to original position rows. Their raw vertices continue to count toward monotoneMaximumVertices when the polygon is routed to monotone. Optional triangleOffsets has polygonCount + 1 triangle offsets, including the terminal; polygon p starts at index 3 * triangleOffsets[p]. Non-degenerate layouts retain their original slices and triangles. getPolygonTriangulationIndexCount(vertexCount, ringCount, polygonCount) returns a safe capacity: 3 * max(0, vertexCount + 2 * ringCount - 4) when polygons exist, otherwise zero. Smaller legacy buffers are still accepted, with insufficient slices flagged invalid. Indices refer to rows of positions. Unused polygon slots hold (first, first, first); spare capacity is zeroed. With no positions, the used triangle count is zero and nothing should be drawn. valid[p] is 0 for untriangulable rings, a hole that cannot be joined to the shell, non-finite coordinates, scratch overflow or exceeded maximumWork (the slice is then all degenerate, unless the monotone path completes it). One invocation per polygon, so Earcut scales with polygon count, not ring size; ear tests use Earcut's z-order hash above 80 vertices (useZOrderHash). Orientation tests use the exact predicate of GPUSegmentIntersection. Empty rings and polygon slots from GPURectangleClip are accepted directly. Triangle area equaled Shapely area and the triangle count equaled the earcut npm package on 14 hand-built cases (order differs), and area matched on 60 random polygons. A seeded search (gpu-polygon-triangulation-search.spec.ts) triangulates random polygons with up to 12 holes, on an integer lattice and at projected-metre magnitude down to float32 resolution, and checks in exact arithmetic that the triangles tile the polygon (non-zero areas, every triangle inside, no two overlapping, areas summing to the polygon area), with the triangle count of the earcut package (3.2.4) where that package tiles the polygon.

A result flagged valid = 1 is a tiling of the polygon whenever the polygon is valid (simple rings, holes inside the shell and outside each other; any winding). Rings may touch at single points, vertex to vertex or a vertex of one ring inside an edge of another. Three departures from Earcut make this hold. Where Earcut cures a local self-intersection of its working ring by writing a triangle across it, the port flags the polygon: that triangle is the only step after which the triangles written and the ring that remains no longer add up to the polygon boundary. A hole that cannot be joined to the shell flags the polygon instead of being dropped, and so does a predicate that cannot be certified. What is left writes only counter-clockwise ears of exact positive area whose boundaries sum to the polygon boundary, so the number of triangles over a point is the polygon's winding number there. An invalid polygon (rings that cross, a hole outside its shell) is flagged, or covered as often as its rings wind: behavior change for such input, which Earcut's cure used to return as valid.

A hole whose leftmost vertex lies inside an edge of another ring is joined there: the vertex is inserted into the edge (exact on-segment test) and the bridge has zero length. Two holes that share their leftmost vertex are ordered by the edge that leaves it, also when that vertex is repeated, and of two edges that the bridge ray meets at one point the one whose side holds the hole is taken. On 126,382 valid polygons with up to twelve holes (90,985 on integer lattices and lattice shears with coordinates up to six million, 35,397 of them again at projected-metre magnitude, half of those at float32 resolution; four in five with touching rings) the Earcut port returned 126,381 tilings and flagged one, a sliver at float32 resolution, where a candidate diagonal is judged at its float32 midpoint, the one decision that is not exact. The earcut package (3.2.4) returned a wrong triangulation for 641 of them. Before these rules the port flagged 535 and returned 32 wrong triangulations as valid. The pinned sample is gpu-polygon-triangulation-touching-rings.spec.ts (Shapely validity and area, test/oracles/polygon-triangulation-touching-rings.py). Delaunay variants are separate because this contributor preserves polygon constraints and holes.

Large polygons (largePolygonMethod, default 'monotone'; 'none' keeps valid = 0 past maximumWork). A polygon with at least monotoneMinimumVertices vertices (default 4096, all rings) skips Earcut, and every polygon on which Earcut fails (maximumWork exceeded or no valid split) is retried, by a parallel y-monotone decomposition (de Berg et al., chapter 3) followed by a linear stack triangulation of each monotone piece. The output contract is unchanged: the same slice, valid and triangleCount, unused slots degenerate, and polygons that Earcut triangulates are bit-identical to 'none'. The steps are data parallel and dispatched over the vertices of routed polygons only, sized by GPU-resident counts: clean the rings (consecutive duplicates and the closing vertex dropped), rank the vertices in sweep order (y descending, x ascending, row last, a total order that needs no special case for equal y), orient the rings (shell counter-clockwise, holes clockwise) and classify every vertex as start, end, split, merge or regular with the exact orientation, find the edge directly left of every split, merge and right-boundary vertex through the shared edge-slab index, derive the helper diagonals from the members of each left edge sorted by (edge, rank), order the at most four diagonals of a vertex by exact angle, label the pieces by pointer jumping on the half-edge next pointers (rounds in GPU-gated pairs), and triangulate each piece on one invocation. A piece is triangulated by one invocation, so a single huge convex piece is linear but not parallel. Triangles are counter-clockwise, and a polygon with collinear points keeps zero-area triangles (triangleCount counts them), which Earcut would have filtered. valid is 0 when the piece triangles do not add up to vertices + 2 * holes - 2, a chain is not monotone, a predicate is uncertain, a diagonal is out of order, a walk does not close, a ray finds no edge, or a triangle would be clockwise. The triangles of the pieces sum to the polygon boundary by construction (every ring edge is in one piece, every diagonal in two with opposite directions, and each triangle replaces two consecutive edges of its piece by the third side), so with no clockwise triangle a valid result tiles a valid polygon; triangles of zero area between collinear vertices add nothing. Not detected: rings that cross or overlapping holes that leave all of these consistent, which are then covered as often as the rings wind. Not supported (flagged invalid by the monotone path): most polygons whose rings touch at a vertex (87 of every 100 in the search above; the others are tiled). The clockwise test is what flags the rest of them: without it the path alone returned 106 wrong triangulations as valid on those 126,382 polygons, and one in the default configuration on a polygon of 4,306 vertices that 'none' tiles. A polygon that skipped Earcut and that the monotone path flags (or that exceeds the capacity) is then given to Earcut within maximumWork in a second pass, so 'monotone' never does worse than 'none': it is valid wherever 'none' is, and a polygon the monotone path returns as valid is a tiling. Cost: the sweep sort, the member sort and the scans are sized by the capacity (monotoneMaximumVertices, default all vertices, at most getGPUPolygonTriangulationMonotoneMaximumVertexCount(device), 3,355,443 on a default device), so they run even when no polygon is large; pass largePolygonMethod: 'none' or a smaller capacity for inputs known to be small. Large polygons beyond the capacity (in row order) go to the Earcut fallback pass. Inputs with more rows than that limit skip the monotone path entirely and behave like 'none'; only an explicit monotoneMaximumVertices above the limit throws. Scratch is roughly 100 uint32 words per vertex (the largest binding, the pointer-jumping arrays, is 40 bytes per vertex). The GPU spec gpu-polygon-triangulation-monotone.spec.ts checks polygons of 5,000 to 200,000 vertices against the area of Shapely and of shapely.constrained_delaunay_triangles (test/oracles/geometry-triangulation-large.py), the exact triangle count, and the cancellation of the directed triangle edges to the polygon boundary (which proves coverage without overlap); the CPU model of the algorithm and the WGSL kernels (in a WGSL interpreter) are checked on adversarial polygons: combs, spirals, stars, 50 holes, collinear runs, equal heights, duplicate vertices and random polygons.

Maximum: the scratch holds 2 * vertices + 5 * (rings - polygons) + 8 * polygons nodes (getPolygonTriangulationNodeCount; one node per hole more than before 2026-11-11, for the vertex inserted into a touched edge), at most getGPUPolygonTriangulationMaximumNodeCount(device) (4,793,490 on a default device, 28 bytes per node; 8,388,608 with useZOrderHash: false). GPURandomPointsInPolygon and GPUDotDensity triangulate internally and apply the same limit under their own ID.

Planar only: spatialContext defaults to native planar coordinates and a longitude-latitude context throws; project to a planar frame first (createGPULocalFrame, GPULocalFrameProjection). Orientation signs are exact.

GPUVoronoiDiagram — Derives Voronoi edges from Delaunay triangles, each with the two sites it separates.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.voronoi-diagram rung=result caption="Same 319 places · blue: Voronoi edges, hull rays clipped 100 m beyond the places"}

GPUDelaunayTessellation builds an unconstrained planar point triangulation by parallel insertion and flipping. Exact duplicate coordinates use first-row ownership, collinear input produces no triangles (so GPUVoronoiDiagram, which derives edges only from triangles, returns nothing for two sites or for collinear sites, although those have bisectors), and cocircular ties are broken by Simulation of Simplicity on row order (a lifted perturbation), so the result is the unique perturbed Delaunay triangulation whatever the parallel schedule. Every output triangle is counter-clockwise, rotated so its smallest row comes first, and the rows are sorted by their first two indices. output.status uses canonical count, exact requiredCount, overflow, and optional invalidCount; status.duplicateCount reports discarded site rows. Optional status.converged is one only for successful convergence, status.iterationCount counts active steps, and status.iterationLimitReached distinguishes budget exhaustion from invalid input. Use getDelaunayMaximumTriangleCount(pointCount) for the general-position upper bound.

The first three steps insert a seed simplex: the first and last distinct sites in the coordinate-bit sort, then the first site not collinear with that edge (exact orientation). This puts a lone apex into the triangulation before bulk insertion of collinear sites, preventing the former linear flip chain. Collinear input simply skips the third seed. Subsequent steps insert, in parallel, one site into every triangle that still contains one (the site of smallest pseudo-random priority, a bijective hash of its row; a site on an edge splits both triangles of the edge), then runs one parallel Lawson flip pass: an edge whose quad fails the exact in-circle test flips when it holds the smallest key of both of its triangles. Insertion is valid in any triangulation and exact Lawson flipping converges from any planar triangulation, so no repair stage is needed (gDel2D needs star splaying only because its float flips are inexact). Steps stop on the GPU when every site is in and no edge is illegal: the remaining steps are indirect dispatches of nothing. The initial budget B = 4 ceil(log2(n + 2)) + 32 + ceil(sqrt(n) / 2) now continues on those same GPU state words up to min(4096, max(B, 2n + 32)) steps. Up to 2,048 sites, only the first B rounds are encoded in parallel; two bounded GPU kernels finish any remaining insertion and then exact Lawson legalization with one active invocation each. They reuse the topology and predicates, without CPU readback or reducing the iteration limit. At 1,000 sites this encodes 794 iteration nodes instead of 18,288. Larger inputs retain nine nodes per allowed step. The GPU phase counter keeps parallel steps on the same nine pipelines; no host iteration enters shader source or overrides. Active work is O(n) per pass, with no universal logarithmic convergence claim. The rare small-input continuation trades parallel throughput for fewer encoded dispatches; its worst-case latency remains input-dependent. At the documented maximum, unconverged work yields zero triangles, increments invalidCount, and publishes converged = 0, iterationLimitReached = 1 and the actual iterationCount. This is a bounded safety net, not a proof that every arbitrary Lawson chain finishes in 4096 steps. The CPU WGSL executor checks 2000 collinear-plus-apex sites against T = 2n - 2 - h (1998 triangles) and exact empty circles.

The starting super triangle is symbolic: its three vertices are points at infinity (towards +x, +y and -x, with infinitesimally tilted directions and hierarchically growing radii), never finite coordinates. Every orientation or incircle test that touches one reduces to exact comparisons of real coordinates: a triangle with one infinite vertex has the half-plane left of its real edge as circumcircle (a site on the edge's line is inside only when strictly between the endpoints), and a triangle with two or three infinite vertices is decided by one exact orientation sign. Real hull triangles are therefore never lost to a finite super vertex, so the result is the complete Delaunay triangulation of the convex hull, 2n - 2 - h triangles for n distinct sites and h hull-boundary points. An earlier finite supertriangle (corners at 32 extents) dropped hull triangles, for example 2 of about 1200 for 600 uniform points and 51 of 185 for a 1e-2-thick band.

GPUVoronoiDiagram consumes those triangle rows and their GPU-resident count without readback. Internal edges connect adjacent circumcenters; hull bisectors extend to a dynamic one-row clipBounds view. Its compact float32x4 segments retain the two source site IDs and canonical bounded status. Input triples may have either winding; hull rays always point away from the opposite site. Voronoi and alpha hull share independently power-of-two-scaled edge equations, preserving the short-axis component when edge lengths differ greatly. The center is restored after adding its origin in a scaled frame, so an overflowing relative offset or radius does not invalidate a representable center. Hull directions use scaled endpoints when subtraction would overflow; ray exits come from the clip bounds without a fixed distance cap. Collinear triangles, an uncertifiable orientation, or an unrepresentable final center produce no Voronoi edges and increment status.invalidCount when supplied. The solve remains float32; exact orientation does not promise an exact center for nearly collinear triangles. Both operations are deterministic for a fixed row order and use finite planar f32 coordinates; they do not claim constrained Delaunay, polygonal Voronoi cells, or concave construction. Orientation and incircle tests are adaptive exact on the f32 coordinates. Both are planar only: a longitude/latitude spatialContext throws at construction (project first with createGPULocalFrame and GPULocalFrameProjection), because a lng/lat Delaunay is not a spherical one.

GPU_LINE_DENSITY_PARAMETER_SCHEMA exposes typed GPUSpatialParameterSchema<'float32'> metadata, including wordLength and fields, through the public spatial-analysis entry point. Its type is preserved for native NodeNext TypeScript consumers as well as workspace imports.

Sums street length per grid cell and divides by cell area, producing a line-density raster.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-density rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: metres of street per 100 m cell"}

Line length per grid cell and its density (length in the cell over cell area; not the QGIS or ArcGIS "Line density" tool, see Semantics). Props: positions, lineOffsets, columns, rows, optional spatialContext (native planar by default, or longitude-latitude with the great-circle metric), recordCapacity, parameters (getGPULineDensityParameterValues({minX, minY, cellWidth, cellHeight})) and output: {lengths, densities?, status: {overflow, requiredCount?}}. Segments are clipped to the grid and walked cell by cell, and the lengths are summed per cell by a stable sort and a fixed-order reduction, so results are bitwise reproducible. Spherical mode measures each clipped piece in the context's length unit using the mean radius (radius); cell areas use getGPUAuthalicSphereRadius (the new areaRadius field), in that unit squared. Lengths default to meters and cell areas to square meters; units: 'kilometers' gives kilometers and square kilometers. A longitude/latitude segment is cut by the grid along the straight line between its vertices in degrees and each piece is measured as a great circle, so the total of a long segment depends on the grid (the segment (0, 60) to (90, 60) is 4,605 km as one piece and about 5,004 km when cut by 1 degree cells): densify long segments first. Exceeding recordCapacity sets status.overflow and under-counts the last segments. A segment on a cell boundary goes to the upper or right cell, and closed rings are not special (pass ring edges as lines). Length per polygon is GPULineLengthPerPolygon.

Maximum: columns * rows is at most GPU_LINE_DENSITY_MAXIMUM_CELL_COUNT (16,777,215), a device-independent constant: the per-cell sum dispatches one 256-thread workgroup per cell and the invocation index is a u32.

Uses resolveGPUSpatialContext and its shared validation errors. Declares measure: 'area': an azimuthal-equidistant local frame throws; project into a Lambert equal-area frame.

Semantics. densities is the line length inside the cell divided by the cell area, a box kernel with the grid cell as its support, in context length units per square unit (meters per square meter by default for longitude/latitude, kilometers per square kilometer with units: 'kilometers'). It is not the ArcGIS or QGIS circular-search-radius line density, which spreads each line over a disk and is smooth; those need a smoothing step. For a smooth field, rasterize lengths (or densities) and convolve it with GPUConvolution (with the same cell-index and zero-boundary caveats as GPUPointDensity smoothing), or sample line points with GPURandomPointsOnLine and use GPUKernelDensity.

GPULineLengthPerPolygon

Sums the street length inside each polygon, with the count of segments that contribute.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-length-per-polygon rung=result caption="Grey: sampled streets crossing three community areas · blue: total clipped street length in metres"}

The 448 sampled street centrelines span Lincoln Park (area 7), Logan Square (22), and West Town (24). Both inputs use UTM 16N metres with the same false origin. Lengths describe this street extract, not each community area's entire network. Regenerate only this extract with python3 scripts/lens-data/s3-geometry-data.py --line-length-per-polygon-only.

Total line length inside each polygon (QGIS "Sum line lengths", PostGIS sum(ST_Length(ST_Intersection(line, polygon)))). Props: positions, lineOffsets, polygons (a four-level GPUPolygonGeometryPort), optional lineWeights, spatialContext (omitted: planar/native; or longitude-latitude with the great-circle metric, replacing the former coordinateSystem and radius), pairCapacity (default max(1024, 8 * positions.length)), maximumCrossingCount (default 64, at most 1024: the crossings live in private memory) and output: {lengths, weightedLengths?, segmentCounts?, status: {overflow}}. Every vertex row that is not the last of its path is a segment. Segments meet polygons through GPUSpatialJoinCandidates, are cut at every ring-edge crossing, and each piece whose midpoint is inside (even-odd over all rings, so holes and multipolygons work, decided by the exact point-in-ring rule of utils/exact-geometry-wgsl.ts; the cut parameters are f32 and only place pieces) is kept. Lengths are summed per polygon by a stable sort and a fixed-order segmented sum, so results are bitwise reproducible (segment counts use integer atomics). weightedLengths is sum(length * pathWeight) (weights are per path, not per segment) and segmentCounts counts segments with positive length inside. A longitude-latitude context clips straight in longitude/latitude, as GPULineDensity does, and measures each piece as the great-circle distance between its endpoints. status.overflow is 1 when the candidate, BVH or crossing capacity was exceeded, and lengths can then be low. Optional status.candidateOverflow isolates the candidate stage (pair capacity or BVH); it is 0 when only the crossing bound was exceeded. The output is per polygon, so there is no per-row flag.

Maxima: positions has at most getGPULineLengthPerPolygonMaximumPositionCount(device) (8,388,608 on a default device) rows and pairCapacity is at most getGPULineLengthPerPolygonMaximumPairCapacity(device) (16,777,216; the default is clamped to it). maximumCrossingCount is in [1, 1024].

Longitude/latitude edges must be linear. Prepare polygons that cross ±180 or enclose a pole with GPULonLatPolygonPreparation; optional one-row uint32 fields output.status.antimeridianUncertainCount / output.status.poleUncertainCount report unprepared rings. They are cleared and rewritten every encoding in lon/lat contexts. Supplying them with a planar context is allowed and leaves them untouched.

Declares measure: 'distance': a Lambert equal-area local frame throws; use an azimuthal-equidistant frame. Longitude/latitude requires metric: 'great-circle'; other metrics throw the shared resolveGPUSpatialContext error. Lengths use the context's length unit (meters by default for longitude/latitude, kilometers with units: 'kilometers').

Limits: pieces lying exactly along a ring edge are ambiguous and may or may not count; lines are not unioned, so overlaps count once per line, as in QGIS; f32 precision (about 1e-6 of the segment length). The spec checks 150 paths against 40 polygons against a float64 oracle at 5e-4 relative with exact counts, and spherical lengths against a haversine oracle. No QGIS pin.

GPUGridGenerator

Generates square, hexagon, triangle or point grids from an origin and cell size, flagging cells that touch a polygon.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.grid-generator rung=result caption="Hexagons coloured by contact with West Town · grey: 1,275 places"}

Grid geometry (turf squareGrid, hexGrid, triangleGrid, pointGrid, QGIS create grid, PostGIS ST_SquareGrid). Props: gridType ('square', 'hex', 'triangle' or 'point'), columns, rows, parameters (getGPUGridGeneratorParameterValues({minX, minY, cellWidth, cellHeight})) and output: {positions?, centers?, corners?, cornerIntersects?}. Size cell outputs with getGPUGridCellCount and getGPUGridVerticesPerCell; size the unique square or hex vertex lattice with getGPUGridCornerCount. Hexagons are pointy-top by default (cellWidth is flat to flat), triangles come in alternating strips with half-cell row shifts (not turf's exact layout), and the point grid sits at cell centers. The cell counts are compile-time. The lattice is planar: spatialContext accepts only a planar context (the default) and throws for longitude/latitude, so project first or use GPUPointToCell.

  • hexOrientation: 'flat' (GeoPandas make_grid(flat_topped=True)): cellWidth is still the flat-to-flat distance (GeoPandas cell_size), columns step 1.5 * R, rows step cellWidth, odd columns shift up half a cell, and the ring is counter-clockwise from angle 0. The lattice is anchored at (minX - R / 2, minY), where GeoPandas starts half a cell off the offset. Matched GeoPandas to 1e-5 on 16 cells.
  • extent (a nested {kind: 'polygons', positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets} port) and output.intersects (GeoPandas make_grid(intersect=True)): a per-cell uint32 flag against a polygon (even-odd rings, holes, multi-polygon) with exact orientation predicates. Boundary contact counts, and point grids test the center. Cost is cells times extent edges. It is a flag, not a compaction: compact it with GPUCompaction for the subset. Matched Shapely intersects on five grid types (240 cells).
  • output.corners implements GeoPandas make_grid(feature_type='corners') for square and hex grids. It emits every shared and boundary vertex exactly once using an integer lattice, without sorting or floating-point hashing. output.cornerIntersects independently tests those points against extent, including polygon and hole boundaries. Triangle and point grids do not expose corner output.
  • Cell polygons compute every vertex from the same integer-lattice expression as output.corners, so neighbouring cells share bit-identical vertices (no 1-ulp cracks or overlaps along seams).

GPUHilbertKeys

Computes the Hilbert curve index of each point or box centre, optionally sorting rows into curve order.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.hilbert-keys rung=result caption="Places coloured by order-6 Hilbert key: neighbours on the curve share colour"}

2D Hilbert curve index (order 1 to 16, uint32) of positions, or of feature box centers (minima and maxima), within per-frame bounds [minX, minY, maxX, maxY] (or the GPU-reduced bounds of the valid items when omitted; items outside are clamped to the border cells). output.keys holds the keys, bit-exact against the CPU xy2d on exactly representable data, and optional sortedRows and sortedKeys give the stable curve-order permutation from a radix GPUSort. GPULonLatPolygonPreparation

Prepares lon/lat polygons for planar consumers by densifying edges, splitting at the antimeridian and capping pole rings.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.lon-lat-polygon-preparation rung=result caption="Blue: 13 harbour zones densified to 0.005 degrees; none crosses the antimeridian"}

Prepares longitude/latitude polygon rings for linear-degree containment, rasterization and other polygon consumers. All classification, densification, capping and splitting runs on the GPU; construction validates only declared properties and view lengths. Non-pole edges take the short longitude path. A degree-plane edge along the same pole retains its raw longitude step, so Natural-Earth-style Antarctica needs no extra cap or split.

Prop Contract
positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets GPUPolygonGeometryFields; implicit ring closure, shell first and holes afterward.
spatialContext (required) Declare coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric 'none' or 'great-circle', and optional edgeModel: 'linear' (default) or 'great-circle'. Omission has no default and throws at construction.
parameters Four float32 words from getGPULonLatPolygonPreparationParameterValues(edgeModel, settings?, target?); GPU_LON_LAT_POLYGON_PREPARATION_PARAMETER_LENGTH = 4.
maximumPieceCount Integer in [1, 65536], default 1024, caps pieces per original edge.
antimeridian 'split' (default) or 'reject' crossing features.
poleRings 'cap' (default) or 'reject' pole-enclosing features.
output GPULonLatPolygonPreparationOutput, caller-owned views and status scalars.
id Defaults to 'lon-lat-polygon-preparation'.

GPULonLatPolygonPreparationSettings.maximumSegmentDegrees is the largest longitude or central-angle step in degrees: default 0.1 for great-circle input and 0 for linear input. Zero disables densification; great-circle input with zero is passed through as linear and sets approximation. The helper rejects negative or NaN steps with getGPULonLatPolygonPreparationParameterValues maximumSegmentDegrees must be >= 0.

The output spatialContext preserves the input metric, units and datum and declares edgeModel: 'linear'. output.positions is a capacity-sized float32x2 view with written longitudes in [-180, 180]. Given F features, P polygons and R rings, allocate featureOffsets with F + 1 rows, polygonOffsets with 2P + 1 rows and ringOffsets with 2R + 1 rows. Each input feature keeps its row: featureOffsetsOut[f] = 2 * featureOffsetsIn[f]. Within a feature, all main polygon copies come first, then all east copies. Ring roles are preserved, including zero-vertex east copies for noncrossing polygons. Unused rings and polygons stay in the topology. Any invalid ring empties its whole feature; ambiguity, width greater than 360 degrees, nonfinite coordinates, latitude outside [-90, 90], or either reject option can cause this.

Optional ringFlags has R uint32 rows: bit 0 crossing, bit 1 pole-capped, bit 2 ambiguous edge, bit 3 wider than 360 degrees, bit 4 nonfinite coordinate or invalid latitude, bit 5 densification clamped. status.overflow is required; optional count reports vertices written, requiredCount reports the exact final total when no intermediate stage overflowed, invalidCount counts rejected features once each, and approximation is one when great-circle densification was clamped or disabled. Dense, cap and both copies' clip streams each have capacity output.positions.length; clipping runs only against longitude -180 and 180 because classification validates latitudes. Overflow from any stage, including final merge, sets overflow = 1; all offsets remain monotone and within capacity. Status is rewritten each encoding. If an intermediate stage overflowed, requiredCount instead reports the conservative bound 8 * (D + 3 * R), saturated at 0xffffffff, where D is the unclamped dense vertex count (after maximumPieceCount) and R is the input ring count. Capping adds at most three vertices per ring, each longitude boundary at most doubles a stream, and there are two copies. When representable, this bound fits every intermediate stream and the final output on a rerun with the same input and parameters. Size output.positions from requiredCount; transient memory scales with it. Dense scan offsets also saturate at 0xffffffff, preserving the written prefix after a uint32 carry. Additional source-row scan arrays and topology metadata scale with input vertices and rings, without a maximumPieceCount multiplier. An overflowed intermediate result is incomplete; use the resized rerun for analysis.

Applications can format readback diagnostics with these exact messages (the library does not log):

  • ${n} features rejected: ambiguous or over-wide rings
  • ${n} rings cross the antimeridian; prepare polygons first (consumer antimeridianUncertainCount)
  • ${n} rings enclose a pole; prepare polygons first (consumer poleUncertainCount)
  • densification clamped; raise maximumPieceCount

Sutherland-Hodgman clipping can leave zero-width bridges on the seam. The half-open containment rule assigns a point at -180 to the west side only; a point at +180 belongs to neither side. Kept canonical vertices retain their exact float32 bits. Wrapped longitude plus integer turns persists through intermediate buffers; reconstructed longitudes exist only in shader registers.

A ring winding once around longitude is capped at the nearer pole, using the sign of its mid-latitude (minLat + maxLat) / 2; exactly zero is ambiguous. The cap retains the unwrapped closing endpoint before the two pole vertices, preserving the final edge. The heuristic ignores winding direction and can choose the wrong cap for an intended complement. Represent that complement as a world shell with the ring as a hole, or choose poleRings: 'reject'. Great-circle minor arcs through a pole, and antipodal endpoints, are ambiguous in v1.

Edge models matter for long edges. At 45 degrees latitude, an east-west edge spanning 1 degree bows about 120 m as a great circle; 10 degrees gives about 12 km. A 60-degree edge reaches about 49.1 N instead of 45 N, a deviation of about 455 km. Approximate H3 average-edge deviations at 45 N:

Resolution Average edge length Great-circle / linear-degree deviation
0 1281 km about 32 km
1 483 km about 4.6 km
2 183 km about 660 m
3 69 km about 93 m
4 26 km about 13 m
5 9.9 km about 1.9 m

Multiply these estimates by about 1.7 at 60 degrees. The originally proposed 1.2 m bound for a 0.1-degree step is slightly low: the binary64 oracle measures 1.336 m on the 60-degree edge at 45 N. Use about 1.3 m for that fixture (the regression limit is 1.4 m), before float32 position rounding; this is not a universal certified error bound. See the longitude/latitude precision notes for the additional coordinate quantization error.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.rectangle-clip rung=result caption="Grey: Wicker Park streets · blue: pieces inside the 900 m by 700 m rectangle"}
