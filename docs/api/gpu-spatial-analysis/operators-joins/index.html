Spatial joins

GPUSpatialPredicateJoin

candidateTileCapacity bounds paged candidate scratch independently of the unchanged global candidateCapacity. Final pairs and weights remain globally capacity bounded; inspect candidate and output overflow separately.

Finds feature pairs that satisfy a spatial predicate such as intersects, contains or within, and counts matches per feature.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-predicate-join rung=result caption="102 parks against 199 tracts; tracts shaded by the number of parks they intersect."}

Joins two sets of features by a spatial predicate, like GeoPandas sjoin or PostGIS ST_Intersects, ST_Contains, ST_Within and ST_DWithin. Coordinates are planar by default; a longitude/latitude spatialContext selects great-circle dwithin in meters (see Spatial context). Either side is {kind: 'points', positions}, {kind: 'lines', positions, lineOffsets} (one linestring per feature) or {kind: 'polygons', positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets} (multipolygons, holes as later rings, GeoArrow layout); all nine combinations are supported.

Multipart points and lines use GPUMultipartPointGeometryPort and GPUMultipartLineGeometryPort from @luma-contributors/analysis/analysis-core. Their featureOffsets map feature rows to point or linestring parts; a line part's lineOffsets then map to vertices. Offsets start at zero, are nondecreasing and terminate at the child count. sourceIds has one row per feature. Repeated IDs do not merge features. Measures, affine transform, prepared join indices and the following joins share that hierarchy.

For GPUSpatialPredicateJoin, multipart operands support intersects, within and coveredBy with engine: 'auto' or 'fast', without a relate output. Both sides may be multipart. leftRows and rightRows always identify FEATURE rows, with one pair per matching feature pair:

  • intersects accepts any matching part pair, including a match in a later part.
  • within requires all usable input parts to lie in the target's closure and at least one interior intersection. A feature with all parts on the target boundary is not within it.
  • coveredBy requires all usable parts to lie in the target's closure, allowing boundary-only contact. Containment is against the complete target feature, including the union of line parts.

Empty features never match. Empty line parts contribute no edges; short line parts with fewer than two vertices are empty. Multipart line boundary points follow the mod-2 endpoint rule: an endpoint shared by two open line parts belongs to the feature interior. Line coverage merges intervals on a supplied coordinate axis, preserving distinct endpoints even when projecting them onto a long query would round them to the same fraction. The BVH indexes feature bounds spanning all parts, so separate parts do not occupy separate leaf slots. Bounds may overlap across a gap, but exact refinement never draws a line across that gap. candidateCapacity budgets logical feature pairs, including bounding-box false positives. If a feature loses a candidate reached through any of its parts, its candidateTruncated row and status.candidateOverflow are set. A short BVH leafCapacity also sets candidate loss. Final pair capacity loss stays in overflow; increasing final capacity cannot recover candidates.

GPUNearestFeatureJoin supports multipart queries and targets in neighbors mode (neighborIds and neighborCounts), with minimum distance over every usable part pair. Query and target rows are feature rows; neighborRows retains the target row separately from neighborIds. Containment of any later part in a polygon gives distance zero. The existing k, tie, radius, attribute and exclusion rules apply to feature rows. Each topology and output binding must fit the device's storage-binding limit; sorted multipart line queries pack their topology into one binding to stay within the eight-buffer traversal budget. Ranking and pruning use the same normalized squared-distance significand and biased exponent on CPU and GPU, including squares outside float32 range. Normal small distances therefore remain distinct from an exact hit without reducing ordinary squared-distance precision. Subnormal coordinates or distances are outside the GPU precision contract and may flush or clamp. At the largest finite distances, the final two squared-rank bins coalesce with their predecessor and publish the maximum float32 distance; row order breaks those ties. For normal distances or exact hits, a radius of either positive or negative zero admits exact hits only, including great-circle searches. Endpoint distances use the endpoint directly, and cancellation-prone segment determinants retain integer product residuals before distance conversion. Targets whose published distances round alike may still have different ranks. Finite linear distances are supported when their squares exceed the float32 range. Combined sorted-query topology binding sizes are checked before graph resources are declared when query sorting actually packs metadata (at least two query features), permitting an unsorted retry after a metadata-size rejection. Prepared consumers require identical position and topology views, including the presence or absence of feature grouping.

Other multipart predicates, DE-9IM output/engine: 'relate', and nearest-feature mode remain rejected with explicit messages. GPUPointInPolygonJoin and GPUBufferSelection still reject multipart point query ports; ordinary point ports have one query per position row.

GPUPointInPolygonJoin and GPUBufferSelection infer the point input when a helper returns the broader GPUSpatialJoinGeometry union and the caller narrows it with kind === 'points'. This preserves valid single-point callers without a cast. A known multipart point port remains a type error; if grouping arrives through a broader union, construction rejects featureOffsets before flattening or declaring graph resources. Their props types keep single-point defaults and expose an optional point-geometry type parameter for explicitly typed union inputs.

Pure computeSpatialPredicateJoinOnCPU and computeNearestFeatureJoinOnCPU take packed typed arrays with the same hierarchy. The CPU predicate supports the three predicates above, excluding polygon/polygon containment, and takes explicit candidateCapacity and pairCapacity limits. CPU predicate candidates use a sorted bounds index and obey the explicit candidate budget; requiredCount is a lower bound after candidate loss. CPU nearest selects one unbounded planar neighbor per query feature, breaks distance ties by lowest target row, uses a sorted bounds index with range pruning and emits float32 distances. Empty queries produce empty per-query columns; queries with no targets produce count 0, row/ID 0xffffffff and distance -1. The CPU predicate result's candidateBoundsTestCount counts index bounds tested, separately from result status; entire ranges ending before or starting after a query are pruned together.

predicate is 'intersects', 'contains', 'within', 'dwithin' (with an inclusive distance: planar in coordinate units, or meters with a great-circle spatialContext), 'covers', 'coveredBy', 'touches', 'crosses', 'overlaps', 'equals', 'containsProperly' or 'relate' (with a DE-9IM pattern). Boundaries follow OGC / DE-9IM: a point has no boundary, a linestring's boundary is its endpoints unless it is closed, and a polygon's boundary is its rings.

  • intersects: the closed geometries share a point, so polygons touching at an edge or corner intersect, and so does a point on a polygon boundary.
  • contains(a, b): b lies in the closure of a and the interiors intersect. A polygon does not contain a point or line on its boundary, and a line does not contain its own endpoints.
  • within(a, b) is contains(b, a).
  • dwithin: the minimum distance between the closed geometries is at most distance.
  • covers and coveredBy: contains and within that allow boundary contact.
  • touches: the boundaries meet and the interiors do not (never true for two points).
  • crosses: the interiors meet and the intersection has a lower dimension than the larger input, by the OGC dimension rules (false for polygon/polygon and point/point).
  • overlaps: the same dimension, the interiors meet and neither contains the other.
  • equals: the same point set. containsProperly: b lies in the interior of a.
  • relate with pattern: any DE-9IM pattern (characters T F * 0 1 2), as a string or an any-of list.

The pipeline is upstream primitives end to end: per-feature bounds into a GPUBVH over the right side, a count pass, GPUScan and an ordered write of candidate pairs, the exact predicate per candidate (single-point/polygon pairs use GPUPairwisePointInPolygon; points on an edge it reports as uncertain are decided by the f32 kernel and counted in uncertainCount), then a scan and a capacity-bounded scatter. Outputs go in output; output.pairs (a GPUCompactPairPort: leftRows, rightRows, status with count, overflow, optional requiredCount) are unique and sorted by (left, right), and scalars go in output.status (overflow, candidateCount, uncertainCount). The sides are geometry ports (GPUPackedGeometryPort of the point, line or four-level polygon port). On overflow the output is a sorted subset; join.getCapacityPlan({candidateCount, requiredCount}) turns the read-back counts into the next capacities (see Shared ports, status and planning). candidateCapacity defaults to that plan's estimate, min(rightCount, 8) per left feature.

Pass output.weights to also write the matches as a GPUSpatialWeights with left features as rows and weight 1; set excludeSameRow for a self join. A predicate join is then a weights producer, for example dwithin between polygons. Optional output.weights.distances contains the minimum closed-geometry distance aligned with neighbors: planar coordinate units, or meters for great-circle dwithin. Intersecting or contained geometries have distance zero; holes contribute their boundaries. Planar distance output uses scaled norms, so representable distances such as 1e20 do not saturate when their square exceeds float32. Normal gaps beside long, asymmetric segments remain measurable even when a projection fraction would underflow. Large dwithin thresholds use the same linear distance in both engines; ordinary thresholds retain squared comparisons. Unused distance slots are NaN on every encoding, including empty results. Distance output requires a metric (native or great-circle); longitude/latitude topology with metric: 'none' cannot produce distances. The optional weights.status fields count, requiredCount, overflow, candidateOverflow and uncertainCount are written even when no pair output is requested. overflow reports final pair capacity, and candidateOverflow reports an incomplete candidate or BVH stage. Distance evaluation adds one packed pair scratch buffer (eight bytes per output slot), included by the instance's getCapacityPlan().

Declared status destinations, including inherited and non-enumerable requiredCount getters, are captured at construction and retained when implicit completeness destinations are added. The pair-gather example preserves the caller's pair requirement destination in the same way. Geometry descriptors, options and the nested pair, unmatched and weights maps are captured too. Replacing unmatched.ids after construction cannot bypass the original output-alias checks.

For downstream statistics, pass join.outputWeights, which always includes both overflow and candidateOverflow. Missing caller scalar views are allocated in the graph and written on every submission. The caller's output.weights is an allocation description; rebuilding an input from only that description can omit completeness signals.

The new predicates are masks over one DE-9IM matrix per candidate pair, computed from structure (vertex locations, edge crossings, collinear overlaps, and edge pieces located against the other geometry, with polygon areas decided from edge pieces) for all nine point, line and polygon combinations. Semantics follow GEOS and Shapely. intersects, contains, within and dwithin keep their short-circuiting kernels and use the matrix only when relate output is requested; the specs assert both paths agree. Only bounding-box candidates are enumerated, so a pattern that admits disjoint geometries (all of II, IB, BI and BB allow F) is rejected at construction; use how: 'anti' with intersects for disjoint. Lines are single linestrings with the OGC endpoint boundary (multilinestrings and mod-2 boundaries are not modeled). The matrix kernel is O(n * m) per pair, which suits small features and is not tuned for large polygons.

Orientation tests are exact. Edge pieces between crossings are located from an exactly located endpoint and the exact crossings between it and the piece, not from their rounded midpoint, so edges within a few ulps of each other (near-parallel or nearly shared) classify like Shapely. Distances and the parameters that order crossings along an edge run in f32, so recenter or project coordinates before measuring near-degenerate geometry. Polygons must be valid.

graph.add(new GPUSpatialPredicateJoin({
  left: {kind: 'points', positions: points},
  right: {kind: 'polygons', positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets},
  predicate: 'within',
  candidateCapacity: 1 << 20,
  output: {pairs: {leftRows, rightRows, status: {count, overflow}}}
}));

relate output. output.relate is an optional GraphDataView<'uint32'> as long as pairs.leftRows; slot k holds the matrix of (leftRows[k], rightRows[k]). Each of the nine cells (order II IB IE BI BB BE EI EB EE, row-major, left parts then right parts) takes 2 bits at [2k, 2k + 1] with value dimension + 1 (0 empty, 1 point, 2 line, 3 area). formatGPUSpatialRelate(value) decodes it on the CPU to a string such as 'FF2F11212'. Bit 31 (GPU_SPATIAL_RELATE_UNCERTAIN_BIT) of a slot is set when an orientation of that pair had no sign (see Exactness); formatGPUSpatialRelate ignores it, and value & 0x3ffff is the matrix alone. It is not available for dwithin.

graph.add(new GPUSpatialPredicateJoin({
  left, right, predicate: 'touches', candidateCapacity: 1 << 16,
  output: {pairs: {leftRows, rightRows, status: {count, overflow}}, relate, status: {uncertainCount}}
}));

Anti joins. how: 'anti' with output.unmatched: GPUCompactOutput emits the left rows that have no match, in ascending order (pairs, weights and relate are not allowed with it). When the candidate capacity overflows, the left rows whose candidates were dropped cannot be classified (they may have a match among the dropped candidates), so they are kept out of output.unmatched and counted in output.status.candidateTruncatedCount; the anti output then never lists a row that may have a match, and may omit true unmatched rows among the truncated ones. (Before this change those rows were emitted as unmatched.) A BVH overflow (more right features than leafCapacity) drops right features that any row may match, so since 2026-11-10 it marks every left row and the anti output is empty; before, it listed rows whose only matches were among the dropped features.

Candidate overflow. Candidates are written in left-row order, so the slots past candidateCapacity are the tail of that layout: the row whose candidates cross the capacity keeps its first ones, and every later row that had a candidate keeps none; earlier rows are complete. A BVH leaf overflow (more right features than leafCapacity, in the join's own build or in a GPUSpatialJoinPrepared) drops features that any row may match, so it marks every row. output.status.candidateOverflow is 1 when either happened (output.status.overflow keeps its combined meaning), and the optional output.candidateTruncated (one uint32 per left feature) is 1 for exactly the rows described above. output.status.candidateTruncatedCount counts them. GPUPointInPolygonJoin and every mode of GPUNearestFeatureJoin (nearest-feature, planar or great-circle, and neighbors) take the same output.candidateTruncated and output.status.candidateOverflow, one flag per query; a flagged query may show no feature, or a farther one. Neighbors mode and the great-circle search have no candidate table, so a BVH overflow is their only cause. GPUSpatialJoinPrepared holds only the right-hand BVH and has no candidate stage. The join consumers pass the flags through: GPUZonalStatistics (output.candidateTruncated per point and output.status.candidateOverflow), GPUBufferSelection (the same pair) and GPULineLengthPerPolygon (output.status.candidateOverflow only; its output is per polygon).

Exactness. Every orientation sign is exact, in the short-circuiting kernels (engine: 'fast') and in the relate engine alike: an f32 filter decides the clear cases and the rest fall back to exact integer arithmetic (256-bit), with one inlined copy per kernel. So touches, crosses, covers, intersects, contains and the other predicates do not depend on f32 rounding of the orientation test, and the result does not depend on the engine or on the feature size that engine: 'auto' looks at, including shared-edge and collinear configurations. Polygon contains / within in the fast kernel decides on which side of a shared boundary run the two interiors lie with a symbolic probe (a point just beyond an outer vertex and just off its edge, never formed in f32), so an inner polygon that fills a hole of the outer one, or has a hole of the outer one inscribed in it, is not contained for any vertex coordinates. The exactness is with respect to the f32 vertices; edge piece midpoints, break parameters and distances are computed in f32: the location of a piece shorter than about one ulp is not exact, so a line starting one ulp outside a polygon edge can have its crosses piece classified as boundary.

Uncertain pairs. A pair is uncertain when an orientation has no sign: a non-finite coordinate, or product exponents spanning more than 200 bits. It is never dropped silently. The undecidable orientation is treated as collinear (sign 0) and the predicate is evaluated with that value, so the pair can come out wrong in either direction, and it is counted in uncertainCount (zero means every decision is certified). Matched uncertain pairs are identifiable per pair through bit 31 of the relate output. Point/polygon pairs use the robust double-single classifier (they do not get the exact re-decision of GPUPointInPolygonJoin); pairs it cannot certify are decided by the f32 kernel and counted. Scalar exact kernels with eight bindings carry uncertainty in candidate flags and count it in a later pass, so adding an identity multipart grouping preserves the diagnostic. The workgroup dwithin kernel still does not track uncertainty (reported as 0).

Per-frame distance and pattern. distance is a number or a GraphDataView<'float32'>, and pattern is a GPUSpatialRelatePattern or a GraphDataView<'uint32'>. A view is read from a parameter buffer, so it changes without recompiling and gives bit-identical results to the compile-time value (a negative, NaN or infinite distance selects nothing). Fill a pattern view with packGPUSpatialRelatePattern(pattern, slotCount), which writes GPU_SPATIAL_RELATE_PATTERN_WORDS (2) words per slot; unused slots are zero and match nothing, and the helper rejects patterns that admit disjoint geometries. Size candidateCapacity for the largest distance. The exact kernels dispatch by the live candidate count, but the cooperative workgroup kernels (dwithin with engine: 'relate' and the relate engine, 64 and 256 lanes per candidate) index their lanes with a u32, so the constructor rejects a candidateCapacity above floor((2^32 - 1) / lanes) for them, naming the prop. predicate stays compile-time because it selects the kernel family; for a per-frame predicate choice use predicate: 'relate' with patterns.

Relate performance. Per-edge break parameters are collected in one pass (up to 24 per edge) and the covers scan runs only when a collinear edge exists. The workgroup relate kernel also builds a per-pair y-slab index of each polygon side in workgroup memory (32 slabs, 1280 entries per side), so point location scans the closing edges and the query's slab instead of every edge; features under 48 edges, with a degenerate y extent or over 1280 entries use the plain scan. Results are identical. On 100 polygon pairs the batched breaks and the slab index took relate from about 7.5 ms to 4.3 to 5 ms, against about 1 ms for GEOS: the break collection, covers scan and vertex loops are still O(n * m), and about 2 ms of the remaining time is fixed graph and readback cost.

onAttribute: {left, right} (GeoPandas sjoin(on_attribute=)). Two uint32 key views, one per left row and one per right row. A pair survives only when the keys are equal. The filter empties candidate slots before the exact kernels, so candidateCount stays the bounding-box count before filtering, and how: 'anti' then reports left rows with no key-equal match. Its presence is compile-time and its contents are per-frame. Defaults are unchanged.

Engine selection

engine?: 'auto' | 'fast' | 'relate' is compile-time and defaults to 'auto'. It selects the kernel for intersects, contains, within and dwithin; every other predicate always uses the relate engine.

  • 'fast' runs a short-circuiting kernel with one invocation per candidate. It is quickest for small features.
  • 'relate' runs the DE-9IM engine with one workgroup per candidate and bounding-box pruning. For dwithin it runs a workgroup distance kernel instead.
  • 'auto' picks 'relate' when neither side is points and the product of the two sides' average vertices per feature is at least 1024.

Requesting relate output always uses the relate engine. Every engine uses the exact orientation sign, so shared boundaries give the same answers on all three. Measured cost is about 10 ms for 100 pairs of 600 x 175 vertices. A pair with hundreds of shared edges costs 5–10 ms. A great-circle dwithin always uses the short-circuiting kernel ('relate' throws, 'auto' picks 'fast').

Spatial context of GPUSpatialPredicateJoin

spatialContext (omitted: native planar) selects how positions are read. It is validated at construction and exposed as the readonly field spatialContext; unsupported combinations throw.

Context Predicates Meaning
planar (default) all Euclidean plane; dwithin distance in coordinate units.
{coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} dwithin only positions are longitude/latitude degrees; distance is meters on the mean Earth sphere (sphereRadius overrides the radius; units: 'kilometers' throws).
{coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'none'} every predicate except dwithin topology in the degree plane, as GEOS and PostGIS geometry do for coordinates in degrees; no distances.

Great-circle dwithin: the candidate stage widens each left box by the angular radius in latitude and by the longitude reach of that radius at the box's far latitude (2 asin(sin(r/2) / cos(lat)), every longitude when the radius reaches a pole), and a box that crosses the antimeridian also probes its copy shifted by 360 degrees, with a 0.1% plus 1e-5 degree margin so rounding never drops a candidate. The exact kernel measures the central angle from a vertex to an edge with geodesicSegmentFraction and geodesicSegmentDistance (GEODESIC_WGSL): edges are straight in longitude/latitude with the longitude difference wrapped at +-180, the fraction of the nearest point is found in the local equirectangular frame of the point, and the distance is the haversine of coordinate differences, which keeps meter-scale offsets well conditioned in f32. The same edge model is used by the intersection and containment tests (exact orientation in degrees, as the planar kernels) and by the network snapping contributors, so there is one convention rather than two. The foot is exact to first order for edges of road length, so densify long edges. Distances are accurate to f32 rounding across the antimeridian and over a pole, up to about 8000 km (a larger compile-time distance throws, a larger per-frame value selects nothing). Pairs whose geometries intersect in the degree plane count as distance 0. Prepare polygons that cross ±180 or enclose a pole with GPULonLatPolygonPreparation. Longitude/latitude contexts require edgeModel: 'linear' (the default); 'great-circle' edges throw with a preparation hint. Optional output.status.antimeridianUncertainCount and output.status.poleUncertainCount report unprepared rings, accumulated over both polygon inputs. They are cleared each encoding in longitude/latitude contexts; planar contexts leave these fields untouched and build no ring check. A numeric great-circle dwithin distance below ten float32 longitude/latitude quanta (about 17 m on the default sphere) warns to use createGPULocalFrame. Oracle: numpy haversine and pyproj.Geod(a=R, f=0) for point pairs, shapely distances in the azimuthal equidistant projection for polygons (see the spec fixtures).

The topological predicates take metric: 'none'; metric: 'great-circle' with them throws, and dwithin with metric: 'none' throws, so the intent is explicit. GPUSpatialJoinCandidates is planar only; GPUPointInPolygonJoin also accepts longitude/latitude with metric: 'none'. GPUNearestFeatureJoin and GPUBufferSelection also take metric: 'great-circle' (see their section); GPUSpatialJoinPrepared and GPUNearestFeatureWeights take no positions with a metric (a prepared tree is a bounding-box tree and may serve a great-circle join) and have no context.

GPUSpatialJoinPrepared

Retains a static geometry snapshot for one side of a join.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-join-prepared rung=result caption="One retained tract snapshot serves both the candidate and the exact park join."}

A static right-hand side for repeated joins. Geometry refresh is gated on the GPU by requested and applied generations. The applied generation changes only after the complete build executes, so discarded encodings and abandoned execution prefixes retry automatically.

The published bounds/BVH/sort stages replay against the retained geometry every encoding. This conservative implementation repeats build work because published nested nodes lack the dispatch metadata needed for whole-build gating. Resident overhead is one copy of every geometry view plus 24 generation-control bytes; the snapshot uses the same view lengths and binding limits.

The first encoding and first encoding after explicit invalidation bypass rebuildWhen; other encodings call it once. Public join props preserve the handle's frame type: use GPUSpatialJoinCandidatesProps<Frame> or GPUSpatialPredicateJoinProps<Frame> when a helper registers props.prepared on a GPUCommandGraph<Frame>. Point-in-polygon and nearest-feature props take the frame type after their existing row/geometry type parameters.

The props and handle accept a frame type parameter, for example GPUSpatialJoinPrepared<{geometryChanged: boolean}>; use the same type on GPUCommandGraph. The default is unknown, suitable for invalidate() without a frame predicate. See the execution guide for a typed predicate example. A recorded refresh request remains pending until its complete preparation executes. encodedBuildCount counts CPU refresh-request encodings, including discarded requests, and excludes automatic GPU retries and replayed builds.

const prepared = new GPUSpatialJoinPrepared({geometry: polygons});
graph.add(prepared); // before the joins that use it
graph.add(new GPUSpatialPredicateJoin({
  left: movingPoints, right: polygons, prepared, predicate: 'within', candidateCapacity: 1 << 20,
  output: {pairs: {leftRows, rightRows, status: {count, overflow}}}
}));
graph.add(new GPUPointInPolygonJoin({
  points: movingPoints, polygons, prepared, candidateCapacity: 1 << 20,
  output: {pointFeatureIds, status: {overflow: pointOverflow}}
}));
prepared.invalidate(); // request a fresh geometry snapshot; discarded encodings retry it

It reuses geometry: every join still evaluates all candidates against the current left and right geometry each encoding. A right side that changed without invalidate() keeps the old tree, so candidates come from the old boxes and a feature that moved outside its old box is missed. Storage is persistent buffers, allocated by the handle and freed by destroy(), or passed as storage. One handle belongs to one graph, and several joins may share it. A sorted prepared handle is accepted by GPUPointInPolygonJoin, GPUSpatialPredicateJoin, GPUSpatialJoinCandidates and GPUNearestFeatureJoin (points, lines and polygons, not segments). Sorting preserves logical feature rows, including multipart grouping.

Maximum (GPUNearestFeatureJoin, neighbors mode and great-circle): the node table 5 * (2 * leafCapacity - 1) + 2 * queries (plus 2 * features with a filter) is at most getGPUNearestFeatureJoinMaximumNodeTableWordCount(device) (2^25 words on a default device), which allows 2^21 leaves without queries; leafCapacity is at most getGPUNearestFeatureJoinMaximumLeafCapacity(device) (2^23).

Spatial joins over a GPUBVH built from feature bounds every encoding. GPUPointInPolygonJoin classifies each (point, feature) candidate with the shared exact orientation sign (even-odd rings, union polygons) and assigns each point the containing feature with the smallest row (see the boundary rule below); GPUNearestFeatureJoin measures candidates with GPUPairwisePointSegmentDistance and keeps the nearest point or segment feature within a per-frame radius. Both take the points as a GPUPointGeometryPort (points and queries; chunked positions and sourceIds allowed) and the features as a geometry port whose optional sourceIds replace feature rows in the outputs. Both write, in output, per-point feature IDs, per-feature counts, optional compact matches, and one output.status.overflow flag covering BVH leaf, candidate, and match capacity. GPUPointInPolygonJoin takes planar coordinates (the default) or {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'none'}. Containment is topological with straight edges in degrees, as in GeoJSON, and gives the same result as planar. A longitude/latitude context with a metric such as 'great-circle' throws at construction, because the join has no geodesic edges ("project positions to a planar frame first"). GPUNearestFeatureJoin also takes the great-circle context below.

Both joins require linear longitude/latitude edges (omitted edgeModel means 'linear'); edgeModel: 'great-circle' throws with a preparation hint. Prepare polygons that cross ±180 or enclose a pole with GPULonLatPolygonPreparation. Optional output.status.antimeridianUncertainCount and output.status.poleUncertainCount report unprepared rings; nearest joins count both polygon inputs. These counters reset each encoding for longitude/latitude and remain untouched for planar contexts, which build no ring check. Empty rings and polygons in the preparation's fixed 2x topology are accepted. getPointInPolygonContainmentContext preserves the input edge model and datum when selecting metric: 'none', so it cannot bypass edge-model validation.

Great-circle nearest features (GPUNearestFeatureJoin, GPUBufferSelection). With spatialContext: {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} positions are longitude and latitude in degrees and every distance (radius, nearestDistances, neighborDistances, GPUBufferSelection distance and output.distances) is in the context's length unit (meters by default; kilometers with units: 'kilometers') on the mean Earth sphere (sphereRadius overrides the radius in that unit). A longitude-latitude context with another metric and planar contexts with great-circle throw at construction. Both distance operators reject local-lambert-equal-area frames. Both output modes use the branch-and-bound BVH traversal of the neighbors mode (nearest-feature mode is the k = 1 traversal followed by the nearest-feature columns, counts and matches), so candidateCapacity is not used on this path (it is accepted and ignored, candidateCount reads 0, and GPUBufferSelection still validates it). The traversal orders and bounds by central angle. A node is pruned with a lower bound of the great-circle distance between its box and the query box, hav(d) = hav(dlat) + cos^2(max |lat|) hav(dlon), with the latitude gap, the circular longitude gap (through the antimeridian when that is shorter) and the cosine of the latitude farthest from the equator over both boxes, which never overestimates; a box 180 degrees or wider in longitude (a feature across the antimeridian) counts as covering every longitude, and a box touching a pole has cosine 0. Edges are straight in longitude/latitude with wrapped longitude differences, so the vertex boxes bound them exactly. Point to edge distance uses geodesicSegmentFraction and geodesicSegmentDistance; a crossing or containment is decided in the degree plane with the exact orientation sign after the second edge is shifted by a multiple of 360 degrees to overlap the first, and counts as distance 0. Foot points and query points are on the edges with longitude wrapped to +-180. The mixed convention of the planar-in-degrees tests is therefore one edge model; densify edges longer than a few kilometers, because the foot is exact to first order in the local frame of the query. Prepare crossing and pole-enclosing polygons as described above. No warning is possible because the distance is a GPU view; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions. The planar kernels are unchanged and compile none of this code. Oracle: geodesic-nearest-fixtures.ts (numpy 2.5.4 haversine of the edge densified in longitude/latitude, pyproj 3.8.0 Geod(a=R, f=0) cross-check), scenes at the equator, latitude 60, across the antimeridian and next to the pole. GPUNearestFeatureWeights adapts the join's outputs; pass the join's spatialContext so its distances and distanceFloor share the context's length unit and inverse-distance weights use those distances.

Exact orientation. The classifier and the nearest-feature kernels use the same exact orientation sign (an f32 filter with an exact 256-bit integer fallback), so "on the edge" means the same thing in the point-in-polygon join, the predicate join and the nearest-feature join: a point exactly on an edge is distance 0 in all three, and polygon containment (distance 0 inside a polygon) is decided with the exact sign too. Distances and foot points stay f32. Only candidates that no arithmetic can decide stay uncertain: non-finite input or malformed offsets. In GPUPointInPolygonJoin an empty polygon part or a ring with fewer than three vertices contains nothing and is skipped, as in shapely, so the feature's other parts still classify the point. Uncertain pairs are never assigned. They are counted in the optional uncertainCount, so such a point stays unassigned visibly rather than silently.

Boundary rule (GPUPointInPolygonJoin). boundaryRule decides a point exactly on a polygon boundary:

Rule Boundary point Notes
'half-open' (default) belongs to exactly one feature of a valid coverage, independent of row order The owner is the feature containing the point after an infinitesimal move toward +x and +y (+y dominates): left and bottom boundaries are included, right and top boundaries are excluded. A horizontal edge belongs to the feature above it, a vertical edge to the feature on its right, a vertex to the feature whose interior touches its upper right. Points on the outer right or top boundary of a coverage belong to no feature.
'closed' contained by every feature it touches, then the smallest row wins Nothing is lost on the outer boundary; the owner of a shared-boundary point depends on the row order.
'open' belongs to no feature Only strict interior counts; boundary points are lost, not half-assigned.

The half-open decision is the crossing-number convention made exact: a supporting-line tie is broken by the sign of dx (the epsilon term of the move), or of -dy for a vertical edge, so the result follows from the exact orientation signs and the vertex order does not matter. Overlapping features (an invalid coverage) still resolve to the smallest containing row. Oracle: shapely Polygon.contains(Point(x + 1e-9, y + 1e-6)) on integer scenes, 24 row permutations, in the specs. GPUZonalStatistics and the recipes that pass no boundaryRule use the half-open default, so a coverage's counts partition the points.

k nearest features. Set output.neighborIds and output.neighborCounts on GPUNearestFeatureJoin to get the k nearest features per query (PostGIS a <-> b ORDER BY ... LIMIT k, GeoPandas sjoin_nearest). The original mode above is unchanged. Each query walks the BVH nearest child first and prunes boxes farther than the current k-th distance, which is exact and deterministic.

  • queries are points (possibly chunked), lines or polygons (one packed view each); features are points, segments, lines or polygons (multipolygons, holes, even-odd). Distance is the minimum planar distance between the geometries and 0 when a polygon contains the other geometry. Polygons must be valid.

  • k and neighborCapacity are positive integers, bounded by the packed-index maximum 0x7ffffffe (2147483646); actual usable capacity is lower when device storage bindings cannot hold the scratch/output views. neighborCapacity >= k. The 64-slot private-storage threshold is not the public maximum: 65 and larger capacities use storage-backed lists. The interleaved top-k scratch needs 3 + 2 * neighborCapacity uint32 words per scratch query plus three shared control words; each output column needs queryCount * neighborCapacity rows in one binding. Foot-point scratch adds five words per slot. Each must fit maxStorageBufferBindingSize, and all packed indices must fit uint32. ties is 'lowest-id' (default) or 'all', which keeps every feature tied with the k-th. neighborCapacity can exceed k only with ties: 'all'. radius (per-frame, inclusive; omitted means unbounded) bounds the search. Negative, NaN and infinite radius values yield no neighbors for that encoding; a later finite nonnegative radius resumes matching on the same compiled graph.

  • Outputs are dense, query * neighborCapacity + slot, ordered by distance and then feature row: neighborIds (remapped by features.sourceIds), neighborCounts, optional neighborRows (the original feature rows for attribute lookup), optional neighborDistances, neighborFootPoints (the nearest point on the feature, or the crossing point when geometries cross) and neighborSegmentIndices (the start-vertex index of the nearest edge; GPU_NEAREST_NO_SEGMENT for points and containment). Unused slots hold GPU_SPATIAL_JOIN_NO_FEATURE, -1, NaN and GPU_NEAREST_NO_SEGMENT. neighborRows uses GPU_SPATIAL_JOIN_NO_FEATURE in unused slots, requires neighborIds, and has the same length. Allocate disjoint views for IDs and rows even when no sourceIds are supplied.

  • output.status.overflow is 1 when ties: 'all' had more ties than neighborCapacity or the BVH leaf capacity was exceeded. Distances are f32, so near-equal distances can order differently from a double reference, and tie order and the foot point at exact vertex ties depend on evaluation order.

  • The per-query scratch (result slots, and the foot-point scratch when a foot-point output is requested) is one binding while it fits maxStorageBufferBindingSize (128 MiB by default). Beyond that, for example 838k queries with k = 8 and foot points, the scratch holds a fixed chunk of queries (a multiple of 64) that every chunk of queries reuses in turn, so the query count is not limited by the scratch. The caller-owned output views still have to fit their own bindings.

  • exclusive skips features whose ID equals the query's ID (features.sourceIds[row] or the row, and optional queryIds, which require exclusive), so the k neighbors are the nearest other features (STRtree.query_nearest(exclusive=True), sjoin_nearest self-joins). It compares IDs, not geometry equality, which is the same when IDs are unique per geometry. Both modes support it.

  • neighborQueryPoints (float32x2, the layout of neighborIds) is the point of the query geometry nearest to the feature, NaN in unused slots. (neighborQueryPoints, neighborFootPoints) is shapely.shortest_line or ops.nearest_points for every point, line and polygon pair. Ties between parallel edges return the first in vertex order, which can differ from GEOS.

  • onAttribute: {left, right} (as on GPUSpatialPredicateJoin) finds the nearest key-equal feature: the filter runs inside the traversal, and nearest-feature mode filters candidates in the reduce passes.

GPUNearestFeatureWeights

Adapter from the k-nearest output of GPUNearestFeatureJoin to a GPUSpatialWeights CSR (libpysal 4.15 KNN and Graph.build_knn, for points, lines or polygons, using the join's exact geometry distances). Pass the join's neighborRows, neighborCounts, optional neighborDistances and its neighborCapacity as slotCapacity. Pass the join's resolved spatialContext to the adapter (omitted means native planar); distanceFloor uses its length unit and outputWeights.spatialContext carries it to consumers. Per row it keeps the first k valid slots in the join's distance order, drops padding and (with excludeSelf, for self-joins run with k + 1) the query's own feature row, then sorts the feature rows ascending. Short rows stay short. weightType is 'binary' (default) or 'inverse-distance' (1 / max(d, distanceFloor)^power; distanceFloor defaults to 1e-6, the same default as GPUNeighborSearch, so coincident or touching features weigh 1e6^power and dominate a row once it is standardized; pass a floor on the scale of the data when that is not wanted), and output.distances is filled when present (in the join's context length unit, also used by distanceFloor). The output is directed, so for 'symmetrize' or symmetric statistics take the union of the weights and their GPUSpatialWeightsTranspose. excludeSelf compares feature rows to the query row. Request the join's neighborRows alongside its neighborIds; application IDs may repeat across segment rows and must never become CSR column indices. Two winning segments with source IDs [7, 7] produce columns [0, 1] when their distinct rows are used. The adapter's old neighborIds input is rejected; precomputed input must likewise contain distinct feature rows per query. output is a GPUSpatialWeightsOutput. Pass the producing join's status as neighborStatus and provide output.status.candidateOverflow: it carries any upstream search loss, including truncated tie slots. output.status.overflow reports only the adapter's final CSR capacity loss. Both flags are rewritten on each submission. Downstream statistics such as local Moran suppress inference when either is nonzero. For precomputed complete slots, omit neighborStatus; an optional candidate flag then receives zero. The adapter likewise captures its input/output maps at construction. Its final and candidate flags are written in separate passes and may occupy adjacent words of one buffer; using the same word for both is rejected.

The spec pins libpysal on 24 random points with k = 4 (identical neighbor sets). Deviations: ties at the k-th distance keep the lowest feature rows (libpysal breaks them in KDTree order, and KNN and Graph.build_knn disagree with each other on grids); inverse-distance floors the distance instead of dividing by zero.

Spatial sort default

spatialSort of GPUPointInPolygonJoin, GPUNearestFeatureJoin and GPUBufferSelection defaults to true from 256 features and false below; pass false to turn it off. With shuffled features each encoding is 50–125x faster; with spatially coherent features it is about 1 ms slower. GPUSpatialJoinPrepared uses the same threshold: sorting defaults on from 256 logical feature rows and off below it (255 logical features: false; 256: true), including multipart points and lines.

spatialSort orders the features along a Hilbert curve (the default) or a Morton curve (spatialSortCurve: 'morton', a prop of the three joins above, type SpatialSortCurve) before the BVH build. Results are identical; in paired runs the Hilbert joins were 10 to 15 percent faster (point-in-polygon, nearest, buffer selection). The prepared join and GPUZoneEvents have no curve prop and use Hilbert.

querySort orders queries the same way: each point chunk, or the bounds centers of line/polygon queries, is processed along a Hilbert curve over the root bounds of the feature BVH (non-finite points and empty bounds last, equal keys in row order), so neighboring invocations walk neighboring subtrees. Every query still reads and writes its own row, so all outputs are identical, including which candidate pairs a candidateCapacity overflow drops. It is a prop of GPUPointInPolygonJoin, GPUNearestFeatureJoin and GPUBufferSelection and defaults to true for point queries from 4096 queries and 256 features; line and polygon queries default to false because the measured sort cost exceeds the traversal saving. Lines default off: the measured 8192-line workload took 0.80 ms without sorting and 1.20 ms with sorting; explicit querySort: true remains available. Point sorting stays enabled; polygon defaults are unchanged. Line/polygon queries use the same source-row output addressing, including chunked scratch and foot-point outputs; polygon query topology is packed to keep every pass within eight storage bindings. Geometry sorting adds bounds and center passes. With a planar radius the second candidate pass keeps input order.

edgeSlabs (GPUPointInPolygonJoin) makes the exact refine test only the edges of the y-slab at the point's height in each polygon part, instead of every edge of the candidate feature: O(slab) per candidate rather than O(vertices). Results are identical (all boundary rules, the half-open owner, malformed offsets as UNCERTAIN); the one exception is a pair that the full walk reports UNCERTAIN only because an unrelated far edge defeats the exact orientation test (terms more than 200 binary orders apart). It defaults on when polygons.positions.length * candidateCapacity is at least 4,000,000 and features average 8 vertices. new GPUSpatialJoinPrepared({geometry, edgeSlabs: true}) builds the index once with the BVH, and joins given that handle reuse it. Slab counts are limited by GPU-computed total vertical edge travel and the entry budget, including ring closure. This prevents jagged long rings from overflowing the index and reverting to a full walk through indirect edge references.

GPUBufferSelection

Keep the observation and reference views distinct: points.positions contains rows to select; features.positions contains station or bus-stop rows. For example, an observation at (0, 0) is outside a 400 m buffer around a station at (1000, 0). Binding the observation view to both sides would create a self-match at zero distance. Size candidate capacity from the query view's row count. The Near Transit snippet uses observationPositions, stationPositions or busPositions, and separate transitStarts/transitEnds for line features.

Selects features within a distance of any reference feature, without building buffer polygons.

The compact output.matches.status.overflow reports final selection capacity loss. Optional output.matches.status.candidateOverflow reports join candidate loss separately. requiredCount counts matches reaching compaction and is a lower bound when candidates were lost.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.buffer-selection rung=result caption="Observations within 300 m of a library are selected; distance is straight-line, not walking."}

No warning is possible because the distance is a GPU view; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.

Selects points within a per-frame distance (inclusive) of point or segment features. A polyline is passed as consecutive segment rows. The contributor composes GPUNearestFeatureJoin. Unlike the join's unordered matches, it writes a source-aligned 0/1 mask and stable IDs in ascending row order (output.outputMask, output.matches), with a clamped count. output.matches.status.overflow reports final output capacity loss; optional output.matches.status.candidateOverflow reports join candidate loss separately. points is a GPUPointGeometryPort whose sourceIds become the match IDs. Optional output.nearestFeatureRows contains the original feature row before application-ID remapping; it replaces the misleading nearestFeatureIds spelling, which is rejected. Selection uses those rows, so a feature ID of 0xffffffff still selects a genuinely matched point. Feature sourceIds are ignored by this selection operation; query points.sourceIds still identify the selected query points in matches.ids. output.drawInstanceCount (one uint32 row, requires output.matches) receives the clamped selected count from the publish node, so it can be bound as an indirect draw instance count. The distance is planar in coordinate units by default; with spatialContext: {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} it is a great-circle distance in the context's length unit (meters by default; kilometers with units: 'kilometers'). The context is forwarded to GPUNearestFeatureJoin, whose section lists the edge model, sphere radius and rejected combinations; candidateCapacity is ignored. It measures distance, so local-lambert-equal-area frames throw. Optional output.candidateTruncated (one packed uint32 per point) and output.status.candidateOverflow pass the join's candidate status through: a flagged point may be left out although a feature lies within the distance (see Candidate overflow above).

graph.add(new GPUBufferSelection({
  points: {kind: 'points', positions: points},
  features: {kind: 'segments', starts: roadStarts, ends: roadEnds},
  distance: bufferDistance.importToGraph(graph), candidateCapacity: points.length * 4,
  spatialSort: true, output: {outputMask, matches}
}));
