Lines & trajectories

GPULineSegmentize, GPUGreatCircleArcs, GPULineSmooth, and GPULineChunk

GPULineSmooth: Optional source-row and source-chainage metadata follows the emitted vertices. Metadata rows are at most 1,073,741,823 and position addresses at most 2,147,483,647.

GPULineSegmentize — Densifies each polyline so no segment exceeds a maximum length, writing new vertices and source-line indices.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-segmentize rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: densified to segments of at most 25 m"}

GPUGreatCircleArcs — Tessellates a great-circle arc between each origin and destination pair into equal-angle vertices, writing one line per pair.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.great-circle-arcs rung=result caption="Grey: 40 destinations · blue: great-circle arcs from ORD, 32 segments each"}

GPULineSmooth — Smooths each polyline with Chaikin corner cutting, writing the refined vertices and source-line indices.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-smooth rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: two Chaikin rounds with ratio 0.25"}

GPULineChunk — Splits each polyline into pieces of a fixed length, writing the pieces with their source-line indices.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-chunk rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: 100 m chunks"}

Geometry-producing line contributors. Each writes a GPULineGeometryOutput: flat positions, clamped lineOffsets (deck.gl PathLayer startIndices), status (count, overflow, and optional requiredCount, approximation, vertexCount and requiredVertexCount), optional sourceLineIndices (output line to input line), sourceRows (output vertex to input segment row) and measures (distance along the source line). As in the generated-geometry layout (contributor contract rule 8), count and requiredCount count output lines and vertexCount and requiredVertexCount count vertices; vertexCount is the indirect draw count of positions. Breaking (2026-11-10): count and requiredCount counted vertices before, and status.lineCount is removed (it is count). A line truncated by the vertex capacity still counts as written. The vertex capacity is positions.length; offsets are clamped to it, so a truncated result is still a valid layout. Inputs are packed positions sorted by line with lineOffsets (lineCount + 1 rows). GPULineSegmentize, GPULineChunk and GPUGreatCircleArcs declare measure: 'distance'; Lambert equal-area local frames throw (use an azimuthal-equidistant frame for planar lines).

  • GPULineSegmentize (PostGIS ST_Segmentize, densify): every segment of length L becomes clamp(ceil(L / maximumSegmentLength), 1, maximumPieceCount) equal pieces; input vertices are kept. With compile-time closedLines: true (default false), each line is a ring that closes implicitly: the closing edge is densified and no vertex is repeated. A last row that repeats the first row bit-exactly is dropped; other repeated input vertices are preserved. Output keeps one line per input line, including empty and single-vertex lines, following the implicit polygon ring convention used by GPURectangleClip. The spherical closing edge follows the short way across the antimeridian using the last row's longitude shift. With a great-circle spatialContext, positions are longitude/latitude degrees: lengths are great-circle distances times the context's sphereRadius (default mean Earth radius, GPU_EARTH_MEAN_RADIUS_METERS; meters, or kilometers with units: 'kilometers'), interior points are slerped, and longitudes are unwrapped along each line so it stays continuous across the antimeridian. Breaking: the coordinateSystem and radius props of GPULineSegmentize and GPULineChunk (and radius of GPUGreatCircleArcs) are replaced by spatialContext; planar is the default. GPUGreatCircleArcs defaults to a great-circle longitude/latitude context and throws for planar ones; GPULineSmooth is planar only and throws for longitude/latitude.
  • status.approximation (optional, GPULineSegmentize and GPUGreatCircleArcs only; other contributors that write this layout reject a view for it) is set to 1 when an edge or arc needed more than maximumPieceCount (maximumSegmentCount) pieces for the requested maximum length, so some output segments are longer than requested; otherwise 0, rewritten on every encoding.
  • GPUGreatCircleArcs (turf greatCircle): one line of segments + 1 vertices per origin/destination pair, segments = clamp(max(ceil(distance / maximumSegmentLength), minimumSegments), 1, maximumSegmentCount). Endpoints are exact; longitudes are unwrapped from the source (Tokyo to San Francisco ends at 237.6), never split. Draw with PathLayer wrapLongitude: true or in a globe view. Near-antipodal interpolation can be numerically unstable in float32; split such arcs in the caller. There is no whole-arc rejection: counts and endpoints are emitted even when the shared interpolator returns NaN for interior vertices within 1e-6 radians of an antipode. Exactly antipodal endpoints do not determine a unique great circle.
  • GPULineSmooth (Chaikin corner cutting, turf polygonSmooth): iterationCount (compile time, 1 to 10 passes) each replace edge (a, b) with mix(a, b, r) and mix(a, b, 1 - r) for a per-frame ratio r (default 0.25). Open lines keep their endpoints (n vertices become n * 2^k); with closed: true a repeated closing vertex is ignored and each ring repeats its first vertex. Output sizes depend only on the layout, so every level's offsets come from two scans in closed form and each level is one kernel; levels ping-pong through two capacity-sized transients.
  • GPULineChunk: 'chunk' splits each line into pieces of chunkLength (turf lineChunk), the last piece ending at the line end; consecutive pieces share their boundary point. The piece count is data dependent: the line capacity is output.lineOffsets.length - 1, and status.count/sourceLineIndices report the pieces; requiredCount is the unclamped piece count, computed independently of output capacity and saturated at 0xffffffff when it reaches the uint32 limit. Per-line requirements use float32 division of the measured length by chunkLength. Requesting requiredCount in chunk mode adds a per-line count scan; layout offsets remain bounded even when the full requirement exceeds uint32. 'substring' extracts [startMeasure, endMeasure] from every line (turf lineSliceAlong, PostGIS ST_LineSubstring by distance); measures are clamped to the line and a start past the end gives an empty line. Zero-length lines are copied as one piece. Both modes support a great-circle spatialContext.
  • Everything per frame lives in the parameter buffer, so resolution, ratio, chunk length and ranges change with no recompile. All kernels are element-wise or sequential per line: deterministic, no float atomics.
  • Precision (f32, measured against f64 oracles on Apple Metal): planar densify and chunks within 2e-5 of the coordinate scale; spherical densify within 2.9 m, arcs up to 16,500 km within 5.7 m.

getGPULineSegmentizeParameterValues, getGPUGreatCircleArcsParameterValues, and getGPULineChunkParameterValues accept optional settings.spatialContext, the context of the lengths. A lon/lat context warns when a positive finite maximumSegmentLength or chunkLength is below ten times the float32 position resolution (about 16.97 meters on the default sphere): <ClassName> distance is too short for float32 lon/lat; use createGPULocalFrame. Pass the same context used by the contributor. This setting does not change the four-float packed layout; planar contexts and omitted contexts do not warn.

GPUGeometryMeasures, GPUGeodesicPairs, and GPUGeodesicDestination

The geodesic precision guide defines per-row status, conditioning exclusions, measured error envelopes and differences from the float64 CPU operation twins.

GPUGeometryMeasures — Measures length, area, centroid, bounding box and vertex count of line and polygon features, planar or on the ellipsoid.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.geometry-measures rung=result caption="Streets coloured by WGS84 geodesic length in metres, beside the 223 Wicker Park streets"}

GPUGeodesicPairs — Computes distance, initial and final bearing and midpoint for each origin and target pair of longitude/latitude points.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.geodesic-pairs rung=result caption="Grey: 40 ORD destinations · blue: route midpoints; distances are WGS84 Vincenty"}

GPUGeodesicDestination — Projects each origin along a bearing and distance, writing the destination point and final bearing.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.geodesic-destination rung=result caption="Grey: ORD · blue: destinations at 8 bearings and 500, 1,000 and 2,000 km (WGS84)"}

Measurement columns for line and polygon features and for point pairs, in planar coordinates or on the Earth (turf area, length, centroid, bbox, distance, bearing, destination, midpoint; PostGIS ST_Area, ST_Length, ST_Perimeter, ST_Centroid, ST_Envelope, ST_NPoints, ST_Distance(geography), ST_Azimuth, ST_Project).

GPUGeometryMeasures.spatialContext is optional (native planar by default). GPUGeodesicPairs and GPUGeodesicDestination require a longitude/latitude context and declare measure: 'distance'; geometry measures declare no measure because they output both lengths and areas. All three use resolveGPUSpatialContext and its shared validation errors.

graph.add(new GPUGeometryMeasures({
  geometryType: 'polygons',
  // GeoArrow four-level layout: feature -> polygons -> rings -> vertices
  positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets,
  // planar (native units), great-circle (spherical), or ellipsoidal (geodesic edges by default)
  spatialContext: {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'ellipsoidal', units: 'meters'},
  output: {
    areas, lengths, centroids, bounds, vertexCounts,
    status: {invalidCount} // geographic polygons with a pole or half-world edge, see below
  },
  groupIds, groupCount: 12,
  groupOutput: {areas: groupAreas, centroids: groupCentroids, featureCounts}
}));

const wgs84 = {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'ellipsoidal', units: 'meters'} as const;
graph.add(new GPUGeodesicPairs({origins, targets, spatialContext: wgs84,
  output: {distances, initialBearings, finalBearings, midpoints, status: {converged}}}));
graph.add(new GPUGeodesicDestination({origins, bearings, distances, spatialContext: wgs84,
  output: {destinations, finalBearings}}));
  • Features: one invocation per feature walks its rings in row order with Neumaier-compensated f32 sums, relative to the feature's first vertex, so projected coordinates far from the origin keep their precision (a 1 m square at x = 1e6 measures exactly 1). Rings close implicitly; a repeated closing vertex adds a zero edge. Outputs: length (lines) or perimeter (polygons), signed and absolute area, area-weighted (polygons) or length-weighted (lines) centroid falling back to the vertex mean, bounds, vertex count. Empty features give zeros and NaN centroid and bounds.
  • Input layout: geometryType selects the layout. 'polygons' takes the four-level layout of GPUPolygonGeometryFields (positions, featureOffsets, polygonOffsets, ringOffsets; rows are features). 'lines' takes positions and lineOffsets, plus optional featureOffsets (feature -> lines) for multi-lines. 'points' takes positions, plus optional featureOffsets (feature -> points) for multi-points; without it every point is a feature.
  • Ring roles are positional: the first ring of every polygon is a shell and later rings are holes whatever the winding, so a multi-polygon with mixed winding and a hole wound like its shell measure correctly, and areas is non-negative. GPUShapeDescriptors, GPUPolygonTriangulation and GPUAffineTransform (centroid origins) use the same layout and rule; GPULabelPoint uses even-odd, which agrees with it for valid (non-overlapping) polygons.
  • With metric: 'great-circle' and linear edges (the default), area uses Chamberlain-Duquette (turf parity): an approximation of degree-straight edges with second-order error in latitude span, exact for longitude/latitude boxes. With edgeModel: 'great-circle', area uses signed spherical excess on the context sphere (areaModel: 'sphere-polygon'), summed per edge as the Lambert chord term from the feature origin less the bulge of the great-circle arc over that chord, so a ring a hundred meters across keeps the same f32 relative precision (about 5e-7) as a continental one. Perimeter uses great-circle chords for both edge models; centroids retain the straight Lambert equal-area construction. For linear-edge area, pass sphereRadius: GPU_WGS84_SEMI_MAJOR_AXIS_METERS for turf area parity. Ellipsoidal with ellipsoidalEdgeModel: 'coordinate-linear' maps latitude to authalic latitude (exact equal-area ellipsoid mapping) and measures edges with an f32 Vincenty inverse.
  • Measure model. The instance's measures field (GPUMeasureDescription) says what the length and area outputs mean: lengthModel ('planar', 'great-circle', 'ellipsoidal-geodesic'), areaModel ('planar', 'sphere-equal-area', 'sphere-polygon', 'wgs84-authalic', 'geodesic') and the length and area units. Units follow getGPULengthUnits: native planar uses position units; a meters local frame reports meters and square meters. Geographic lengths default to meters and areas to square meters; units: 'kilometers' gives kilometers and square kilometers, including ellipsoidal kernels (previously rejected). Feature and group areas use the context's length unit squared.
  • Linear-edge spherical areas use getGPUAuthalicSphereRadius (areaRadius); great-circle-edge areas use getGPUSphereRadius. Lengths keep the mean radius (radius). With the default sphere, area results change by about 5e-7 relative. Ellipsoidal kernels honor spatialContext.ellipsoid (default WGS84); the geodesic classes expose the resolved ellipsoid in the context's length unit.
  • Antimeridian and poles (geographic polygons). Non-pole longitude steps are wrapped, so an edge with a longitude difference above 180 degrees (Fiji's 179 to -179) takes the short way and the ring is measured correctly. Same-pole edges retain their raw step, so explicitly capped Antarctica rings have zero winding and finite area. Unprepared rings with winding +-360, rings wider than 360 degrees, and ambiguous edges report NaN areas and centroids (lengths, bounds and vertex counts stay valid) and are counted in output.status.invalidCount, a one-row uint32 view cleared every encoding. Linear edges with a wrapped step of 180 degrees are ambiguous; great-circle edges are ambiguous when antipodal or passing through a pole. Prepare global rings with GPULonLatPolygonPreparation. Ellipsoidal contexts take ellipsoidalEdgeModel; declaring edgeModel throws. The check covers the serial and cooperative kernels. Line features are unaffected.
  • Groups: features are stably sorted by group ID and one workgroup per group reduces them with a fixed tree, so group sums are bitwise reproducible. Group centroids are area- (or length-) weighted means of feature centroids. Behavior change: in geographic contexts the mean is taken on 3D unit vectors (a spherical centroid), not on longitude and latitude numbers, so a group straddling the antimeridian lands on the antimeridian (for example 179 and -179 give 180) instead of at longitude 0. An antipodal group has no mean direction and reports NaN; a group containing an invalid feature reports NaN. Planar groups are unchanged.
  • 'geodesic' (pyproj.Geod.geometry_area_perimeter, geo GeodesicArea): lengths and perimeters are Vincenty geodesic lengths, as 'wgs84', and polygon areas follow geodesic edges on the context's ellipsoid (WGS84 by default). Edges of 20 km or more are sampled along the geodesic (spans of at most 250 km, at most 16) and summed as a chord plus the exact parabolic-segment bulge in local equal-area coordinates, so f32 never subtracts two huge areas. Within 1e-6 relative of pyproj on regional and continental polygons (perimeter 8e-6), where 'wgs84' straight edges are off by up to 30% on very long edges. Centroids still use straight equal-area edges, edges where Vincenty does not converge stay straight, and edges under 20 km ignore the bulge.
  • output.extremeVertices (uint32x4): per feature [argMinX, argMinY, argMaxX, argMaxY] as global positions rows, lowest row on ties, 0xffffffff for empty features. Geographic x is the unwrapped longitude of bounds. Per feature only (rejected in groupOutput).
  • geometryType: 'points': every feature is one point or a multi-point. Outputs are lengths (0), centroids (vertex mean, as Shapely MultiPoint.centroid), bounds, vertex counts and extremeVertices; areas is rejected. Group centroids weight by vertex count.
  • GPUGeodesicPairs: distance, initial and final bearing (degrees clockwise from north in (-180, 180]), and midpoint per pair. 'sphere' uses a Vincenty-form central angle that is well conditioned from millimeters to antipodal; 'wgs84' runs bounded Vincenty inverse iteration. See the precision guide for the iteration limits, conditioning exclusions, fallback status and CPU twin. luma.gl's GPUHaversineDistance computes the same sphere distance with a default radius of 6371 (kilometers); this class takes radius and unit from the spatial context (6371008.8 m by default) and takes longitude differences on the seam side, so short pairs across the antimeridian keep their precision in every model. Pair outputs, including status views, must not overlap one another or either input view; disjoint slices of one buffer are allowed and checked at construction.
  • GPUGeodesicDestination: destination and final bearing from origin, bearing and distance columns, on the sphere or by Vincenty's direct solution. No warning is possible because the distance is a GPU view; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.
  • model: 'rhumb' on both classes (turf rhumbDistance, rhumbBearing, rhumbDestination; geo Rhumb): a line of constant bearing on a sphere of radius (default 6371008.8 m). Pairs give distances (in radius units), initialBearings equal to finalBearings (constant, in (-180, 180]) and the rhumb midpoints (the midpoint by distance: the mean latitude, and the longitude of the loxodrome at that latitude, which is linear in the Mercator latitude and differs from the mean longitude unless the line runs east-west). Coincident pairs have zero distance and zero initial and final bearings. The destination longitude is continuous with the origin, and finalBearings flips to the opposite north-south sense when a move overshoots a pole and is reflected, as turf does (the reflected case is approximate). Longitude differences take the short way across the antimeridian, latitudes clamp at 89.9999 degrees, equal-latitude edges use the east-west limit cos(lat), and the Mercator-stretched latitude difference is an atanh of a cancellation-free ratio. Sphere only (turf and geo are spherical too), status.converged is 'wgs84'-only, and the model switch is compile-time. Distance error against an f64 closed form was 0.74 m (6e-8 relative) over 12,108 km and bearings were within 1e-4 degrees. Rhumb length in GPUGeometryMeasures and rhumb densify in GPULineSegmentize are not provided.
  • Geometry-measure precision (f32 vs f64 oracles, Apple Metal): WGS84 and spherical box areas within 3e-7 relative from 100 m blocks to 2,000 km boxes. Geodesic-pair and destination bounds and the distinction between nominal arithmetic measurements and GPU certification are in the precision guide. See Precision of longitude/latitude positions for input quantization.

GEODESIC_WGSL, createGPULocalFrame, and GPULocalFrameProjection

GPULocalFrameProjection — Projects longitude/latitude positions on the GPU into a local azimuthal-equidistant frame in meters.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.local-frame-projection rung=result caption="Blue: 223 Wicker Park streets projected from longitude/latitude to local metres"}

Two ways to work on longitude/latitude data, used consistently across the package:

  • Measure on the sphere. GEODESIC_WGSL is the public WGSL source of the spherical functions every great-circle path in this package uses; include it once in your own compute shader. Positions are vec2<f32>(longitude, latitude) degrees and distances are in the units of the radius argument (GPU_EARTH_MEAN_RADIUS_METERS for meters, the package default). geodesicHaversineDistance(a, b, radius) is the great-circle distance; geodesicSegmentFraction(point, start, end) and geodesicSegmentDistance(point, start, end, fraction, radius) give the point-to-segment distance for segments that are straight in longitude/latitude (the edge model of the containment tests and network edges; exact to first order for kilometer-length segments, densify longer ones). Projection and interpolation follow the same wrapped start-to-end longitude arc. The fraction helper compares its projected candidate with both endpoints, so its distance cannot exceed either endpoint distance. An interior projection survives a rounded distance tie; a tie between endpoint candidates selects the start. CPU line simplification uses the same wrapped arc and endpoint comparison as the GPU. Local GPU candidates are ordered by their common haversine terms before inverse trigonometry, so different asin/atan2 rounding cannot displace a local interior projection. Distant candidates keep central-angle comparisons to preserve resolution near antipodes. The distance helper evaluates the supplied fraction; it does not select a new fraction. Also available: geodesicHaversineTerm, geodesicCentralAngle, geodesicInitialBearingDegrees, geodesicDestination, geodesicInterpolate and geodesicWrapLongitudeDelta.
  • Project first. Planar operators throw on longitude/latitude contexts. createGPULocalFrame({origin, kind}) returns a binary64 CPU frame in meters: 'azimuthal-equidistant' (spherical, mean radius; distances and bearings from the origin exact) or 'lambert-equal-area' (WGS84 ellipsoidal, areas exact), matching PROJ aeqd +R=6371008.8 and laea +ellps=WGS84 to a millimeter. It offers project(lng, lat), unproject(x, y) and projectArray(lngLats, stride) for uploads, and a spatialContext that carries the frame identity: contexts of frames with different origins are incompatible, so data projected around different origins is not mixed. Float32 frame coordinates keep about 1e-7 of the frame extent (millimeters at 10 km). See Precision of longitude/latitude positions for float32 degrees. GPULocalFrameProjection projects GPU-resident float32x2 or binary64 (uint32x4) longitude/latitude rows into the frame without a readback (compiled adaptive patches from gpu-project, default tolerance 1 cm, output in absolute frame meters). Add it with graph.add(new GPULocalFrameProjection({frame, positions, output, bounds})). The frame's spatialContext is {coordinateSpace: 'planar', metric: 'native', units: 'meters', frame}. Choose 'azimuthal-equidistant' for distance operators and 'lambert-equal-area' for area/density operators; the wrong frame kind throws at operator construction. GPULocalFrameProjection.spatialContext is the frame's context.

GPULineSplit — Splits polylines at their mutual intersections, writing the pieces with their source-line indices.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-split rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: pieces split at street intersections"}

GPULineMerge — Merges polylines that meet end to end at degree-two nodes into longer chains.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-merge rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: chains merged through degree-two endpoints"}

Maximum (GPULineSplit): lines.positions has at most getGPUSegmentIntersectionMaximumVertexCount(device) (4194304 on a default device: the 32-byte segment-table row per vertex must fit one storage binding) rows, and the line count plus four times intersectionCapacity is at most getGPULineSplitMaximumPieceBound(device) (4194304 on a default device).

GPULineSplit is planar only: spatialContext defaults to native planar coordinates and a longitude-latitude context throws; project to a planar frame first. GPULineMerge also accepts a longitude-latitude spatialContext: lines join only where end points have identical coordinates (bit patterns), nothing is measured, and longitude 180 and -180 are different coordinates.

GPULineSplit splits linestrings at every crossing, touch, shared vertex and overlap end (turf lineSplit against the union of the lines; verified against Shapely unary_union). It reuses GPUSegmentIntersection in self mode, groups the split events by segment with a stable GPUSort, removes duplicates and writes GeoArrow-layout pieces. Props: lines ({kind: 'lines', positions, lineOffsets}), intersectionCapacity, output, optional spatialSort and leafCapacity. output is {pieces, status?: {uncertainCount?}}; pieces is a GPUGeneratedGeometryPort: geometry holds {kind: 'lines', positions, lineOffsets}, sourceIds identifies the source line, and status holds count (pieces, not vertices), overflow, optional candidateOverflow, optional requiredCount and optional vertexCount and requiredVertexCount, which report the flattened vertex cardinality. Pieces are ordered by source line, then traversal order. Splits at line ends are ignored, and a line with fewer than two vertices yields no piece. Piece or vertex truncation sets status.overflow; an insufficient intersectionCapacity independently sets status.candidateOverflow.

GPULineMerge is the inverse (Shapely linemerge, turf lineMerge): it joins linestrings at endpoints shared by exactly two line ends (an exact f32 match) into maximal chains. Junctions of three or more ends stop a chain, a closed line never joins itself, and lines with fewer than two vertices are ignored. With directed: true (Shapely line_merge(directed=True)) only a line end meeting a line start joins, chains follow the input direction and lineReversed is all 0. Props: positions, lineOffsets and output: {chainOffsets, positions, status: {count, requiredCount?, overflow?, vertexCount?, requiredVertexCount?}, sourceIds?, lineChains?, lineOrders?, lineReversed?}; status.count counts chains. The capacities always suffice, so requiredCount equals count and overflow is 0. sourceIds is the source row of each chain, its first line; lineChains and lineOrders list every member. The list-ranking rounds are the shared helper in line-merge/list-ranking.ts, which GPUSharedPaths also uses. The result is deterministic: chains are ordered by their lowest line, paths start at the lower endpoint ID and cycles start at their lowest line and are emitted closed. Chains are ranked by pointer jumping (list ranking), so a chain of n lines takes O(log n) dispatches rather than n serial steps. Merging with a tolerance is not provided.

GPULineSimplification

Simplifies polylines with Visvalingam or Douglas-Peucker importance, keeping vertices above a metre tolerance.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.line-simplification rung=result caption="Grey: 223 Wicker Park streets · blue: simplified with a 5 m tolerance"}

Pass optional spatialContext to getGPULineSimplificationParameterValues(settings) to warn on short Douglas-Peucker tolerance values; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.

spatialContext defaults to native planar coordinates, with the tolerance in position units. {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} (optional sphereRadius, default mean Earth radius) runs Douglas-Peucker (both metrics) with great-circle distances: tolerance and importance use the context's length unit (meters by default, now also kilometers with units: 'kilometers'), chords are straight in longitude/latitude, and the distance from a vertex to its chord is the haversine distance to the closest point of the chord (found in the local equirectangular frame of the vertex, exact to first order for chords of kilometers). The 'time-ratio' expected position is interpolated linearly in longitude/latitude with the longitude difference wrapped. Great-circle distances use f32 transcendental functions, so the bit-exactness below holds for planar contexts only. method: 'visvalingam' is planar only and throws in a great-circle context (its tolerance is an area, its rounds mirror a bit-exact planar triangle area, and no spherical-excess variant exists); other metrics throw. For Visvalingam, or to keep planar bit-exactness, project first (createGPULocalFrame, GPULocalFrameProjection). Douglas-Peucker declares measure: 'distance' (a Lambert equal-area frame throws; use azimuthal-equidistant); Visvalingam declares measure: 'area' (an azimuthal-equidistant frame throws; use Lambert equal-area).

Douglas-Peucker (and approximate Visvalingam-Whyatt) simplification of many polylines or tracks, stored as rows sorted by line and delimited by lineOffsets. It runs in two parts: a per-vertex importance column computed once, and a per-frame selection at a tolerance from a parameter buffer. Changing the tolerance re-runs only the mask, compaction, and publish nodes.

// One-shot graph: importance.
importanceGraph.add(new GPULineSimplification({
  positions, lineOffsets, importance,
  metric: 'segment', // or 'time-ratio' (TD-TR) with float32 `timestamps`
  maximumIterations: 64,
  output: {status: {converged, iterationCount}}
}));

// Per-frame graph: selection only.
frameGraph.add(new GPULineSimplification({
  positions, lineOffsets, importance, computeImportance: false,
  parameters: toleranceParameters.importToGraph(frameGraph),
  output: {ids, status: {count, overflow}, outputMask, lineCounts, lineStarts}
}));
toleranceParameters.write(getGPULineSimplificationParameterValues({tolerance: 2})); // no recompile
  • Importance: every line starts as one open interval between its endpoints (importance +Infinity). Each round, every open interval picks its farthest row (segmented argmax with ties to the smallest row) and splits there. The row's importance is min(distance, min(importance[a], importance[b])), so importance never increases from a split to its children. An interval whose largest distance is 0 is resolved at once with importance 0.
  • Rounds are GPU gated. They stop when no row is left (status.converged = 1) or after maximumIterations. The rounds are unrolled, so the command node count grows with maximumIterations: estimatedCommandNodeCount (exact for the importance rounds, excluding the GPUCompaction nodes of a row selection) is checked at construction against the shared 32,768-node budget (validateCommandNodeBudget); the largest accepted maximumIterations (1024) needs about five thousand nodes, so the budget is never the limit. The CPU twin also honors an explicitly supplied iteration limit and reports converged = 0 when work remains, retaining the current importance. Unresolved Visvalingam rows have infinite importance; unresolved Douglas-Peucker rows retain their parent split bound. Omitting the CPU limit leaves its sequential computation uncapped; CPU Visvalingam counts sequential removals. A balanced split tree takes about log2(n) rounds and a spiral can take up to n - 2. If the cap is reached, the remaining rows get their interval's split importance, so the kept set is a superset of Douglas-Peucker's.
  • Selection: a row is kept when importance > tolerance. output.ids holds the kept rows in ascending order, and status.count is clamped so it can drive an indirect draw. outputMask is the per-row 0/1 mask. lineCounts (not clamped) and lineStarts give each line's range inside output.ids.
  • Exactness: with status.converged = 1, the kept set at any tolerance e equals classic recursive Douglas-Peucker at e (split when dmax > e, first maximum on ties) under the same metric. The f32 distances are bit-identical to a Math.fround CPU oracle: products are prevented from fusing into FMAs, and division and square root are corrected to defined values.
  • Metrics: 'segment' measures the distance to the chord segment, which is the perpendicular distance inside the segment and the distance to the nearer anchor outside it (GEOS and simplify-js behave the same way). 'time-ratio' is TD-TR's synchronized Euclidean distance, which needs float32 timestamps.
  • Use tile- or view-local coordinates. Simplified lines may self-intersect, because topology is not preserved (Douglas-Peucker and Visvalingam alike).

Visvalingam-Whyatt. method: 'visvalingam' writes each vertex's effective triangle area (the context's length unit squared; square meters in a default local frame) into importance, and the per-frame tolerance is then an area: a vertex is kept when importance > tolerance, and endpoints are always kept. The selection outputs are the same as for Douglas-Peucker. Each round removes every surviving vertex whose area is the smallest within neighborhoodRadius steps both ways (1 to 8, default 3, compile-time), then unlinks them; the effective area is the maximum of its own area and those of its removed neighbors, which is the sequential running maximum. It is approximate: the sequential heap order cannot be parallelised exactly. Against simplification.simplify_coords_vw on 3000-vertex random walks the kept set differed by 3.1, 0.65 and 0.13 percent of vertices at radius 1, 2 and 3, and lines up to 300 vertices matched exactly at radius 3. It needs about 31 rounds for 300 vertices and 55 for 3000; maximumIterations (default 256) caps them, and undecided rows get +Infinity (a superset). Not supported: metric: 'time-ratio' and topology-preserving VW (simplify_coords_vwp).

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.coverage-simplification rung=result caption="Grey: 17 community areas, 3,201 vertices · blue: 397 after 50 m simplification, borders still shared"}

Gap-free simplification of a polygon coverage (Shapely simplify_coverage, PostGIS ST_CoverageSimplify). Each iteration also tests every kept vertex against the area between each span's original chain and its shortcut (exact point-in-ring signs, candidates from one BVH over the input segments): a shortcut that passes over an island crosses nothing but would move the island into the neighboring polygon, so such a span is repaired the same way. output.topologyStatistics (4 uint32, length GPU_COVERAGE_SIMPLIFICATION_TOPOLOGY_STATISTICS_LENGTH) reports the crossings and swept spans before repair, those remaining, the vertices restored and a candidate-pair overflow flag; topologyPairCapacity (default max(256, 4 * vertices)) sizes the candidate list. Residual crossings are reported, never hidden, and input that already crosses itself cannot be repaired (it counts in both numbers). On a wavy-strip scene the repaired output passes Shapely coverage_is_valid, where plain Douglas-Peucker gave three invalid polygons. The criterion is distance, not area (Shapely's simplify_coverage is area based and keeps a different vertex set), and there is no guarantee of zero residual within the iteration count.

simplifyBoundary: false (Shapely coverage_simplify(simplify_boundary=False)) keeps every vertex that touches an unshared edge and simplifies only the shared arcs.

One thread walks each arc, so one huge arc is a serial walk (and a long dropped span costs a serial walk per kept vertex), and non-finite vertices are not supported. A shared arc that crosses a ring start keeps one extra vertex.

The maximumGap rule

GPUTrajectoryPlayhead, GPUTrajectoryResample with clock or time spacing, addClockEncounters, GPUTrajectoryClosestApproach, GPUZoneEvents, GPUTrajectoryKalman and GPUTrajectoryRestriction use one rule. maximumGap is in the unit of the timestamps (word timestamps: the integer unit, and context.unit when a temporalContext is declared). A step between two consecutive samples is a gap only when it is strictly longer than the limit; a step exactly equal to the limit is still interpolated. The samples at both ends of a gap are real fixes and stay present, so only times strictly inside the gap are affected. Omitted (views) or 0 (everywhere) means no limit. Arc-length resampling takes no limit: its samples are spaced along the path, and a straight line across a gap is still path length.

Trajectory port and sort check

These contributors share the GPUTrajectoryGeometryPort layout, including Kalman and restriction, which also keep their times and offsets names. The optional temporalContext must match the rows: float32 is float32-relative (double-single with timestampsLow), uint32x2 is int64-words, and the axis is {kind: 'irregular'}; a mismatch throws at construction.

addTrajectorySortCheck(graph, {positions, timestamps, trackOffsets, output: {status: {invalidCount}}}) is an optional check of the sorted-by-track-then-time precondition, which nothing else validates. It writes one uint32 row, the number of rows out of order, and costs nothing unless called. Each non-finite float timestamp counts once, including a track's first or only row. For double-single timestamps, either non-finite component makes the row invalid. A finite row after an invalid row starts a new ordered run and is not counted again for that predecessor. Int64 timestamps retain exact word comparisons. A reversed offset range counts the rows in that range. status.invalidCount must contain at least one row; an empty view throws before any node is added.

output.events.eventPositions (float32x2) holds the interpolated crossing position of each event. output.visitTable is a sparse (track, zone) table that replaces the dense matrices for large trackCount * zoneCount: rows are ordered by track, then zone, as a GPUCompactOutput (ids is the track, plus status.count, status.overflow and status.requiredCount) extended with the columns zones, visits, dwellTimes, firstEnterTimes and lastExitTimes (see GPUZoneVisitTableOutput). addFleetDwellZoneEventsRecipe forwards both outputs. Times are relative to the track start, a track that starts inside has first enter 0, and an open visit has last exit equal to the track duration. The table is built from the dense per-cell state, so it bounds the output, not the working memory.

Optional visitTable.status.candidateOverflow reports candidate-scratch loss affecting the dense visits, dwell and table. It does not include trackEventCapacity truncation: that cap only bounds the displayed event list and leaves visits/dwell intact. Encounter and closest-approach compact pair ports likewise propagate their hit/grid/entry loss through pairs.status.candidateOverflow. The fleet-dwell recipe preserves caller event status views; its zone-events stage uses the compact event candidate flag (scratch or track loss), while its separate scratch diagnostic continues to identify scratch loss alone.

Limits: the scratch of candidateCapacity rows is sorted (three stable GPUSort passes) every encoding; the walk and dwell kernels use one invocation per track; the dense matrices cost trackCount * zoneCount rows; samples exactly on an edge follow the half-open rule and can disagree with other predicates by one event; zones with overlapping rings are not supported (even-odd); at equal timestamps the crossing position is not unique.

Status: the optional output.status takes four one-row uint32 views, candidateCount, candidateOverflow, trackOverflow and eventOverflow.

  • candidateCount is the unclamped number of segment-edge bounding-box candidates, including one ray query per track. Set candidateCapacity to at least this for exact results.
  • candidateOverflow means the scratch was too small, so events may be missing.
  • trackOverflow means a track has more than trackEventCapacity events. Its list is truncated, but dwell and visits stay exact.
  • eventOverflow means there are more events than output.events.ids.length. output.events.status.requiredCount holds the size needed.

output.events.status.overflow reports eventOverflow only; optional output.events.status.candidateOverflow reports the OR of candidate and track overflow.

An event-list readout is complete only when both event status flags are zero. Dwell totals, visits and unclamped per-track counts instead require the scratch-only output.status.candidateOverflow to be zero: an event-list cap alone does not invalidate them. The standalone and fleet-dwell lenses capture these as eventOverflow, eventCandidateOverflow and candidateScratchOverflow, with the two loss domains kept separate. The website qualifies retained crossings as incomplete on either event-list flag, and qualifies dwell only on scratch loss; pending status readbacks never certify completeness. The standalone lens example accepts candidateCapacity (default 131072 scratch rows) independently of trackEventCapacity (the maximum retained events per track).

GPUTrajectoryEncounters

Candidate capacities reserve a strictly higher saturated-count sentinel; device bindings usually impose much lower limits.

Finds pairs of tracks within a distance of each other in the same time bucket of resampled tracks.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.trajectory-encounters rung=result caption="GPU pair table: 8 track pairs within 200 m at the same clock, smallest distance in metres"}

Pairs of tracks within distance in the same time bucket (MobilityDB tdwithin on resampled tracks).

Construction warns when the host-known maximumDistance derived from cellSize is too short for float32 lon/lat; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions for this check and the unchecked per-frame distance GPU view.

Maximum: columns * rows * bucketCount of the lattice is at most getGPUGridIndexMaximumCellCount(device) (2^25 - 1 on a default device); raise cellSize or lower bucketCount.

  • Inputs: dense samples ([trackCount * bucketCount] float32x2, row track * bucketCount + bucket, the GPUTrajectoryResample.samples layout), trackCount, bucketCount, optional mask, per-frame distance (one float row, clamped to cellSize), compile-time cellSize and bounds [minX, minY, maxX, maxY], hitCapacity and optional bucketTimes.
  • Outputs, all in output: output.pairs (a GPUCompactOutput, ids is the lower track, extended with the optional columns partners, firstBuckets, minimumDistances, bucketCounts and firstTimes (needs bucketTimes)) and the required output.status.candidateOverflow. Pairs have track < partner, are deduplicated across buckets and sorted by (track, partner); the sentinel is 0xffffffff (IDs, buckets) and 0 after status.count. output.pairs.status.overflow reports only pair-capacity truncation; output.status.candidateOverflow reports hit-scratch or grid incompleteness.
  • It uses a 3D GPUGridIndex (x, y and the bucket as an exact third axis) with a 3x3 scan per sample, three stable sorts of the hits and GPUCompaction for the runs.
  • Great-circle: with spatialContext: {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} (optional sphereRadius) the samples are (longitude, latitude) degrees, distance and the reported minimumDistances are in the context's length unit (meters by default; kilometers with units: 'kilometers'), and cellSize and bounds stay in degrees. The default sphere radius is the mean Earth radius in that unit. It measures distance, so local-lambert-equal-area frames throw. The test is the great-circle distance of the two samples of one bucket, so there is no tangent-plane approximation. Pruning is conservative: two points within angle theta differ by at most theta in latitude and by at most asin(sin(theta) / cos(L)) in longitude, where L is the largest |latitude| of bounds. The lattice therefore searches up to maximumDistance = radius * asin(sin(cellSize) * cos(L)) (times 1 - 1e-6), exposed as encounters.maximumDistance, and distance is clamped to it. Verified against pyproj: walking every azimuth at that distance from latitude ±L stays within one cell. Pick latitude bounds that exclude the polar caps (the reach shrinks to 0 as L approaches 90). Bounds with longitudes exactly [-180, 180] wrap the lattice across the antimeridian (sample longitudes are folded into that range first and cellSize must be below 120 degrees); other longitude bounds do not wrap, and cellSize must be below 90. Latitudes must lie in (-90, 90). Planar contexts keep maximumDistance = cellSize. addClockEncounters forwards spatialContext to the resample and the encounters.
  • It is discrete in time: the samples must share a clock (column k is one instant for all tracks), and encounters between buckets are missed. With GPUTrajectoryResample that holds only when the tracks cover the same window.
graph.add(new GPUTrajectoryEncounters({
  samples, trackCount, bucketCount, distance, cellSize: 50, bounds: [0, 0, 1000, 1000],
  hitCapacity: 1 << 16,
  output: {
    pairs: {ids, status: {count, overflow}, partners, firstBuckets, minimumDistances},
    status: {candidateOverflow}
  }
}));

addClockEncounters(graph, props) builds the shared-clock chain in one call: it resamples all tracks onto one clock (GPUTrajectoryResample with spacing: 'clock', a per-frame [start, step] buffer) and feeds GPUTrajectoryEncounters, so tracks need not cover the same window. It takes the trajectory layout (positions, timestamps, trackOffsets) and the encounter options as flat props. Optional output.bucketTimes and output.samples expose the clock and the resampled tracks (AddClockEncountersProps, ClockEncounters). The optional per-frame maximumGap view is forwarded to the clock resample, so a bucket inside an over-long track interval reads that track as absent and produces no encounter.

Clock mode compares positions only at the shared bucket instants, so two tracks that pass each other between buckets are missed. Treat it as a fast preview and use GPUTrajectoryClosestApproach for the exact answer.

GPUTrajectoryClosestApproach

Candidate capacities reserve a strictly higher saturated-count sentinel; device bindings usually impose much lower limits.

Finds pairs of tracks within a distance of each other at the same instant, with closest-approach distance, time and duration.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.trajectory-closest-approach rung=result caption="GPU pair table: 10 track pairs passing within 200 m, closest approach and time inside"}

Exact encounters between trajectories on their raw samples, the MobilityDB tdwithin and nearestApproachDistance question: tracks move linearly between samples, and two tracks are an encounter while their positions at the same instant are at most distance apart. Each sample row becomes a segment to the next sample of its track with a space-time box (x and y padded by half the distance), boxes are binned into a hashed grid of cellSize by cellSize by timeCellSize cells and sorted, and every pair of segments of different tracks that shares a cell is tested once (in the cell holding the minimum corner of the two boxes' intersection). The test solves the closest point of approach over the common time interval: the separation is D + V tau, its minimum is at tau = -(D . V) / |V|^2, and the time within the distance lies between the roots of |D + V tau|^2 = distance^2. With the optional per-frame maximumGap view (one float32 row, the timestamps' unit) a segment longer than the limit is not motion: it adds no hits and no cell entries, so a long gap can no longer raise entryOverflow.

  • Inputs: the trajectory layout (positions, timestamps as float32 values or uint32x2 Int64 words in the declared timestamp unit, trackOffsets), optional mask, a per-frame one-row distance, and the compile-time cellSize, timeCellSize, entryCapacity (cell entries) and hitCapacity (segment-pair hits). Planar only; project longitude/latitude first with createGPULocalFrame. timeCellSize uses the declared timestamp unit, so rescale it with timestamps when changing units.
  • Outputs, in output: pairs, a GPUCompactOutput (ids is the lower track) in (track, partner) order with optional partners, minimumDistances, closestTimes, firstTimes and durations, all times in the declared timestamp unit relative to the first sample of the lower track, with durations in that same unit; and status.entryOverflow and status.hitOverflow for the two scratch capacities (a segment covering more than 4096 cells also sets entryOverflow).
  • Word timestamps stay exact: times are measured from a segment's own start row before the f32 conversion. Verified against test/oracles/trajectory-closest-approach.py, a float64 brute force over every segment pair, self-checked by dense sampling and against shapely path distances.

  cellSize: 50, timeCellSize: 60, entryCapacity: 1 << 20, hitCapacity: 1 << 18,
  output: {
    pairs: {ids, status: {count, overflow}, partners, minimumDistances, closestTimes, firstTimes, durations},
    status: {entryOverflow, hitOverflow}
  }
}));

GPUTrackSimilarity

invalidPair also reports invalid track ranges and densification overflow; an invalid pair must not be interpreted as a valid similarity.

Measures Hausdorff and discrete Frechet distance between pairs of tracks or rings, with optional maximum distance and dynamic time warping cost.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.track-similarity rung=result caption="All 66 track pairs: Hausdorff and Fréchet distance in metres"}

Hausdorff (symmetric, vertex-to-segment as GEOS computes it, unbounded length) and discrete Frechet (vertex-based) distances for given pairs of tracks or rings (hausdorff_distance(a, b, densify), frechet_distance(a, b, densify)). Behavior change: Hausdorff used to be vertex-to-vertex, which agreed with Shapely only by coincidence; it now measures from the (densified) vertices to the other polyline's segments, so values can be slightly smaller than before. Inputs are linesA (a tagged line geometry: kind: 'lines', positions and lineOffsets; optionally linesB, by default the same set), pairA, pairB (uint32 views) and an optional GPU-written activePairCount. Outputs, all in output, are hausdorff, frechet, optional maximumDistance (largest vertex-to-vertex distance per pair, Sedona ST_MaxDistance, original vertices) and pairStatus (bits of GPU_TRACK_SIMILARITY_STATUS: emptyTrack 1, frechetCapExceeded 2, invalidPair 4). Skipped pairs get NaN. densify (0 or in [1/4096, 1], compile-time) splits each segment into round(1/densify) parts, as Shapely does; the virtual vertices are computed on the fly. Frechet sweeps the longer track in strips of 256 lanes, so only the shorter (densified) track is capped: maximumFrechetVertexCount is 1 to 2048 (default 256). A pair over the cap reports frechetCapExceeded with a NaN Frechet while its Hausdorff is still computed. Polygon boundaries are passed as closed vertex lists. Against Shapely 2.1.2 (GEOS 3.13.1) all three measures were within 2e-4 relative on line and polygon-boundary pairs, including a 700 by 300 Frechet over three strips. Rings are open vertex sequences, so Frechet is sensitive to the start and the orientation. Pairs can come straight from GPUTrajectoryEncounters (output.pairs.ids, output.pairs.partners and output.pairs.status.count).

For frechetEpsilon, no warning is possible because the distance is a GPU view; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.

Maximum: the pair capacity pairA.length is at most GPU_TRACK_SIMILARITY_MAXIMUM_PAIR_COUNT (2^24 - 1).

spatialContext defaults to native planar (Euclidean distances in the f32 input coordinates; the planar kernels compile no geodesic code). {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle'} makes Hausdorff, Frechet and maximumDistance great-circle distances in the context's length unit (meters by default; kilometers with units: 'kilometers') on a sphere of sphereRadius (default the mean Earth radius in that unit). Other geographic metrics throw. It measures distance, so local-lambert-equal-area frames throw. Vertices are [longitude, latitude] degrees and segments are straight in longitude/latitude, as everywhere in this package. The vertex-to-segment distance is the haversine distance to the closest point of the segment, with that point found in the local equirectangular frame of the vertex (geodesicSegmentFraction, geodesicSegmentDistance): exact to first order for segments of kilometers, so densify long segments. Densified points interpolate longitude and latitude linearly with the longitude difference wrapped to +-180, so tracks may cross the antimeridian. Against numpy haversine with a 20000-part segment densification (cross-checked with pyproj.Geod(a=6371008.8, f=0)) the f32 results are expected within 2e-3 relative; the spec covers a mid-latitude, a 66 degree and an antimeridian pair.

graph.add(new GPUTrackSimilarity({
  linesA: {kind: 'lines', positions, lineOffsets},
  pairA: pairs.ids, pairB: pairs.partners, activePairCount: pairs.status.count,
  maximumFrechetVertexCount: 256, densify: 0.25,
  output: {hausdorff, frechet, maximumDistance, pairStatus}
}));

Two more measures share the Frechet layout and its shorter-track cap. output.frechetWithin (uint32, with the per-frame one-row frechetEpsilon in the context's length unit) is the Frechet decision, 1 when the discrete Frechet distance is at most epsilon: endpoint distances reject pairs before scanning their vertices; a fixed monotone coupling accepts pairs in O(n + m). Undecided planar pairs also use coordinate-extrema bounding-box lower bounds. The rest run the boolean free-space program, evaluating distances only for cells with reachable predecessors; it stops once no monotone path can survive a row or two consecutive anti-diagonals. Use it to filter candidate pairs at a threshold without requesting the full distance or DTW, which still compute when requested alongside the decision. track-similarity-dtw-frechet-ab.spec.ts times decision-only and distance-only graphs separately. output.dynamicTimeWarping is the dynamic time warping cost, the smallest sum of point distances in the context's length unit over monotone warping paths (similaritymeasures.dtw; not the square root of summed squares of tslearn and dtaidistance), with an optional compile-time Sakoe-Chiba band dynamicTimeWarpingBandRadius equal to tslearn.metrics.sakoe_chiba_mask(n, m, radius); cells outside the band are never evaluated. Both are verified against test/oracles/track-similarity-dtw-frechet.py (similaritymeasures, shapely, tslearn), planar and great-circle.

  linesA: {kind: 'lines', positions, lineOffsets},
  pairA: pairs.ids, pairB: pairs.partners, activePairCount: pairs.status.count,
  frechetEpsilon, dynamicTimeWarpingBandRadius: 8,
  output: {frechetWithin, dynamicTimeWarping}
}));
