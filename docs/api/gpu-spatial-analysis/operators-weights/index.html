Spatial weights

A GPUSpatialWeights is the sparse W that spatial statistics are built on, in CSR form: row i lists the neighbors j of focus row i and the weight w_ij of each. Producers write it on the GPU, transforms rewrite it, and the statistics below read it, so one statistic works with any neighborhood definition. Applications can also upload weights built elsewhere.

Every producer guarantees: offsets holds rows + 1 non-decreasing entries starting at 0; row i occupies slots [offsets[i], offsets[i + 1]); neighbor IDs are strictly ascending within a row; a self join never lists a row as its own neighbor (w_ii = 0); weights are finite and non-negative. Capacity overflow clamps offsets to neighbors.length and sets an overflow flag on the GPU.

A GPUSpatialWeights is a first-class object, not only buffers. Every producer in this section (GPUContiguityWeights, GPULatticeWeights, GPUSpatialWeightsTransform, GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra, GPUSpatialWeightsTranspose) exposes outputWeights: the caller-owned views with information (rowCount, transform state 'raw' | 'binary' | 'row' | 'double' | 'variance' | 'kernel' | 'symmetric' | 'unknown', symmetric as true | false | 'unknown' only where guaranteed, hasSelfWeights, provenance with source, criterion, k, radius and order) and status (overflow and, where the producer computes it exactly, requiredCount) attached. Pass outputWeights to consumers: statistics read information.transform (a 'row' W is re-standardized over the neighbors that remain after masking) and gate on both loss flags. Uploaded weights may omit information, which reads as 'unknown'. validateGPUSpatialWeights checks on the CPU what it can: view formats and lengths, information.rowCount, the declared transform and symmetry, and the status formats.

The optional spatialContext describes the coordinates and metric used to build the weights; distances and information.provenance.radius use its length unit. GPUNeighborSearch sets it; GPUNearestFeatureWeights sets it from the producing join's context passed to the adapter. Transforms, transpose and algebra propagate it; algebra checks declared input contexts with assertCompatibleGPUSpatialContexts. Topology-only producers and uploaded weights may omit it.

For weighted statistics, a slot is an active neighbor when its weight is positive, its neighbor ID is below the row count and it is not the row itself. A zero-weight slot is listed but is not a neighbor. An island is a row with no active slot, whatever slots it lists.

GPUSpatialWeightsMinimumSpanningTree uses topology instead: every listed slot is a link, including reciprocal zero-weight slots. It ignores the weight value and uses attribute dissimilarity as edge cost. SKATER and REDCAP forest compositions keep that topology-only rule; zero-weight links must be removed from the CSR if they should not connect forest components.

Uploaded CSR must satisfy the invariants above; consumers do not re-check them (a binary search over unsorted neighbors silently gives wrong S1, S2 and symmetry counts). Run GPUSpatialWeightsValidation once after upload, and sort, merge duplicates and drop self slots on the CPU if it reports violations.

Capacity grow loop: GPUContiguityWeights, GPULatticeWeights and GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra write into one caller-owned output: GPUSpatialWeightsOutput, the CSR views plus status: {overflow, requiredCount?, candidateOverflow?} (overflow required). Read overflow and requiredCount after a frame; when requiredCount exceeds the capacity, allocate getGPUSpatialWeightsCapacityPlan({rowCount, capacity, requiredCount}).capacity slots (the required count plus 25 percent headroom, GPU_SPATIAL_WEIGHTS_CAPACITY_HEADROOM) and rebuild. The plan's estimatedOutputBytes is the bytes of the CSR views at that capacity (GPU_SPATIAL_WEIGHTS_SLOT_BYTE_LENGTH per slot, plus GPU_SPATIAL_WEIGHTS_DISTANCE_BYTE_LENGTH with distances, plus the offsets). The contiguity and lattice producers also expose getCapacityPlan(observation), which adds their own transient bytes (from the same constants their getCommandNodes allocates with; the device-dependent gpgpu sort and scan scratch is not included) and, for contiguity, the intermediate pair stage. Rows past the point of truncation are empty, so never use weights whose overflow or candidateOverflow is nonzero.

Keep the complete producer port (for example, contiguity.outputWeights) when wiring weights into a minimum spanning forest, SKATER, REDCAP or Ward. These consumers accept supplied loss words even when both are zero, read their contents on the GPU on every submission, and suppress partitions on either loss cause. Forest component labels and region labels become 0xffffffff; forest edge counts and SKATER/REDCAP region counts become zero/unavailable. Forest compact edge status reports either upstream cause as candidateOverflow, while its overflow describes final edge capacity only. SKATER cut gains become NaN. Clearing the flags restores ordinary results without rebuilding the graph. See the regionalization contract for the operation and CPU-twin ports. Forest status is available without compact edges: its packed candidateOverflow word reports either upstream cause, separately from the attribute invalidCount word. Forest and SKATER lens captures retain both producer loss flags and refuse a record if either is nonzero. Forest figures size their edge key spaces and segment captures from the published compact count, including disconnected graphs, rather than assuming rowCount - 1 edges.

GPUContiguityWeights

Join Chicago tract polygons that share a vertex (queen) or an edge (rook) into binary neighbour weights.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.contiguity-weights rung=result caption="Queen links join 791 Chicago tracts that share a vertex; tracts with no link are islands."}

Polygon contiguity as binary weights. criterion: 'queen' joins features that share at least one vertex; 'rook' joins features that share a ring edge (both endpoints, either direction). Input is polygons: GPUPolygonGeometryPort (the four-level layout positions, ringOffsets, polygonOffsets, featureOffsets), with one output row per FEATURE: every ring of every part counts, holes included, so a multipart feature is the neighbor of anything any of its parts touches, and parts of one feature are never self neighbors. This is the row space of GPUZonalStatistics and the points-in-polygons recipe, so points -> counts -> W -> Moran chain on one feature table without a cast.

  • snapTolerance (default 0, exact f32 equality) snaps vertices with floor((x - origin) / tolerance + 0.5) per axis (from 2^23 steps the float32 quotient is an integer and is used as it is), where the origin is the first vertex of the data, so the grid depends on the extent of the data and not on its distance from the coordinate origin. It is the snap key of GPUCoverageDissolve and GPUCoverageSimplification. This is a grid snap, not a distance test: two vertices closer than the tolerance can fall in different cells. The extent divided by the tolerance must stay at or below GPU_CONTIGUITY_WEIGHTS_MAXIMUM_SNAP_CELLS (2^24); pass coordinateExtent to have that checked at construction.
  • Exact 64-bit vertex keys, stable radix sorts and run detection; no hashing, so no collisions, and the output is deterministic and symmetric.
  • A point shared by m features emits m^2 candidate pairs, bounded by pairCapacity (default GPU_CONTIGUITY_WEIGHTS_PAIR_CAPACITY_FACTOR = four times the neighbor capacity); exceeding it sets candidateOverflow only. Overflow reports final neighbor-slot loss only. The producer's outputWeights always exposes both flags, allocating candidate status in the graph if omitted.
  • output.status.requiredCount is exact unless the pair list overflowed (then a lower bound). getCapacityPlan({requiredCount, candidateOverflow}) returns the next slots and pairs capacities (pairs keep the default ratio and double after a candidate overflow), so the rebuild loop converges. outputWeights.information is binary, symmetric, with provenance {source: 'GPUContiguityWeights', criterion}.
graph.add(new GPUContiguityWeights({
  criterion: 'rook',
  polygons: {kind: 'polygons', positions, ringOffsets, polygonOffsets, featureOffsets},
  output: {offsets, neighbors, weights, status: {overflow, requiredCount}}
}));

GPULatticeWeights

Connect the cells of a width by height grid to their rook or queen neighbours as binary CSR weights.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.lattice-weights rung=result caption="Queen neighbours on a 32 by 32 terrain lattice: 8 inside, 5 along edges, 3 in corners."}

Weights over a width x height grid with row-major IDs y * width + x. criterion: 'rook' uses Manhattan distance <= radius, 'queen' Chebyshev distance <= radius (radius defaults to 1, at most 32). A zero in the optional mask removes the cell as a row and as a neighbor. With cellSize it also writes Euclidean distances, so kernel transforms apply. output.status.requiredCount is the exact unclamped neighbor total; getCapacityPlan({requiredCount}) plans the next slots. outputWeights.information is binary and symmetric, with provenance {criterion, order: radius}.

cellSize accepts either a numeric [width, height] default or a packed float32 view from getGPULatticeWeightsParameterValues. Host that view in a GPUParameterBuffer and write new values between encodings to update distances on the same compiled graph. Width, height, criterion, radius, capacities and optional views remain compile-time; changing the distance scale keeps the same neighbors and capacity status.

The capacity plan includes 8 bytes for uploaded numeric cell sizes when output.distances is present. Borrowed parameter views and configurations without distance output add no parameter scratch; the per-cell count and offset buffers use 8 bytes per cell.

GPUSpatialWeightsTransform

Rewrite CSR weights by row, binary, kernel, symmetric, double or variance standardisation into a new weights graph.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-weights-transform rung=result caption="Row standardisation keeps the queen links of 791 tracts and rescales each row to sum to one."}

Rewrites weights in place or into a separate output. Chain several instances, for example a kernel followed by row standardization. Numeric bandwidth uses the length unit of weights.spatialContext, matching weights.distances. The optional spatialContext declares the bandwidth's context and is checked against weights.spatialContext with assertCompatibleGPUSpatialContexts when both are supplied.

  • 'row': w_ij / sum_j w_ij. A row whose weights sum to zero (or to a non-finite value) stays all zero: it has no active neighbor, so it is an island for every statistic (no uniform fallback). To restore row standardization after masking or 'subgraph' removed neighbors, apply 'row' again to the result.
  • 'binary': w_ij > 0 ? 1 : 0, keeping every slot.
  • 'kernel': K(d_ij / h_i) from weights.distances, with kernel one of gaussian (exp(-z^2 / 2) / sqrt(2 pi)), triangular (max(1 - z, 0)), epanechnikov (3/4 max(1 - z^2, 0), PySAL quadratic), bisquare (15/16 max(1 - z^2, 0)^2, PySAL quartic) or uniform (1/2 for z <= 1). bandwidth is required (no default) and is either a positive number, the same h for every row (PySAL fixed bandwidth; for distance-band weights pass the band radius), or 'adaptive': PySAL's adaptive bandwidth, h_i = the distance to the k-th nearest neighbor times eps = 1.0000001 (GPU_SPATIAL_WEIGHTS_ADAPTIVE_BANDWIDTH_EPSILON). On the CSR that is the row's largest distance, but only for kNN rows, so 'adaptive' requires weights.information.provenance.criterion === 'knn' and throws otherwise. Because of eps the k-th neighbor keeps a tiny positive weight, as in libpysal (in float32 eps rounds to 1 + 2^-23, so the tail weight is about 1e-7, never exactly 0). Only the stored neighbors take part, so the Gaussian is truncated at the neighbor set. Weights never hold self slots, so K(0) is not written: compute it with getGPUSpatialWeightsKernelValue(kernel, 0) and pass it as selfWeight to GPUHotSpotAnalysis for a kernel Gi*. getGPUSpatialWeightsKernelValue(kernel, z) is the CPU value of every profile.
  • 'double': w_ij / S0, with S0 the sum of all weights, so the weights sum to 1 (PySAL 'D'). doubleSum: 'rows' scales by n / S0 instead, so the weights sum to n.
  • 'variance': s_ij = w_ij / sqrt(sum_j w_ij^2), then w'_ij = s_ij n / sum s over the whole matrix, with n the row count (PySAL 'V').
  • 'symmetrize': (w_ij + w_ji) / 2, treating a missing reverse slot as 0. It keeps the sparsity pattern, so it is only symmetric for symmetric patterns (contiguity, lattice, distance band), and needs a separate output. For a directed pattern such as kNN, take the union of the weights and their GPUSpatialWeightsTranspose with GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra.

'double' and 'variance' compute a per-row partial, reduce it with GPUReduction, and apply it in a second pass, in place or into output. A zero or non-finite total gives zero weights. The operation, kernel profile and adaptive-versus-fixed choice are compile-time options. A fixed bandwidth also accepts a packed float32 view from getGPUSpatialWeightsTransformParameterValues; write new values through its GPUParameterBuffer between encodings. Numeric bandwidths remain supported as uploaded defaults. Updating bandwidth changes only existing slots; it does not rebuild the neighbor set or update a previously supplied numeric provenance radius. output, when given, must have exactly weights.neighbors.length values.

outputWeights.information.transform is the operation ('symmetrize' gives 'symmetric'). symmetric is kept only where it is guaranteed: 'binary', 'double', 'symmetrize' and a fixed-bandwidth 'kernel' keep a true input flag; 'row', 'variance' and an adaptive kernel give 'unknown' (row standardization makes a symmetric pattern asymmetric unless all row sums are equal).

GPUSpatialLag

If either supplied weights.status.overflow or weights.status.candidateOverflow is nonzero, every lag component is NaN; a retained prefix is never presented as a complete weighted sum. For chunked weights, loss on the outer status or any chunk suppresses the whole result. Clearing the loss words and submitting complete CSR data restores the lag on the same graph.

Average neighbouring values through row-standardised weights, turning a tract variable into its spatial lag.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-lag rung=result caption="Poverty percentage beside its neighbour-weighted lag across 791 tracts; the lag smooths local extremes."}

lag_i = sum_j w_ij y_j (PySAL lag_spatial). An optional mask skips masked neighbors and writes 0 for masked rows (square weights only); normalize: true divides by the included weight sum, which gives the row-standardized lag without a transformed copy of the weights. Rows are summed by a load-balanced (merge-path) CSR loop, so a hub row with 1e5 neighbors spreads over many workgroups instead of one thread. The association depends only on the offsets, so the result is bitwise reproducible; it matches a sequential sum within f32 rounding. A weights matrix with a self term (GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra 'selfWeight') includes it in the sum.

Weights need not be square. The value row count is values.length / columnCount: the rows of weights are targets and the neighbor IDs index those value rows; neighbors at or above it are skipped. columnCount (default 1) lags several columns in one pass: values is row-major, output is rows * columnCount, and normalize shares one weight sum per row. This is how the area shares of GPUArealInterpolation transfer values between zone systems. values may be float32 or uint32 (counts, exact below 2^24; one conversion node is added).

const contiguity = new GPUContiguityWeights({criterion: 'queen', polygons, output: {...weights, status: {overflow}}});
graph.add(contiguity);
graph.add(new GPUSpatialWeightsTransform({operation: 'row', weights: contiguity.outputWeights}));
graph.add(new GPUSpatialLag({weights: contiguity.outputWeights, values, output: lag}));

GPUNeighborSearch

Find the k nearest tract centroids by great-circle distance and write them as CSR neighbour weights.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.neighbor-search rung=result caption="Each of 791 tract centroids links to its 4 nearest others, giving 3,164 directed links."}

Maximum: gridSize[0] * gridSize[1] is at most getGPUGridIndexMaximumCellCount(device) (2^25 - 1 on a default device), checked when the contributor is added to a graph.

GPUNeighborSearch finds exact k nearest neighbors (mode: 'knn', k up to GPU_NEIGHBOR_SEARCH_MAXIMUM_K, 256) or every point within a distance band (mode: 'radius') between planar points, or between longitude/latitude points with great-circle distances (see below). A self join never lists a row as its own neighbor; pass queryPositions for a cross join. It writes a GPUSpatialWeights CSR: offsets (queryRows + 1), neighbors (the slot capacity), weights, and optional distances.

  • Rows list neighbor IDs in strictly ascending order with distances and weights aligned, so consumers can binary-search w_ji.
  • kNN keeps the k smallest (d^2, id) pairs, so ties go to the lowest ID. An optional radius bounds the search. Fewer valid targets give a shorter row.
  • Weights: binary, inverseDistance (max(d, distanceFloor)^-power, default floor 1e-6, the same default as GPUNearestFeatureWeights; two rows at the same position then weigh 1e6^power to each other and, with rowStandardize, take nearly all of each other's row, so pass a floor on the scale of the data when positions repeat), or kernel (gaussian, triangular, epanechnikov, bisquare, uniform). The bandwidth is radius in radius mode and the row's k-th neighbor distance times 1.0000001 in kNN mode, which is libpysal Kernel(fixed=False) with the self slot removed (a row with fewer than k neighbors uses its farthest listed neighbor). rowStandardize divides each row by its sum. Non-finite weights are written as 0. Rows never hold a self slot: the kernel's self weight K(0) is the consumer's selfWeight (getGPUSpatialWeightsKernelValue(kernel, 0)). A zero-weight slot (for example a triangular kernel at d = radius, or coincident points under inverse distance with distanceFloor: 0) is not a neighbor: consumers skip slots whose weight is not positive.
  • Capacity: offsets are clamped to neighbors.length, so readers always see consistent, possibly truncated rows. output.status.overflow is set on the GPU, and output.status.requiredCount and output.neighborCounts stay unclamped. The total requiredCount saturates at 0xffffffff; offsets remain monotonic after a uint32 carry, preserving the output prefix and setting overflow.
const searchParameters = {bounds, rowStandardize: true};
const parameters = new GPUParameterBuffer(device, {
  id: 'knn', format: 'float32', length: GPU_NEIGHBOR_SEARCH_PARAMETER_LENGTH,
  values: getGPUNeighborSearchParameterValues(searchParameters)
});
graph.add(new GPUNeighborSearch({
  mode: 'knn', k: 8, gridSize: [256, 256], positions,
  parameters: parameters.importToGraph(graph),
  output: {offsets, neighbors, weights, distances, status: {overflow}},
  weightTransform: getGPUNeighborSearchWeightTransform(searchParameters)
}));

Spatial context. spatialContext (compile-time) defaults to native planar coordinates. Planar contexts require metric 'native'; local frames with units: 'meters' are accepted. This operator measures distance, so local-lambert-equal-area frames throw. The resolved context is attached to outputWeights.spatialContext. For longitude/latitude pass {coordinateSpace: 'longitude-latitude', metric: 'great-circle', units?: 'meters' | 'kilometers', sphereRadius?} (sphereRadius in the context's length unit, default the mean Earth radius). Positions are [longitude, latitude] degrees, bounds are [minLongitude, minLatitude, maxLongitude, maxLatitude] degrees (longitude span at most 360, for example the world), and radius, distanceFloor and the output distances are great-circle distances in units (meters by default). Any other space or metric throws at construction (project to a planar frame first). Distances are f32 central angles on a sphere. The lattice stays in degrees: radius mode visits every cell that can hold a target in the cap (latitude span radius / R, longitude span the exact cap extent asin(sin(a) / cos(latitude)), the whole circle when the cap reaches a pole, and bands wrap across +-180), and kNN stops only when a conservative spherical lower bound of the distance to every unvisited cell exceeds the k-th distance, so results never depend on gridSize. The bound is deliberately loose near the poles, which only costs speed. Points with latitude beyond +-90 are invalid. Whole longitude turns do not change query distances or neighbor ordering: distance evaluation reduces each stored float32 longitude exactly modulo 360 using integer significand arithmetic, including very large finite values. Candidate lookup shifts that canonical angle by whole turns toward the lattice midpoint; the shift does not affect distance evaluation.

Planar precision. Planar f32 coordinates keep about seven digits: UTM northings (~4e6 m) and Web Mercator (~1e7) quantize to 0.5 to 1 m and make band edges and ties noisy. Project to a local frame with createGPULocalFrame (or subtract an origin) before upload; the node cannot recover digits lost in its inputs.

Pass optional spatialContext to getGPUNeighborSearchParameterValues(settings) to warn on short radius values; see Precision of longitude/latitude positions.

Row standardization. The optional compile-time rowStandardize prop is the single source of truth when given: the weights kernel uses it as a constant and ignores the per-frame rowStandardize parameter. Consumers re-standardize rows over the neighbors left after masking only when info.transform is 'row', so set it for any row-standardized band. rowStandardize: true with a weightTransform other than 'row', or false with 'row', throws. When it is omitted, the per-frame field and the weightTransform declaration apply as before.

Output. outputWeights is output with information and status attached, for consumers: rowCount, transform ('row' when the compile-time rowStandardize prop is true, otherwise the optional compile-time weightTransform, else 'unknown', because weight kind and row standardization are per-frame parameters; derive it from the same parameters with getGPUNeighborSearchWeightTransform), symmetric (true only for a planar radius self join without queryMask and a declared non-row transform; otherwise 'unknown'), provenance (criterion 'knn' or 'distance-band', and k) and status (overflow, requiredCount, invalidCount, and optional candidateOverflow). A requested candidateOverflow is overwritten with 0 on every submission, since neighbor search has no bounded intermediate candidate list. kNN output has criterion 'knn', which bandwidth: 'adaptive' kernel transforms require. PySAL's min_threshold_distance (the smallest band that leaves no island) is the maximum of the returned distances of a k = 1 kNN run.

Dropped rows. Targets and self-join rows count only when their mask is nonzero, their coordinates are finite, and they lie inside the per-frame bounds; the others silently become isolates and never a neighbor. A cross-join query (queryPositions) needs only a nonzero queryMask and finite coordinates: a query outside bounds returns exactly the brute-force neighbors, so bounds need only cover the targets. Great-circle queries also require latitude within ±90 degrees; longitude wrapping includes targets across the seam, even with regional bounds. A planar query far outside bounds (beyond a side or corner by many lattice widths) cannot stop its kNN ring walk early and visits every lattice cell in one invocation: with 200,000 targets in a 512 x 512 lattice, k = 8 and 2,000 queries the dispatch took about 7.5 ms with the queries inside and about 25 ms with them 1e6 beyond a side or corner, same results. When every squared distance of a query overflows float32 (offsets beyond about 1.8e19), all targets tie at infinity and kNN returns the lowest target IDs, as the scan does, on the grid and on the BVH; the written distances of those rows are infinite. Pass the optional output.status.invalidCount (one uint32) to learn how many masked rows were dropped for a non-finite coordinate or for lying outside bounds (target rows, plus the query rows of a cross join). A self join applies mask to the query rows too. Bounds, radius, the weight function and row standardization are per-frame parameters, so changing them never rebuilds the graph. Targets are bucketed with GPUGridIndex, whose boundsBuffer carries the per-frame lattice (cells at least radius wide) without recompiling. kNN searches expanding cell rings with a private sorted top-k list and stops once the k-th distance is strictly inside the visited box, with a slack for f32 rounding. Radius mode counts, scans, emits, and insertion-sorts each row by ID. No float atomics are used, so outputs are bitwise reproducible.

Search structure. The optional compile-time searchStructure ('auto' by default, 'grid' or 'bvh') only changes speed: all three give the same IDs, offsets, distances and weights (bitwise), except which neighbors the one row cut by an exceeded capacity keeps. A grid cell that holds most of the points (clustered data) makes every query in it scan that cell, so 'bvh' instead walks a Hilbert-packed point BVH in near-first depth-first order with the same (d^2, id) top-k list and radius limit, then shares the grid path's count, scan, emit and sort-by-ID stages. Invalid targets (mask, non-finite, outside the per-frame bounds) get empty boxes, so per-frame inputs still never recompile. 'auto' now uses exactly the grid graph, without allocating or building a BVH or computing an occupancy gate. All four measured 200k-point cases (clustered/uniform, kNN/radius) favored the grid; occupancy alone did not predict traversal cost. 'bvh' remains opt-in. GPU_NEIGHBOR_SEARCH_BVH_OCCUPANCY_THRESHOLD is retained for compatibility but no longer used. The BVH path is planar only: 'bvh' with a longitude-latitude spatialContext throws, and great-circle search always uses the grid. The new prop is compile-time and not part of the per-frame parameter packer, so the Parameters section below is unchanged.

Space-time search

Pass times (one relative float32 per target, with a float32-relative temporalContext) to search a space-time cylinder: a target is a neighbor only when it also satisfies |t_target - t_query| <= timeRadius, and kNN means the k nearest targets inside the window. A cross join also needs queryTimes. The parameter view grows to GPU_NEIGHBOR_SEARCH_TEMPORAL_PARAMETER_LENGTH (15) words holding timeRadius and timeBounds, packed by the same getGPUNeighborSearchParameterValues. A target or self-join row whose time is non-finite or outside timeBounds is invalid like a row outside bounds, and invalidCount counts it; a cross-join query needs only a finite time.

timeBucketCount (compile-time, default 1, at most GPU_NEIGHBOR_SEARCH_MAXIMUM_TIME_BUCKETS) also indexes the targets by time, so a window much shorter than timeBounds skips most of them. It never changes a result. On 100,000 clustered points with a window of 5% of the time span, 8 buckets took 29.7 ms against 40.4 ms for one (radius) and 13.1 ms against 25.2 ms (kNN, k = 16); 64 buckets gave no further gain. A kNN query whose window holds fewer than k targets finishes every ring, so set a radius when windows may be sparse. searchStructure: 'bvh' does not take times.

GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra

Combine or expand CSR weights by union, intersection, higher order, self weights, subgraph or block into a new graph.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-weights-algebra rung=result caption="Second-order queen neighbours of 791 tracts: each tract links to tracts exactly two steps away."}

Builds a new GPUSpatialWeights from existing ones with a capacity-bounded output (rows + 1 offsets; it must not alias an input). operation is one of:

  • 'union', 'intersection', 'difference', 'symmetricDifference' with left and right (same row count) and an optional weightRule: 'left' (default), 'right', 'sum', 'min', 'max', 'product' or 'binary'. The rule applies where both operands list a link, and one-sided links keep their weight. 'binary' gives 1 everywhere, which reproduces libpysal 4.15 Graph set operations. Distances come from left where it lists the link, else from right.
  • 'higherOrder' with weights, order (1 to 32), cumulative (default false) and workCapacity (intermediate capacity, default the output capacity). Writes the neighbors exactly order steps away by shortest path, excluding the diagonal, with weight 1; cumulative matches lower_order=True. Cost grows with the squared frontier degree per row, so it suits sparse contiguity and kNN weights.
  • 'selfWeight' with weights and selfWeight (a number at least 0, or a per-row float32 view). Inserts or replaces w_ii with distance 0. It relaxes the w_ii = 0 invariant. GPUSpatialLag, GPUSpatialWeightsTransform, GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra and GPUSpatialWeightsSummary accept it; GPUGlobalSpatialStatistics, the permutation tests and the local autocorrelation kernels ignore the self slot.
  • 'subgraph' with weights and a per-row mask. Rows keep their IDs: masked rows become empty and slots that point at masked rows are dropped.
  • 'block' with groupIds and groupCount. Row i lists every other row of the same group with weight 1; IDs at or above groupCount have no group. The output holds the sum of squared group sizes minus the row count.

output.status.overflow (one uint32, required) is 1 when the capacity was too small, and the optional output.status.requiredCount holds the slots needed (published as outputWeights.status.requiredCount; omitted there for 'higherOrder' with order > 1, where an overflowing intermediate set would make it a lower bound, so raise workCapacity instead). On overflow the output is still a valid CSR: the leading rows are complete, one row is a prefix and the rest are empty. Output rows are sorted and unique with finite non-negative weights. outputWeights.information carries the transform state ('binary' for weightRule: 'binary', 'higherOrder' and 'block'; an unscaled 'raw' or 'binary' input stays so; a row-standardized input becomes 'unknown' because slots changed) and symmetric: true only when it follows from symmetric operands.

Algebra carries either operand loss cause into candidateOverflow, independently of whether the retained input fits the new destination. Higher-order work scratch loss also sets candidateOverflow; overflow is only destination slot loss. Increasing destination capacity cannot repair inherited loss. The named operation and CPU twin require a candidate output name when operand loss ports or higher-order intermediate work are supplied. Named contiguity operations always require both output loss names; their candidate scratch is bounded. Correlogram incompleteOrders includes either upstream cause as well as loss introduced by that order, and incomplete orders suppress global Moran inference. The CPU twin honors an explicit higher-order workCapacity at every intermediate frontier and visited union. When omitted, its frontiers are unbounded; candidate loss makes requiredCount a lower bound in either form.

Algebra, contiguity and neighbor-search constructors snapshot their declared props and nested port/status maps once, including inherited getters. Their GPU buffer contents remain per-frame inputs. Rebuild the contributor to change a view, status destination, operation or capacity. Both named contiguity backends validate scalar options before output-port requirements and report the same scalar error even when a candidate destination is missing. Both forms also require distinct named output values: reusing a name for final and candidate loss is rejected before either diagnostic can be overwritten.

graph.add(new GPUSpatialWeightsAlgebra({
  operation: 'union', left: contiguity, right: knn, weightRule: 'binary',
  output: {offsets, neighbors, weights, status: {overflow, requiredCount}}
}));

GPUSpatialWeightsSummary

Summarise CSR weights into S0, S1, S2, slot counts, asymmetry, isolates and degree range.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-weights-summary rung=result caption="Read S0, S1, S2 and the isolate count of the queen weights of 791 tracts."}

Summary statistics of a weights matrix, written into output: {statistics, counts, cardinality?}. statistics (float32, at least 3) receives S0, S1 = 1/2 sum (w_ij + w_ji)^2 and S2 = sum_i (row_i + col_i)^2, as in libpysal. counts (uint32, at least 6, see GPU_SPATIAL_WEIGHTS_SUMMARY_LAYOUT) receives the used slots, the asymmetric slots, the isolates, the minimum and maximum cardinality and the empty rows. The optional cardinality is the per-row active neighbor count. symmetryTolerance accepts a numeric default (0 for exact comparison) or a packed one-float view from getGPUSpatialWeightsSummaryParameterValues hosted in a GPUParameterBuffer. Updating that buffer changes the reverse-weight comparison on the same compiled graph, for both whole and chunked weights. View shapes and chunk layout stay compile-time. Sums run in a fixed order. Isolates are rows with no active slot, the one island definition of every statistic (libpysal islands for a W built without zero-weight entries); emptyRows counts rows with no slot at all. Cardinality counts active slots, so a zero-weight slot is not a neighbor: this deviates from libpysal cardinalities, which counts every listed neighbor, and agrees with it for a W built without zero-weight entries (pinned against libpysal in the specs). The used slots count stays the listed total. Connected components are not computed. A pair of unequal weights counts as two asymmetric slots, where libpysal asymmetry() returns pairs.

GPUSpatialWeightsTranspose

Reverse every link of CSR weights, turning outgoing weights into incoming ones.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.spatial-weights-transpose rung=result caption="Transposed row-standardised queen weights: rows no longer sum to one, since they hold incoming weights."}

The CSR transpose W' of any GPUSpatialWeights, square or rectangular (libpysal Graph with focal and neighbor swapped). columnCount (default: the row count) sets the number of rows of the result for cross weights whose neighbor IDs index target rows; slots with a neighbor ID at or above it are dropped, and columns that nothing reaches give empty rows. output is a caller-owned CSR with columnCount + 1 offsets and at least the input capacity in neighbors and weights (and distances when the input has them); slots past offsets[columnCount] are zeroed. Slots are keyed by column, stable-sorted, counted, scanned and gathered, so every output row is ascending and the result is bitwise reproducible with no atomics. The optional output.asymmetricSlots (one uint32, square weights only, with symmetryTolerance, default 0) counts the positions (i, j) of the union of both patterns whose weights differ or that are present in one only, so a one-way link counts twice and 0 means W = W'. Reference: libpysal 4.15 (no deviations; the check is exact by default).

graph.add(new GPUSpatialWeightsTranspose({weights, output: {...transposed, asymmetricSlots}}));

symmetryTolerance accepts a numeric default or a packed one-float view from getGPUSpatialWeightsTransposeParameterValues hosted in a GPUParameterBuffer. Updating that buffer changes the symmetry comparison on the same compiled graph. Equality at the tolerance is symmetric; a missing reverse slot always differs, including when the present slot's weight is zero. CSR values, distances and tolerance are per-frame; view shapes, column count and optional outputs stay compile-time. The tolerance is unused when asymmetricSlots is omitted.

outputWeights carries the input symmetric flag (a transpose is symmetric exactly when W is) and the input status (a truncated W gives a truncated W'); a 'row' or 'variance' state becomes 'unknown'.

GPUMapColoring

Assign each tract a colour index so that no two neighbouring tracts share one.

::lens{scenario=gpu-spatial-analysis.map-coloring rung=result caption="At most five colours separate 791 rook-adjacent tracts; neighbouring tracts never match."}

Consumes an adjacency pattern only, so it takes no spatialContext. Weights may carry their own context; no compatibility check is needed because coloring uses only adjacency.

Greedy parallel graph coloring (Jones-Plassmann, largest degree first by default) over a symmetric GPUSpatialWeights such as contiguity, for choropleth maps where neighbors need distinct colors (QGIS topological coloring). output.colors holds one uint32 per row, GPU_MAP_COLORING_UNCOLORED for rows left unfinished. Optional outputs are colorCount, conflictCount (pairs sharing a color, 0 for a proper coloring) and status: {converged, iterationCount}. The per-frame parameters view (getGPUMapColoringParameterValues({seed}), GPU_MAP_COLORING_PARAMETER_LENGTH uint32 words) selects the priorities without recompiling, and maximumIterations (default 64, at most 1024) bounds the rounds. The compile-time priority prop selects the row order: 'largest-degree-first' (default; Welsh-Powell order, degree is the CSR row length, ties broken by the seeded hash and then the lowest ID) or 'random' (seeded hash, then lowest ID). The result equals sequential greedy coloring in that order, a proper and seed-deterministic coloring that uses at most the maximum degree plus one colors, not the minimum number. Largest-degree-first typically saves a color on planar maps (6 versus 6 to 7 on Delaunay graphs, matching networkx largest_first) at about the same number of rounds. Different seeds give different valid colorings.
