Proper scoring rules

Scores of probabilistic forecasts against observed values: the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), the energy score and the variogram score, for ensembles such as conditional Gaussian simulations, and the closed-form Gaussian CRPS for kriging predictions. Lower is better for all of them, and each is a proper score: in expectation it is smallest for the true distribution.

import {GPUEnsembleScores, GPUGaussianPredictiveScores} from '@luma-contributors/analysis/gpu-spatial-analysis';

The classes are in the directory src/gpu-spatial-analysis/scoring-rules/. The re-export line export * from './scoring-rules/index'; in src/gpu-spatial-analysis/index.ts is what makes the import above resolve; until it is added, import from ./scoring-rules/index.

Definitions

Definitions are those of scoringRules 1.1.3 (Jordan, Krueger and Lerch, Journal of Statistical Software 90(12), 2019). With members x_1 ... x_m and observation y:

Score scoringRules function Formula
CRPS crps_sample(method = "edf") (1/m) sum_i abs(x_i - y) - 1/(2 m^2) sum_i sum_j abs(x_i - x_j)
Energy score es_sample (Gneiting et al. 2008) the same with Euclidean norms of the d-vectors
Variogram score of order p vs_sample(p = ...) (Scheuerer and Hamill 2015) sum_(i,j) w_ij (abs(y_i - y_j)^p - (1/m) sum_k abs(x_ki - x_kj)^p)^2
Gaussian CRPS crps_norm (Gneiting et al. 2005) sd (z (2 Phi(z) - 1) + 2 phi(z) - 1/sqrt(pi)), z = (y - mean) / sd

Fair and non-fair ensemble variants

scoringRules 1.1.3 defines only the non-fair forms above, the empirical-distribution scores whose member-pair term is divided by 2 m^2. They are the default (fair false). They are biased upward for a finite ensemble: even a calibrated ensemble of m draws scores worse, by E|X - X'| / (2m), than its distribution would.

With fair: true the pair term of the CRPS and the energy score is divided by 2 m (m - 1) instead (Ferro 2014; Zamo and Naveau 2018). That removes the bias and lets scores of ensembles with different member counts be compared. The fair variant is an extension of this package: scoringRules 1.1.3 does not define it, so no scoringRules value exists for it. It needs memberCount >= 2. The variogram score has no fair variant here.

GPUEnsembleScores

Variogram pair count is at most 2,147,483,647 to prevent flattened-index overflow; all score inputs and outputs remain device-binding bounded.

const scores = new GPUEnsembleScores({
  ensemble,        // GraphDataView<'float32'>, memberCount * dimension rows, member-major
  observations,    // GraphDataView<'float32'>, dimension rows
  memberCount: 100,
  dimension: 4096,
  fair: false,
  variogramOrder: 0.5,
  variogramPairs,  // GraphDataView<'uint32x2'>: ordered site pairs [i, j]
  variogramWeights, // optional GraphDataView<'float32'>, one weight per pair
  output: {crps, energyScore, variogramScore}
});
graph.add(scores);
  • ensemble is member-major, row member * dimension + site. That is the layout of GPUConditionalGaussianSimulation output.values, so a simulation batch is scored in place.
  • output.crps has one row per site. output.energyScore and output.variogramScore have one row each and score the whole field. At least one output is required.
  • variogramPairs lists ordered pairs; every listed pair contributes one term. Listing every [i, j] with i != j reproduces vs_sample with its default unit weights, which sums the full symmetric matrix. A list of neighbouring pairs restricts the score to them: the all-pairs form costs O(d^2 m), so for large fields use neighbour pairs, a documented choice that matches the w_vs weights argument of vs_sample.
  • Members, observations or pairs that are NaN give NaN scores; a pair index at or beyond dimension gives a NaN variogram score.

How it is computed

The CRPS runs one 64-lane workgroup per site. Lane l adds the terms of members l, l + 64, ... in order, then a fixed binary tree adds the lanes, so the f32 sum does not depend on scheduling. The energy and variogram scores write one term per member or per pair and the published GPUReduction sums them. There are no float atomics. The result is repeatable on one device; the published reduction may use subgroup collectives where the device offers them, and their order is the device's.

Cost: O(m^2 d) for the CRPS and energy score, O(P m) for the variogram score over P pairs. memberCount is at most 65535, dimension at most 67108863 (floor((2^32 - 1) / 64)), and the ensemble must fit one storage binding. Tile larger fields by calling the class on subsets of sites for the CRPS; the energy and variogram scores are scores of the whole field.

Accuracy: f32. The CRPS and variogram scores agree with a binary64 evaluation to about 2e-5 relative on the test fixtures; the CRPS is a difference of two means of similar size when the ensemble is wide relative to the error, so its absolute error scales with the spread.

GPUGaussianPredictiveScores

const gaussianScores = new GPUGaussianPredictiveScores({
  means: prediction,      // kriging prediction
  variances: variance,    // kriging variance, squared value units
  observations,
  output: {crps}
});

The closed-form CRPS of N(mean, variance) per site, for GPUKriging, GPULocalKriging and GPUConditionalGaussianSimulation output.moments (split into means and variances). Use the leave-one-out prediction and variance of GPUKriging with the sample values to score kriging by cross-validation. A zero variance is a point forecast and scores the absolute error; a negative or NaN variance scores NaN.

2 Phi(z) - 1 = erf(z / sqrt(2)) has no WGSL built-in. The kernel sums the all-positive series erf(x) = 2/sqrt(pi) exp(-x^2) sum_n 2^n x^(2n+1) / (2n + 1)!! for x <= 4 (no cancellation in f32) and takes 1 beyond, which truncates erf by less than 1.6e-8 (|z| above 5.7). The agreement with math.erf on the fixture is within 2e-6. The leading term is evaluated as |error| * erf(...) and only the bounded correction is scaled by the deviation. For |error| > 4 sqrt(2) * deviation, the kernel uses |error| - deviation / sqrt(pi) before dividing, so an overflowing standardized error does not turn a finite representable score into infinity.

A Gaussian ensemble scored with GPUEnsembleScores converges to this score as m grows: the test suite checks a 200-member quantile ensemble of N(0, 1) against crps_norm(1; 0, 1) within 2e-3.

Verification

  • Oracle test/oracles/scoring-rules.py writes fixtures/scoring-rules.json: binary64 evaluations of the definitions above, with the CRPS cross-checked against the exact integral of (F_m - 1{y <= x})^2 and the Gaussian CRPS against numerical integration.
  • Oracle test/oracles/scoring-rules-fair.py (NumPy and SciPy) writes fixtures/scoring-rules-fair.json for the fair variants, which scoringRules does not define: the fair CRPS by order statistics, the fair energy score as a mean over distinct pairs (scipy.spatial.distance.pdist), and the closed-form CRPS and energy score of the standard normal distribution, which the mean of the fair scores over many small ensembles must equal (and the non-fair mean must exceed). Spec gpu-ensemble-scores-fair.spec.ts.
  • The external scoringRules fixture is pending. scoringRules is an R package and R was not installed where the fixture was made. A sibling script that calls crps_sample, es_sample, vs_sample and crps_norm on the same inputs should replace the self-computed values.
  • Specs in test/gpu-spatial-analysis/scoring-rules/: GPU values against the fixture, hand values (members 0, 1, 2 with y = 0 score 5/9, fair 1/3), bitwise repeatability, propriety of the CRPS on simulated data, kernel limits and prop validation.
