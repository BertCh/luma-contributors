Track similarity: EDR, ERP, SSPD and bounded search

GPUTrackSimilarity computes, besides Hausdorff, discrete Frechet, DTW and LCSS, three more measures on the same pair list, and GPUTrackSimilaritySearch can prune on cheap lower bounds before it evaluates any of them. Tracks are planar or great-circle vertex lines without time.

Output Measure Source
editDistanceOnRealSequence EDR Chen, Ozsu and Oria, SIGMOD 2005
editDistanceWithRealPenalty ERP Chen and Ng, VLDB 2004
symmetricSegmentPathDistance SSPD Besse, Guillouet, Loubes and Royer, 2016
hausdorff (existing) symmetric Hausdorff Shapely/GEOS, the one movingpandas.Trajectory.hausdorff_distance calls 
new GPUTrackSimilarity({
  linesA, linesB, pairA, pairB,
  editDistanceTolerance: 25,          // EDR match distance, context length unit
  editDistanceGapPoint: [0, 0],       // ERP gap point
  output: {
    editDistanceOnRealSequence: edr,
    editDistanceWithRealPenalty: erp,
    symmetricSegmentPathDistance: sspd
  }
});

Definitions

  • EDR. The least number of insertions, deletions and substitutions that turns one (densified) vertex sequence into the other. Two vertices match, at cost 0, when their distance is at most editDistanceTolerance; a substitution of non-matching vertices, an insertion and a deletion cost 1. The result is a whole number and is not normalized; divide by the longer vertex count for the normalized form. The match test is a metric ball like this package's lcssTolerance; Chen's original uses a per-axis box.
  • ERP. A substitution costs the distance between the two vertices; an insertion or a deletion costs the distance of the vertex to the fixed editDistanceGapPoint. ERP is a metric. The gap point is [x, y] in planar contexts and [longitude, latitude] degrees on the sphere.
  • SSPD. The mean over the original vertices of one track of the distance to the nearest point of the other track's polyline (the segments, not only the vertices), averaged over both directions. It is not densified and has no vertex cap.
  • Hausdorff is the existing output, unchanged.

For geographic Hausdorff and SSPD, projection and interpolation use one wrapped start-to-end longitude arc. The nearest-segment candidate is compared with both endpoints; its distance never exceeds either endpoint distance. This holds for queries on the opposite side of the globe as well as for queries near the antimeridian. For example, the point (0°, 0°) and segment (100°, 0°) → (-100°, 0°) have Hausdorff and SSPD distances of 100° × sphereRadius × π / 180 with default densification. The local projection remains an approximation for long edges.

EDR and ERP run the anti-diagonal dynamic program of DTW with one extra table row and column for the empty prefix, so the boundary prefix costs come from the same recurrence; they share the shorter-track cap maximumFrechetVertexCount (frechetCapExceeded in pairStatus, NaN result) and densify. SSPD uses 64 lanes per pair with fixed-order lane sums and a fixed binary tree (no atomics). EDR is exact in f32; ERP sums in f32, so its error grows with n + m like DTW.

The CPU twin creates densified points only for requested measures that use them. Requests limited to maximumDistance and/or symmetricSegmentPathDistance use the original vertices, even with a small positive densify. For measures that densify, each expanded track must have at most 2^32 - 1 vertices (the JavaScript array length limit); the CPU checks that count before allocating the dense points. An explicit maximumFrechetVertexCount also binds CPU dynamic-program measures: beyond the shorter densified track cap, status is frechetCapExceeded and their distances are NaN (decisions are zero). Hausdorff, SSPD and maximum distance remain available. Omitting that prop leaves the CPU shorter-track count uncapped. The GPU also checks expanded counts only when a requested measure uses densification, and checks the shorter-track cap only when a dynamic-program measure is requested. A maximum-distance-only or SSPD-only request therefore remains valid when an unused expanded count would exceed uint32.

Hausdorff, EDR, ERP and SSPD are not in the MovingPandas 0.23 distance list except Hausdorff, DTW, LCSS and Frechet, so EDR, ERP and SSPD are verified against double-precision definitions written from the papers (see Verification).

Lower bounds and pruned search

GPUTrackSimilaritySearch takes metric 'hausdorff', 'edit-distance-on-real-sequence', 'edit-distance-with-real-penalty' or 'symmetric-segment-path' in addition to the previous metrics. For each pair it first computes an O(n + m) lower bound from the original vertices and writes it to output.lowerBound. Pairs whose bound exceeds the threshold (with a relative f32 margin of 1e-6) get status 1 and skip the measure, and their distance is NaN. The survivors are compacted and evaluated, and topK selects the exact stable top-k among the pairs within the threshold, grouped by pairA.

Measure Lower bound Why it never exceeds the measure
Hausdorff largest distance of a vertex of one track to the bounding box of the other the other track lies in its box, so the distance to the box is at most the distance to the track
SSPD the same with means the mean of box distances is at most the mean of polyline distances
EDR max(n, m) minus the fewer vertices that lie within the tolerance-widened box of the other track each edit script costs at least max(n, m) minus its matches, and a vertex outside the widened box cannot match
ERP absolute difference of the two sums of vertex distances to the gap point ERP is a metric and the ERP of a track to the empty sequence is that sum (reverse triangle inequality)
Frechet, DTW larger endpoint distance (existing) the first and last vertices are always coupled

The EDR and SSPD bounds subtract an f32 slack proportional to n + m so that summed rounding cannot push a bound above the evaluated distance; the ERP bound subtracts (n + m) * 1.2e-7 * (sumA + sumB).

Pruning is exact: it only skips pairs that cannot be within the threshold. The threshold is the caller's. To get the k nearest tracks without a known radius, run a first search with a generous threshold or take the k-th distance of an earlier frame, widen it by about 1e-4 for f32 rounding, and search again; a threshold-free adaptive top-k that tightens the radius while it runs is not implemented.

Why this matters: a 2024 study (Ni et al., PVLDB 2024, arXiv 2311.00960) measured batched GPU DTW over 512 pairs at only about 4.5 times a CPU (28.11 s against 6.21 s), so the pairs that never reach the dynamic program are the larger saving than a faster program.

Verification

  • test/oracles/track-similarity-edit-measures.py (MovingPandas 0.23.0, Shapely 2.2.0, Python 3.15) writes fixtures/track-similarity-edit-measures.json. hausdorff and dynamicTimeWarping come from Trajectory.hausdorff_distance and Trajectory.dtw_distance and are asserted equal to Shapely and to an independent DTW program; EDR, ERP and SSPD are written from the paper definitions in recursive head/rest form, which differs from the GPU's table.
  • Specs in test/gpu-spatial-analysis/track-similarity/: the GPU against the fixture (planar and great circle, tracks across the 256-lane strip boundary, cap status), fixed-order SSPD repeatability, lower bounds at most the true distance for 404 random and degenerate pairs in double precision and on the GPU, and pruned top-k equal to the unpruned top-k and to a CPU reference.
